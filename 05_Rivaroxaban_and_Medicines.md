# Rivaroxaban and other medicines

Rivaroxaban is the daily medicine that prevents new LV clots. Take it every day and never stop or change it without the doctor.

## Current medicines

| Medicine | Use | Notes |

| Arcoxia (etoricoxib) 90 mg | Pain, only when needed | An NSAID. Raises bleeding risk with rivaroxaban and can affect the kidneys. Ask the doctor before using it |

## What rivaroxaban is

- A blood thinner (anticoagulant). It is licensed for leg and lung clots, for stroke prevention in atrial fibrillation, and after hip or knee replacement.
- Using it for LV is off label, which is normal and legal when evidence supports it.
- It does not mean Thomas has a heart or deep vein problem.

## Evidence in LV

- **RILIVA trial (Germany, 25 patients).** Pain fell from 65 to 6 out of 100 over 12 weeks. Pain halved within about 11 days. About 30% needed extra heparin injections.
- **Review of about 70 patients.** It worked for both pain and ulcers in about 82%.
- **Long term study (34 patients).** The first course lasted a median of 9 months. After stopping, 16 relapsed at a median of 7 months, and 10 stayed well for a median of 17.5 months. Of 14 who restarted, 12 went back into remission. Four had mild flares from heat even on treatment.

## How to take it

- Same time every day. Use a phone alarm.
- 10 mg can be taken with or without food. 15 mg and 20 mg must be taken with a meal.
- Keep a spare tablet in the work bag.
- Order refills a week ahead.
- Tell every doctor, dentist and pharmacist. Carry an anticoagulant alert card.

## Missed dose

- **Once a day:** take it as soon as remembered on the same day, even late at night. Take the next one at the usual time. Never take two at once.
- **Twice a day:** the rule depends on the tablet strength, so ask the pharmacist.
- On 11 Nov 2026 the morning dose was forgotten. The plan was to take it at about 10:30pm that night.

## What to expect

| Time on treatment | What usually happens |
| --- | --- |
| About 2 weeks | Pain eases. This is the first sign it is working |
| 4 to 6 weeks | Clear drop in pain and wounds visibly healing. Checkpoint with the doctor |
| 1 to 3 months | Ulcers heal. Ankle skin and a biopsy site on LV skin heal slowly |
| About 8 weeks with no change | Ask about a repeat biopsy, vasculitis tests or another treatment |

No new spots is the main sign the medicine is working. Old wounds heal later.

## Is 10 mg the right dose

- The German guideline describes higher doses for active ulcers and a step down to 10 mg a day once things are stable.
- The RILIVA trial used 10 mg twice a day at first, then once a day.
- Thomas takes 10 mg tablets. How many times a day is not recorded. Old wounds were healing slowly on 7 Nov, so it is reasonable to ask the doctor whether a higher dose is needed while ulcers are open.
- The guideline also says the dose should go up when pain clearly rises. Only the doctor decides this.

## Long term plan

1. Take it daily. The first goal is no pain and fully healed ulcers.
2. Once that is reached, the doctor may step down to a maintenance dose.
3. The doctor may try stopping once things are stable.
4. At the first sign of a flare after stopping, contact the doctor quickly about restarting. Ask in advance for a standing prescription.

- Feeling no pain does not mean the disease has gone. Do not stop when the pain stops.
- Some people need it for years. Fast or frequent relapse after stopping is the main reason.
- Singapore is hot all year and heat triggers flares, so the doctor may prefer a longer maintenance period.
- **Every other day dosing.** The medicine wears off within about a day, so skipping days leaves gaps. One long term study reportedly stepped some patients down to 10 mg every other day, but that detail is unconfirmed. Do this only if the doctor prescribes it.

## Risks

The main risk is bleeding. A large trial of 3,365 people with leg or lung clots, average age 58, followed them for about a year:

| Daily treatment | Major bleeding | Smaller bleeds needing a doctor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rivaroxaban 10 mg | 0.4%, about 1 in 250 | 2.0% |
| Rivaroxaban 20 mg | 0.5% | 2.7% |
| Low dose aspirin | 0.3% | 1.8% |

- At 10 mg a day the risk is about the same as daily low dose aspirin. Thomas is 35 with normal clotting tests and platelets, so his risk is probably lower.
- **Common and minor:** easy bruising, nose or gum bleeds, cuts that bleed longer.
- **Rare:** serious bleeding in the stomach or brain, liver injury, allergy.
- **Kidneys:** the drug is not known to harm them, but about a third is cleared by the kidneys. Weak kidneys make it build up.
- **Monitoring:** kidney function, liver function and blood count at least once a year. None has been done yet apart from the blood count.

### What raises bleeding risk

- Arcoxia, ibuprofen, aspirin and other NSAIDs
- Heavy drinking
- Weak kidneys
- Head knocks and contact sport
- Supplements such as ginkgo, nattokinase, high dose fish oil, high dose vitamin E, turmeric capsules
- St John's wort does the opposite: it lowers the drug level by about a quarter, so protection drops

Bleeding warning signs are in 08_Wound_Care_and_Red_Flags.md.

## Buying it

- Prescription only in Singapore at every strength. There is no over the counter substitute.
- SGH charged S$105.56 before GST for 14 tablets of Xarelto 10 mg, about S$8.20 a tablet with GST.
- GPs, polyclinics and licensed telemedicine services can prescribe it. Cheaper generic brands exist in Singapore.
- Buy only from licensed pharmacies. Sites that sell it without a prescription risk fakes.
- The 2.5 mg strength is not a milder version for LV. No study supports it for LV.
- Ask for a longer prescription, for example 2 to 3 months, to avoid running out.

## If rivaroxaban is not enough

All of these need the doctor.

| Option | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Higher rivaroxaban dose or heparin injections | Usual next step during a flare |
| Aspirin, dipyridamole, pentoxifylline | Older, cheaper options with weaker evidence |
| IVIG (given by drip in hospital) | About 95% respond in small series. Costly |
| JAK inhibitors | Newest option. Case reports only |
| Colchicine, dapsone, prednisolone | Used if the problem turns out to be vasculitis. Dapsone needs a G6PD test first |
