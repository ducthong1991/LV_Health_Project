# You may not need rivaroxaban forever

**Short answer.** Yes, there are other ways, but none is proven to work better than the tablet you take now. The best-supported alternative to "tablets forever" is a planned stop: after a stable period, you and your dermatologist stop the tablet, watch closely, and restart quickly if signs return. In the best study, about 4 in 10 people who stopped had not relapsed after about a year and a half; the others relapsed, usually more mildly, and most improved again on treatment. No treatment cures LV, and none has been tested in a randomised trial.

*This is general information to discuss with your dermatologist. Do not stop or change your medicine on your own.*

**What matters most for you**

- **A planned stop is allowed.** The German LV guideline offers stable patients 10 mg a day or a trial of stopping. At about 5 weeks in, this is for later.
- **Finish the antibody test first.** The missing anti-beta-2 glycoprotein I blood test could change which medicine is right for you.
- **10 mg is not the stroke dose.** It is the low, preventive dose. In trials its bleeding risk was similar to low-dose aspirin.
- **Other clot medicines are not clearly better.** Aspirin is weaker for LV and not clearly safer.
- **Immune treatments are backups.** They are for when clot medicines fail, or if your problem is more vasculitis (inflamed vessel walls) than LV.
- **Habits help but cannot replace medicine.** Heat is the best-documented trigger. A pain diary is the most useful tool.

**Questions and tests to raise**

1. Can we add anti-beta-2 glycoprotein I (IgG and IgM)? Rivaroxaban does not affect it.
2. After how many stable months would you support a trial of stopping? What is the restart plan?
3. Have these been checked: protein C, protein S, antithrombin, lipoprotein(a), hepatitis B and C, ANCA, urine test, protein electrophoresis?
4. If new spots appear while I take rivaroxaban, is a deeper repeat biopsy and an anti-inflammatory medicine such as colchicine the next step?

## More detail

Read only the parts you want. Each part starts with the main point.

A note on evidence: LV is rare. Everything below comes from case reports, small patient series and expert opinion. The German guideline itself says no large trials are expected ([AWMF S1 guideline, 2025](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)).

### 1. Stopping or reducing: about 6 in 10 relapse, but mildly

**Main point.** A planned stop is normal guideline practice once you are stable. Expect roughly a 6 in 10 chance that LV returns within about 2 years. Relapses were usually milder than the first illness, and second treatment courses were much shorter.

**What the guideline says.** The German guideline (2025 version, expert consensus) sets no fixed treatment length. For a stable patient its flow chart gives two choices: rivaroxaban 10 mg a day, or a trial of stopping. It does not say how long you should be stable first. It says nothing about every-other-day or seasonal dosing ([AWMF S1 guideline, 2025](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)).

**What happened to people who stopped**

| Study | Who | Result |
|---|---|---|
| China, rivaroxaban ([Deng et al., JAAD Int 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10897505/)) | 26 people who stopped after a median 9 months of treatment | 10 (38%) had no relapse over a median 17.5 months. 16 (62%) relapsed after a median 7 months. |
| China, largest series ([Ban et al. 2026](https://www.dpcj.org/index.php/dpc/article/download/6930/3938/114821)) | 236 people followed after different treatments | 62% relapsed within 2 years. 23% relapsed between 2 and 4 years. 14% stayed clear more than 4 years. |
| France ([Gardette et al. 2018](https://medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-2965)) | 26 people, median 5 years of follow-up | 9 (36%) had no flare for 4 years or more. 5 of them took no medicine. |
| Korea ([summary in Ramphall et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9420174/)) | 13 people who stopped rivaroxaban | 10 relapsed, more mildly. This figure is second-hand and not checked in the original letter. |

**Other key facts**

- **Relapses were milder and shorter to treat.** In the Deng study the relapse severity score was 2.5, against 7.0 at the start. The second course lasted a median 3 months, against 9 months for the first ([Deng et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10897505/)).
- **There is an early warning.** New ulcers usually follow 1 to 3 days of burning pain. Pain falls within 2 to 4 days of starting or raising the medicine. The guideline recommends a pain diary and acting when pain clearly rises. Studies used 10 mg twice a day for a relapse ([AWMF S1 guideline, 2025](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)).
- **No "rebound" is proven.** In a large trial of people with vein clots, there were no extra clots in the 30 days after stopping 10 mg (0.4%, against 0.6% after stopping aspirin) ([Weitz et al., NEJM 2017](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10050925/1/nejmoa1700518.pdf)). The drug leaves a young adult's body within about a day ([FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/022406s041lbl.pdf)). So a slow taper has no clear purpose. That last point is an inference, not a tested fact.
- **Lower doses are unverified.** The Deng paper says some patients took 10 mg every other day. It is one sentence, with no patient numbers and no results. A 2021 Indian letter describes "intermittent low dose dabigatran" (a similar drug) for keeping LV away, but its details could not be retrieved ([Rajan et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34028946/)).
- **Seasonal treatment is untested.** No study has tried "treat only in the hot months". Singapore has no cool season, so this idea probably does not fit your situation. This is an inference.

**Cautions for your case**

- You have been on rivaroxaban about 5 weeks. People in the Deng study stopped after a median 9 months.
- That study was 79% women, with illness starting in the teens, in a place with a cool season. With more than 10 years of relapses, your chance of relapse is probably at the higher end. This is an inference; no study has measured it.
- Staying on the tablet is not perfect either. Four people in the Deng study relapsed after heat exposure while still taking it.
- A failed stop can leave permanent white scars. Nerve damage was found in 10 of 20 French patients tested, and in 9% of a Singapore series ([Gardette et al. 2018](https://medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-2965); [Gan et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23052434/)). This is why any stop should be planned with your doctor.

### 2. Other clot-related medicines: none clearly better or safer

**Main point.** No other clot medicine has been shown to keep LV away better than low-dose rivaroxaban. Aspirin is the simplest swap, but it works less well for LV, and its bleeding risk was about the same in the one direct comparison.

| Option | What the evidence shows | Main drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Other tablets of the same family (apixaban, dabigatran, edoxaban) | Single case reports and 2-patient series only. Never compared with rivaroxaban ([Burg et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9574051)). | Same type of drug, same type of bleeding risk. Worth a switch only if rivaroxaban fails or causes side effects. |
| Heparin injections (enoxaparin) | Best-supported alternative for an active flare. Full response in 83% of flares in the French series, given for 15 days to 3 months ([Gardette et al. 2018](https://medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-2965)). | Daily self-injection. Used for short courses, not for years. |
| Warfarin | Case-level support. Three Indian men did well on a very low dose; one relapsed a year after stopping ([Saoji and Madke 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5618839/)). | Needs regular blood tests. Many food and drug interactions. |
| Aspirin | Mixed. Singapore: only 1 of 6 did well on aspirin or pentoxifylline alone ([Lee et al. 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14716957/)). India: 6 of 26 patients were healed on aspirin 150 mg alone at 6 months ([Gupta et al. 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41655089/)). France: aspirin-type drugs cleared 33% of flares ([Gardette et al. 2018](https://medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-2965)). | Weaker than anticoagulants. Not in the German guideline's treatment list ([AWMF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)). |
| Aspirin plus clopidogrel | 12 of 17 improved, but 11 of 17 flared within a year ([Shah et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9650729/)). | Frequent breakthrough flares. |
| Sulodexide (a heparin-like tablet) | 31 of 35 (89%) cleared in 12 weeks in a 2026 Chinese study. Read as an abstract only; no relapse data ([Ban et al. 2026, JDDG](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41482702/)). | Not found in Singapore's medicines register in one automated check. This needs confirming with a pharmacist ([HSA register](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_767279312753558cbf19d48344577084/view)). |
| Danazol (a hormone-type drug) | Stopped new spots in small series of 6 and 4 patients ([Hsiao and Chiu 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9187843/); [Criado et al. 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25824252/)). | Short courses of 4 to 12 weeks only, because of hormone side effects ([Bilgic et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8721056/)). |

**Is aspirin safer?** Not clearly. In the only head-to-head trial (3,365 people with vein clots, not LV), major bleeding was 0.4% on rivaroxaban 10 mg and 0.3% on aspirin 100 mg over about a year. Rivaroxaban prevented far more clots: 1.2% had a new clot, against 4.4% on aspirin ([Weitz et al., NEJM 2017](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10050925/1/nejmoa1700518.pdf)).

**A pattern some doctors use.** In the French series, all 4 patients with 2 or more years of remission after heparin were kept on aspirin or clopidogrel ([Gardette et al. 2018](https://medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-2965)). This "strong drug first, then aspirin" step-down has never been tested against simply continuing the anticoagulant.

### 3. Immune and newer treatments: backups that control but do not cure

**Main point.** These are used when clot medicines fail. All of them calm the disease while you take them, and relapse after stopping is common. They matter for you mainly because your biopsy showed a mixed picture.

| Treatment | What the evidence shows | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| IVIG (antibodies from donated blood, given by drip) | About 80 published patients; 76 of 80 improved ([Gao and Jin 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9134453/)). In a Korean series 6 of 7 relapsed after about 13 months; one stayed clear over 7 years ([Kim et al. 2015](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25845419)). Remission lasted a median 27 months after a first episode but 7.5 months after later ones ([Monshi et al. 2014](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25022849)). | Hospital drip, off-label, costly. No Singapore price was found. Most patients also stayed on a clot medicine. Several figures are from abstracts only. |
| JAK inhibitors (immune-calming tablets such as baricitinib) | Roughly 30 to 45 published patients, mostly from China, mostly after anticoagulants failed. About 80 to 100% improved in 1 to 3 months. Where the drug was stopped, relapse was common: 2 of 4 in one series ([Zhao et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12803528/); [Wang et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13714699/)). | The US regulator warns this drug class can raise the risk of blood clots, heart events and cancer. That warning comes from older arthritis patients ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/fda-requires-warnings-about-increased-risk-serious-heart-related-events-cancer-blood-clots-and-death)). No clots are reported in LV, but too few patients have been treated to be reassured. |
| Colchicine (an old anti-inflammatory tablet) | In Singapore's National Skin Centre series (70 patients, 1997 to 2007) it was the most successful single drug: 59% of single-drug successes ([Gan et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23052434/)). It is also a usual first choice for skin vasculitis that keeps returning ([Front Med 2022 vasculitis review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.1059612/full)). | From the time before rivaroxaban. No data on relapse after stopping. The only small vasculitis trial (20 people, 1 month) showed no benefit over placebo ([ARAMIS protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7189702)). |
| Steroid tablets alone | Worst result in the largest series: 14 of 19 relapsed within 2 years ([Ban et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13557247/)). | For short flares or linked autoimmune disease only. |
| Hyperbaric oxygen (breathing oxygen in a pressure chamber) | Ulcers healed in about 3.4 weeks, but 6 of 8 later relapsed ([Juan et al. 2006](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/16433793)). | A rescue for ulcers, not prevention. |

Standard immune-suppressing drugs (methotrexate, mycophenolate, azathioprine, ciclosporin) appear in LV reports mostly as treatments that failed. No LV drug trial that is open to patients was found on the US or EU trial registers. The Chinese register could not be checked.

**Why your mixed biopsy matters here.** Anticoagulants are not standard treatment for vasculitis. So your response is useful information. Staying clear on rivaroxaban alone points towards LV. New spots despite it would point towards vasculitis or an inflammatory form of LV, where an anti-inflammatory medicine makes more sense. This reasoning is an inference from the notes, not a tested rule, and 5 weeks is too short to judge.

### 4. Non-drug measures: sensible, but they cannot replace medicine

**Main point.** No habit or device has been shown to prevent LV flares, and none has been tested properly. They are low-risk add-ons. If you already avoid heat, keep moving, protect your feet and do not smoke, the research adds only a few things.

| Measure | Evidence | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Avoid heat and big temperature swings | Summer worsening in 82% of Chinese patients with a seasonal pattern (150 of 183), 54% in France, but only 1 of 24 in Finland ([Ban et al. 2026](https://www.dpcj.org/index.php/dpc/article/download/6930/3938/114821); [Gardette et al. 2018](https://medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-2965); [Köykkä et al. 2026](https://journals.cambridgemedia.com.au/jwm/volume-27-number-2/livedoid-vasculopathy-24-finnish-patients-single-center-institution/10.35279/jowm2026.27.02.14)). | Best-documented trigger. No study has tested cooling or air conditioning. Ice on the skin has no support. |
| Pain diary and early action | Recommended by the German guideline with near-full expert agreement ([AWMF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)). | The most useful tool. It guides when to restart or raise medicine. |
| Light compression stockings (about 20 mmHg) | The guideline says they "can be considered" if you tolerate them. No data on relapse. | Check the leg arteries first. Hard to wear in a hot, humid climate. |
| Avoid long standing; raise legs; stay active | Long standing worsened LV in 12 of 24 patients in one small series (not verified in full text) ([Feng et al. 2014](https://medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-1711)). | Borrowed from vein disease. No exercise type is shown to be harmful. |
| Protect feet and ankles from knocks | Expert advice ([DermNet](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/livedoid-vasculopathy)). | Scarred ankle skin heals slowly. |
| No smoking or nicotine | Two French patients reached long remission after stopping smoking ([Gardette et al. 2018](https://medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-2965)). | Only relevant if you use nicotine. |
| Weight and blood pressure | Described as co-factors, but no proof they change flares ([AWMF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)). | Good for general health. |
| Special diet | One single case report ([BMJ Case Reports 2021](https://casereports.bmj.com/content/14/2/e237895)). | Not enough to rely on. |

**Sport while on rivaroxaban.** Non-contact sport is considered safe. Contact and collision sport is traditionally discouraged, and Singapore patient advice says to avoid activities likely to cause injury ([ACC 2023](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2023/08/31/11/58/antithrombotic-therapy-in-athletes); [SingHealth](https://www.singhealth.com.sg/medicine/rivaroxaban)). This advice comes from people treated for vein clots, not LV.

### 5. Underlying causes and tests: one missing test could change your medicine

**Main point.** A fuller set of tests rarely finds something that cures LV. No study shows that people with a "found and treated" cause relapse less. But testing is useful in three ways: it can show rivaroxaban is the wrong drug, it can find a look-alike disease with its own treatment, and it can show how careful to be about stopping.

A clotting abnormality is found in fewer than half of LV patients in larger series, and treatment is the same with or without one, except for antiphospholipid antibodies ([AWMF S1 guideline, 2025](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)).

| Test (if not already done) | Why it matters | Affected by rivaroxaban? |
|---|---|---|
| **Anti-beta-2 glycoprotein I, IgG and IgM** | Completes the screen for antiphospholipid antibodies (immune proteins that make blood clot too easily). Your other two tests are recorded as normal. The guideline says this drug family should not be used if these antibodies are present, because rivaroxaban did worse than warfarin in people with antiphospholipid syndrome ([AWMF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)). A positive result needs a repeat test some weeks later. | No ([Moser and Smock 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8791165)) |
| Protein C, protein S, antithrombin | The inherited clotting problems that matter most in East and Southeast Asian people ([Tsuda et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7695562)). A real shortage would argue for more caution about stopping (an inference). | **Yes.** Some test methods read falsely normal on rivaroxaban. Ask the lab to use a method that is not affected. |
| Lipoprotein(a), a blood fat | Raised in about 42% of Brazilian and German LV patients but about 13% in one Chinese series ([Front Med 2022 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515); [Deng et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10897505/)). Matters for heart health. | Not expected (no source found) |
| Hepatitis B and C | Found in 7% and 4% of the Singapore series ([Gan et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23052434/)). Treatable. | No |
| ANCA, complement, urine test, kidney function | Look for vasculitis elsewhere in the body. The guideline lists ANCA vasculitis among the look-alikes. The urine and kidney tests are standard vasculitis checks, not taken from an LV source. | No |
| Protein electrophoresis, cryoglobulins | Look for abnormal blood proteins. Rare, but treating them cleared LV-like spots in case reports read only as summaries ([case report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6403116)). | Not expected |

**Tests with little value for you**

- **Factor V Leiden and prothrombin gene tests.** These are close to absent in Asian populations (0 to 0.2%). This figure is from a search summary ([Wang et al. 2018](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1186/s12959-017-0155-z)).
- **PAI-1 and MTHFR gene tests.** The PAI-1 variant is found in about 85% of LV patients tested, but it is also common in healthy people ([Gao and Jin 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8450804)). MTHFR adds nothing when homocysteine is normal, and yours is recorded as normal. B vitamins are only advised when homocysteine is high.

**About lipoprotein(a).** Even if it is high, there is no proven LV treatment for it. No drug that specifically lowers it is approved, according to secondary sources ([Medscape 2026](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/not-so-fast-throwing-out-lp-hypothesis-these-experts-say-2026a10011hv)). The first big trial (pelacarsen) lowered it by about 78% but did not reduce serious heart and vessel events ([Novartis release, 2026](https://www.novartis.com/news/media-releases/novartis-announces-lpahorizon-phase-iii-topline-results-pelacarsen-patients-elevated-lpa-and-established-cardiovascular-disease-cvd); [FH Europe summary](https://fhef.org/news/lpahorizon-results-of-landmark-clinical-trial-presented-at-aha-2026/)).

**About the mixed biopsy.** Some inflammation fits LV: it was seen in 38% of 358 LV biopsies ([Yuan et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37837185/)). But the guideline says clear inflammation or damage of the vessel wall should prompt a search for true vasculitis. The main look-alike is cutaneous polyarteritis nodosa, a vasculitis of deeper, medium-sized vessels. One older study found it on deep repeat biopsy in 6 of 29 patients with LV-type white scars; this was read only as a search summary ([Mimouni et al. 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12752140)). If a new spot appears, a deep biopsy from the edge of a fresh spot gives the clearest answer ([Burg et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9574051)).

### 6. Long-term safety: 10 mg is a low dose, with aspirin-like bleeding risk

**Main point.** Rivaroxaban is a clot-prevention medicine with many uses. Stroke prevention is one of them, at 15 or 20 mg, in older people with an irregular heartbeat. Your 10 mg is the low, preventive dose. Its bleeding risk in trials was low and similar to aspirin, but no study has measured it in healthy people under 50 or beyond about 5 years.

| Dose | Used for |
|---|---|
| **10 mg once a day** | Preventing clots after hip or knee replacement. Long-term prevention of repeat leg or lung clots. |
| 15 or 20 mg once a day | Preventing stroke in atrial fibrillation (an irregular heartbeat). |
| 15 mg twice a day, then 20 mg | Treating a new leg or lung clot. |

Source: [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/022406s041lbl.pdf). In Singapore, a generic 10 mg tablet is registered for two uses, and stroke prevention is not one of them ([National Drug Formulary](https://www.ndf.gov.sg/about-drugs/product-information/SIN17450P/)). LV itself is a clotting problem in the small vessels of the skin, which is why a clot-prevention tablet is used. Use in LV is off-label (outside the official registered uses), as are all LV treatments.

**Bleeding in the main trial** (vein-clot patients, median age about 60, treated for about a year) ([Weitz et al., NEJM 2017](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10050925/1/nejmoa1700518.pdf)):

| | Rivaroxaban 10 mg | Aspirin 100 mg |
|---|---|---|
| Major bleeding | 0.4% (5 of 1,127) | 0.3% (3 of 1,131) |
| Smaller bleeding needing medical attention | 2.0% | 1.8% |
| Any bleeding | 13.4% | 12.2% |

- **Longer term.** A 2025 trial followed people on reduced doses for a median 37 months. Bleeding needing medical attention reached 9.9% at 5 years, against 15.2% on full dose. These people had a median age of about 62, and the figures come from secondary summaries ([The Hospitalist summary of RENOVE](https://www.the-hospitalist.org/hospitalist/article/39034/in-the-literature/evidence-for-reduced-dose-doac-in-those-at-high-risk-for-vte-recurrence-in-extended-phase-anticoagulation-the-renove-trial/)).
- **Your age.** Bleeding is higher in people over 65, with poor kidney function, past bleeding, or who also take aspirin-type drugs ([Khan et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34516270/)). A healthy man in his 30s is probably below the trial average. This is an estimate, not a measured figure.
- **The risk adds up year by year.** It does not come from a build-up of damage. Studies in older heart patients found no sign of lasting harm to kidneys or bones ([kidney study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9996329/); [fracture analysis](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9168033/)). Liver injury is rare ([FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/022406s041lbl.pdf)).
- **In LV studies.** Bleeding was mostly heavier periods in women. No major bleed is described in the rivaroxaban LV series ([Deng et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10897505/)).

**Daily life on 10 mg** ([FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/022406s041lbl.pdf); [SingHealth](https://www.singhealth.com.sg/medicine/rivaroxaban))

- Take with or without food. No routine blood monitoring.
- Missed dose: take it the same day. Do not double up.
- Tell dentists and surgeons. The tablet is usually stopped at least 24 hours before a procedure.
- Avoid regular use of painkillers such as ibuprofen, and do not add aspirin without a clear reason. Both raise bleeding risk.
- In an emergency, hospitals can use clotting-factor concentrates. A specific reversal drug was withdrawn from the US market in December 2025; its availability in Singapore could not be confirmed ([AABB news](https://www.aabb.org/news-resources/news/article/2025/12/23/astrazeneca-withdraws-factor-xa-reversal-agent-from-u.s.-market)).
- Cost: Singapore's subsidised drug list names the 15 and 20 mg strengths, not 10 mg. No local price for 10 mg was found. About a dozen generic brands are registered, so asking for the cheapest one is reasonable ([National Drug Formulary](https://www.ndf.gov.sg/about-drugs/active-ingredient/m01108/)).

### 7. What is not known

- **No randomised trials.** Every number in this report comes from small, uncontrolled studies. Good results get published more often than bad ones.
- **Continue or stop?** No study compares staying on low-dose rivaroxaban with a planned stop or on-demand treatment. No study shows who will stay in remission.
- **How long to be stable before stopping.** No guideline says.
- **Tropical climates.** No relapse data exist from a place without a cool season. Statements in this report about Singapore's climate are inferences.
- **Men.** Most studies are 70 to 80% women. Data for men are thin.
- **Does LV fade with age?** Not studied. After more than 10 years, it is safer not to count on it.
- **Bleeding risk at your age, and beyond 5 years.** Not measured.
- **Your exact diagnosis.** No source says how to treat a case where LV and small vessel vasculitis overlap. Your response to rivaroxaban over the coming months is part of the answer.
- **Details not verified.** Every-other-day rivaroxaban, intermittent dabigatran, the Korean relapse figure, sulodexide availability in Singapore, and local prices for IVIG and JAK inhibitors.
