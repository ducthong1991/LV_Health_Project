# Other antithrombotic, antiplatelet, fibrinolytic and vasoactive drugs for keeping livedoid vasculopathy (LV) in remission (alternatives to rivaroxaban)

Research date: 21 Nov 2026. General information to discuss with a dermatologist or haematologist, not a treatment recommendation.

Evidence labels used below: [RCT] randomised trial; [Trial] non-randomised prospective trial; [Cohort] retrospective or prospective cohort / large series; [Series] small case series; [Case] case report; [SR] systematic review; [Review] narrative review; [Opinion] expert opinion or consensus.

Overall caveat that applies to every section: no randomised controlled trial of any drug exists in LV. The only formal drug trial is the single-arm rivaroxaban RILIVA study. Everything on the alternatives is case reports, small series, retrospective cohorts and reviews of those. Almost all studies measure ulcer healing and pain, not prevention of relapse. Most cohorts are majority female; male-specific data are essentially absent.

Method notes: PubMed E-utilities and Europe PMC full text were used directly; several key letters (Rajan 2021, Osada 2019, Sawada 2017, Yamaguchi 2017, Lee and Cho 2020, Gipple 2025) have no abstract and are paywalled (Wiley/OUP returned HTTP 403), so only their titles and secondary citations could be used. The JAMA Dermatology full text of Micieli and Alavi 2018 could not be opened; only the abstract was read.

## 1. Other direct oral anticoagulants (apixaban, edoxaban, dabigatran): what LV evidence exists, and is any a better long term choice?

### Takeaway
Evidence for apixaban, dabigatran and edoxaban in LV is limited to single case reports and 2-patient series (almost all from Japan and India); none has been compared with rivaroxaban, and reviews state explicitly that it has not been studied whether one anticoagulant is better than another. The most relevant items for "not staying on a daily anticoagulant forever" are a 2019 Japanese letter on long-term apixaban monotherapy and a 2021 Indian letter on intermittent low-dose dabigatran for maintenance, but neither has an abstract and the details could not be retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- [Review, patient information page, Vasc Med 2022] DOACs are "the most commonly prescribed treatments for LV"; apixaban, dabigatran and rivaroxaban are named as examples; the page states it has not been studied whether one blood thinner is more effective than another for LV. — [Eswaran et al. 2022, PMC9732783](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9732783)
- [Review, Front Med 2022, Münster/Hamburg group who ran RILIVA] "So far, other DOACs such as apixaban were not analyzed" in LV; efficacy is assumed from the shared factor Xa mechanism. Suggested apixaban dosing by analogy: 5 mg twice daily initially, reduced to 2.5 mg twice daily for maintenance. Dabigatran and edoxaban are not mentioned. The same review says LMWH or a DOAC (rivaroxaban or apixaban) is first line. — [Burg et al. 2022, PMC9574051](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9574051)
- [Cohort abstract, Adv Wound Care 2026] The German S1 guideline is summarised as recommending "low-molecular-weight heparins, rivaroxaban, and other direct oral anticoagulants as first-line therapy"; a 26-patient single-centre follow-up (treatment duration from at least 3 months to more than 24 months) reports effective long-term control of pain and disease activity with guideline-followed anticoagulation, but the abstract gives no relapse numbers and does not separate DOAC types. — [Hillgruber et al. 2026, PMID 41264385](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41264385/)
- Apixaban:
  - [Case, letter, J Eur Acad Dermatol Venereol 2017, Japan] "Rapid remission of severe pain from livedoid vasculopathy by apixaban" (title only; no abstract). — [Yamaguchi et al. 2017, PMID 27166520](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27166520/)
  - [Case, letter, J Dermatol 2019, Japan] "Prevention of recurrent ulceration of livedoid vasculopathy with long-term apixaban monotherapy" (title only; no abstract; dose and duration not retrievable). This is the only publication found whose stated topic is relapse prevention with a non-rivaroxaban DOAC as monotherapy. — [Osada et al. 2019, PMID 30156315](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30156315/)
  - [Secondary citation in a Spanish review of DOACs in dermatology] Apixaban 10 mg/day is cited (Yamaguchi) as a "success story" in LV refractory to vasodilators and/or antiplatelets; the review notes LV doses "tend to be lower" than those for licensed indications. — [Actas Dermo-Sifiliográficas, rivaroxaban in LV](https://actasdermo.org/en-treatment-livedoid-vasculopathy-with-rivaroxaban-articulo-S1578219018300118)
  - [Case, Eur J Case Rep Intern Med 2023, USA] A young man with LV and peripheral neuropathy was started on aspirin, pentoxifylline and apixaban "without much clinical improvement, especially regarding pain control" (a negative experience; inpatient, pain-focused). — [Palanisamy et al. 2023, PMC9930874](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9930874/)
  - [Case series/letter, Actas Dermosifiliogr 2022, Portugal] "Livedoid vasculopathy associated with PAI-1 polymorphisms treated with factor Xa inhibitors" (title only; which Xa inhibitors not confirmed). — [Bouceiro-Mendes et al. 2022, PMID 35636505](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35636505/)
- Dabigatran:
  - [Case, Clin Exp Dermatol 2017, Japan] "Dabigatran in the management of livedoid vasculopathy" (no abstract). — [Sawada and Suehiro 2017, PMID 28052437](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28052437/). A secondary source cites the dose as 220 mg/day in LV refractory to vasodilators and/or antiplatelets. — [Actas Dermo-Sifiliográficas](https://actasdermo.org/en-treatment-livedoid-vasculopathy-with-rivaroxaban-articulo-S1578219018300118)
  - [Case/letter, Dermatol Ther 2021, AIIMS Jodhpur, India] "A novel use of intermittent low dose dabigatran for maintenance of remission in livedoid vasculopathy" (no abstract; number of patients, dose and schedule not retrievable). — [Rajan et al. 2021, PMID 34028946](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34028946/). A later case report cites it as: "Intermittent low-dose dabigatran has also been proven to be effective in the maintenance of remission in LV" (the word "proven" overstates a letter-level report). — [Palanisamy et al. 2023, PMC9930874](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9930874/)
  - [Series, n=2, Hifu no Kagaku (Skin Research) 2023, Japan] Two women aged 35 and 25 with LV improved on anticoagulants including edoxaban plus antiplatelets and vasodilators, then relapsed; switching edoxaban to dabigatran gave "significant improvement". Authors: recent DOAC results in LV are "mixed"; "changing DOAC may be effective in the treatment of refractory livedo vasculopathy". Doses and follow-up not available (restricted full text). — [Kuwata and Takehara 2023, J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/skinresearch/22/1/22_42/_article/-char/en)
- Edoxaban:
  - [Case, BMJ Case Rep 2017, Japan] 17-year-old male, 1-year history, no identifiable coagulopathy, ulcers recurred in summer on antiplatelet therapy; low-dose edoxaban 15 mg/day gave epithelialisation of ulcers and disappearance of livedo racemosa within 8 weeks. — [Furukawa et al. 2017, PMID 28739563](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28739563/)
  - Two relapses on edoxaban-containing regimens (see Kuwata and Takehara above). — [J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/skinresearch/22/1/22_42/_article/-char/en)
- Singapore availability (HSA register of therapeutic products, open-data copy, queried by ATC code on 21 Nov 2026): apixaban (6 registered products, 2.5 mg and 5 mg), dabigatran (6 products incl. 75 mg and 110 mg), edoxaban (3 products incl. 15 mg and 60 mg) and rivaroxaban (33 products) are all registered, prescription only. — [HSA listing of registered therapeutic products, data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_767279312753558cbf19d48344577084/view)

### Inferences
- Switching from rivaroxaban to apixaban, dabigatran or edoxaban does not address the user's underlying concern: all are full anticoagulants used for stroke prevention in atrial fibrillation and venous thrombosis, with the same class bleeding risk. No LV publication claims any of them is safer or better for long-term use than rivaroxaban 10 mg.
- The published LV doses for the alternatives are low (edoxaban 15 mg/day, apixaban 2.5 mg twice daily maintenance by analogy, "low dose" dabigatran), mirroring the rivaroxaban 10 mg/day prophylactic-range practice.
- The only practical reasons the literature gives to change DOAC are (a) relapse or inadequate response on one agent (Kuwata 2023) or (b) individual tolerability; neither applies to someone currently in remission on rivaroxaban 10 mg.
- Dabigatran is the only non-rivaroxaban DOAC with a publication specifically about an intermittent maintenance schedule, but it rests on a single letter whose content could not be verified.

### Gaps
- Dose, schedule ("intermittent" meaning alternate-day, seasonal or on-demand), patient number and remission duration in Rajan et al. 2021 could not be retrieved (no abstract, Wiley 403). Worth obtaining the full text: DOI 10.1111/dth.14997.
- Dose and follow-up length in Osada et al. 2019 (apixaban long-term monotherapy), DOI 10.1111/1346-8138.14633, not retrievable.
- No head-to-head or even side-by-side cohort comparing DOACs in LV was found. General (non-LV) comparative bleeding data between DOACs were outside this search and are not cited here.
- The German S1 guideline text itself was not re-read (user already has it); whether its latest version ranks apixaban/dabigatran/edoxaban was not checked.

## 2. Low molecular weight heparin (LMWH) and warfarin: role, evidence, intermittent or seasonal use

### Takeaway
LMWH is the best-supported alternative to rivaroxaban for inducing remission (first line in German and French practice and the most used anticoagulant in the largest Chinese cohort), and is typically given for weeks to months until healing rather than indefinitely; warfarin, including "low-dose" warfarin with INR 1 to 1.5, has case-level support and has been used as long-term maintenance. Flare-limited courses of heparin are effectively what several cohorts did, but relapse after stopping is common (roughly 60% within 2 years in the largest cohort) and no study has tested a planned seasonal schedule.

### Cited Findings
LMWH / heparin
- [Review] LMWH is "widely accepted as first line treatment" and described as "most effective, safe and relatively favourable". Therapeutic dosing: tinzaparin 175 IU/kg once daily or enoxaparin 1 mg/kg twice daily; after pain relief and ulcer healing, reduce to a "semi-therapeutic" maintenance dose "up to an escape attempt" (i.e. a trial off treatment); continue full dose if high risk of progression. Risks: bleeding, haematoma, anaemia, injection-site reactions, allergy, heparin-induced thrombocytopenia; caution in renal impairment. Pain relief usually "within a few days". — [Burg et al. 2022, PMC9574051](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9574051)
- [Cohort within the RILIVA trial population, n=27, Germany] Review of prior treatment histories: heparin was most effective (12/17 responded), anti-inflammatory regimens were often used (17/24) but not effective (0/17). Authors conclude anticoagulant monotherapy should be first line. — [Weishaupt et al. 2019, PMID 31009111](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31009111/)
- [Cohort, n=26 (18 women, 8 men), France, median follow-up 5 years (range 1.2 to 9.4)] LMWH was the most prescribed therapy and had the best outcome (effective in 14 patients). Per-flare complete response: heparins (UFH+LMWH) 83%, vitamin K antagonists 59%, antiplatelets 33%, IVIG 63%. Tinzaparin at curative dose was given "from 15 days to 3 months until ulcer healing", i.e. per flare, not continuously. 54% (14 patients) had flares triggered by warm temperatures. Long-term remission (4 or more years without flare) in 9 patients (36%): 5 of these had no maintenance drug at all, 4 were on maintenance (VKA 2, aspirin 1, aspirin + VKA 1). Short-term remission (2 or more years) in 4 patients, all LMWH responders then kept on antiplatelet maintenance. Authors: "the benefit of anticoagulation maintenance therapy as secondary prevention has not been well evaluated". Two patients on VKA flared when INR was low and healed with dose adjustment. Fondaparinux in 2 patients: one thigh haematoma and one fatal haemorrhagic stroke at age 56. — [Gardette et al. 2018, Acta Derm Venereol](https://medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-2965)
- [Cohort, n=358, Xi'an, China, 2010 to 2022; largest LV series] 409 treatment episodes; 70.7% involved anticoagulation; LMWH was the predominant anticoagulant. Outcomes in 236 followed patients, defined after treatment ("recurrence" = new active ulcers within 2 years; "short-term remission" = 2 to 4 years; "long-term remission" = more than 4 years): anticoagulant alone n=165: long-term remission 21, short-term 39, recurrence 105 (63.6%); anticoagulant + glucocorticoid n=30: 7 / 6 / 17; glucocorticoid alone n=19: 1 / 4 / 14; anticoagulant + antiplatelet n=14: 2 / 4 / 8; antiplatelet alone n=8: 3 / 2 / 3. Overall 14.4% long-term remission, 23.3% short-term remission, 62.3% recurrence within 2 years. No significant difference between anticoagulant monotherapy and other approaches. Authors: anticoagulation works in most patients but "it is difficult to reduce short-term recurrence or prevent long-term relapse". Doses and durations are not reported. — [Ban et al. 2026, PMC13557247](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13557247/)
- [Prospective series, n=34 tested, 13 treated with warfarin or heparin, São Paulo] 11 of 13 responded; pain improved in 1 to 3 weeks (mean 1.8); complete healing in about 2.3 months; "Remission was sustained even after treatment interruption and lasted an average 7.8 months"; no severe adverse effects. — [Di Giacomo et al. 2010, PMID 20337814](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20337814/)
- [Series, n=27 (8 men), Taiwan 1991] After first-line aspirin + dipyridamole, subcutaneous heparin 5000 units once daily controlled intractable painful ulceration in 70% of the remaining patients; used "in active stage" only. 63% had summer exacerbation. — [Yang et al. 1991, PMID 1797367](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1797367/)
- [Case, Germany 2013] 50-year-old woman, rapid pain reduction and complete healing "after some weeks" of LMWH; LMWH called "one treatment of first choice". — [Becker et al. 2013, PMID 23821447](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23821447/)
- [Case report text, JAAD Case Rep 2025] Describes LMWH as given at 1 mg/kg every 12 hours for 6 months followed by once-daily dosing, and LV as following a relapsing-remitting course "with flares lasting 55 days and recurring every 8 months". — [JAAD Case Rep 2025, PMC12648687](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12648687/)
- [SR of anticoagulant literature inside a Thai cohort paper] 39 studies, n=219; anticoagulant monotherapy in 104 patients; mean anticoagulant duration 7.2 ± 3.8 months; improvement in 97/104 (93.3%); bleeding in 9 (8.7%). — [Rujitharanawong et al. 2021, PMID 33219608](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33219608/)

Warfarin / vitamin K antagonists
- [Series, n=3, all men aged 39, 30 and 18, India] Low-dose warfarin 1 to 2 mg/day, INR kept at 1 to 1.5 (below the usual 2 to 3 target). Pain relief within 15 days in all; complete healing in 1 to 2 months; continued 6 to 8 months. One patient (30 M) stopped after 8 months in remission and relapsed 1 year after stopping, then went back on warfarin 2 mg/day. Coagulation and autoantibody screens were normal in all three (similar to an idiopathic profile). No bleeding. — [Saoji and Madke 2017, PMC5618839](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5618839/)
- [Case, Japan] 28-year-old woman relapsing with new peripheral neuropathy on sarpogrelate; marked improvement after adding low-dose warfarin. — [Osada et al. 2010, PMID 20175830](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20175830/)
- [Case, USA] 50-year-old man with LV plus cryofibrinogenaemia and hyperhomocysteinaemia, refractory to multiple drugs, responded "dramatically" to warfarin. — [Browning and Callen 2006, PMID 16415390](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16415390/)
- [Case, Korea] 33-year-old woman with LV and primary antiphospholipid syndrome: aspirin plus low-dose warfarin gave near-complete remission at 2 months. — [So et al. 2015, PMC4622899](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4622899/)
- [Review table] Warfarin dosing in LV literature: 2 to 5 mg once daily then adjusted to INR 2 to 3; listed harms: teratogenicity, bleeding, skin necrosis, purple toe syndrome, osteoporosis, valve and artery calcification, many drug interactions. — [Bilgic et al. 2021, PMC8721056](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8721056/)
- [SR, Cureus 2022] States warfarin, subcutaneous heparin and tPA were used "with moderate effectiveness" and that they "are difficult to give or need regular monitoring, which often leads to a reduction in patient compliance"; rivaroxaban is preferred as injection-free and monitoring-free. — [Ramphall et al. 2022, PMC9420174](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9420174)
- Singapore: enoxaparin (9 registered products) and warfarin (6 products incl. 1 mg and 2 mg tablets) are registered; tinzaparin returned no entry. — [HSA register, data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_767279312753558cbf19d48344577084/view)

### Inferences
- In real-world cohorts heparin is mostly used as a time-limited induction (2 weeks to about 6 months), which is in effect "flare-triggered anticoagulation"; the price is a high relapse rate after stopping (63.6% within 2 years after anticoagulant-only treatment in the Chinese cohort; relapse after a mean 7.8 months in the Brazilian series).
- A minority do stay well off all drugs: 5 of 26 French patients were flare-free for 4 or more years with no maintenance, and 14.4% of the Chinese cohort had more than 4 years' remission. This supports a supervised trial off treatment as a legitimate question to raise, with an agreed plan to restart quickly.
- Compared with rivaroxaban 10 mg/day for a young man: LMWH means daily self-injection (unattractive for years, but usable for short flare courses); warfarin means INR monitoring, drug and diet interactions and no clear safety gain. Low-dose warfarin (INR 1 to 1.5) is an interesting low-intensity concept but rests on 3 patients, and one relapsed a year after stopping.
- The fondaparinux fatal haemorrhagic stroke (1 of 2 patients, age 56) in the French series is a reminder that serious bleeding does occur on LV anticoagulation, although it was at curative dosing with a different agent.

### Gaps
- No study of scheduled "summer-only" or "hot-month" LMWH or warfarin was found.
- The Chinese cohort does not report drug doses or durations, so its relapse figures cannot be tied to a specific regimen; follow-up was available for only 65.8% of patients.
- No data comparing relapse after stopping heparin versus after stopping rivaroxaban.

## 3. Antiplatelets and vasoactive drugs (aspirin, dipyridamole, clopidogrel, cilostazol, pentoxifylline, sulodexide, beraprost and other prostanoids, nifedipine): prevention of relapse

### Takeaway
Antiplatelet and haemorheological drugs are widely used as first-line or maintenance therapy in Asia, Brazil and Turkey because they are cheap and low risk, but their efficacy is clearly weaker than anticoagulation (for example 33% versus 83% complete response per flare in the French cohort; 1 of 6 responders in a Singapore open trial), and breakthrough flares on them are common. The most notable new non-rivaroxaban option is sulodexide (an oral heparinoid with low bleeding risk): a 2026 prospective Chinese study reported 88.6% complete resolution in 35 patients, but relapse-prevention data are not yet available and it does not appear to be registered in Singapore.

### Cited Findings
Aspirin
- [Open trial/series, n=6, National Skin Centre Singapore] 4 patients on pentoxifylline and 2 on aspirin assessed at 3 and 6 weeks: only 1 of 6 had a good response (a pentoxifylline patient). "Pentoxifylline or aspirin did not result in significant improvement for most of our patients when used alone"; most needed prednisolone, colchicine or azathioprine. — [Lee, Ang and Tan 2003, PMID 14716957](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14716957/)
- [Cohort, n=70, National Skin Centre Singapore 1997 to 2007, mean follow-up 50 months] Of 49 who achieved remission, 55% needed combination therapy (most often colchicine, pentoxifylline, prednisolone). Among those successfully treated with monotherapy: colchicine 59%, prednisolone 17.5%, pentoxifylline 17.5%, aspirin 6%. LV described as "high morbidity, chronic relapsing". Anticoagulants were barely used in that era. — [Gan et al. 2012, PMID 23052434](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23052434/)
- [Cohort, n=26 (20 men), AIIMS Bhubaneswar, India, retrospective, minimum 6 months' follow-up] Initial therapy rivaroxaban 10 mg daily (18) or aspirin 150 mg daily (8); the other drug was added if response incomplete at 3 months. At 3 months 17/26 (65.3%) had pain score 0 and complete healing (11 on rivaroxaban, 6 on aspirin). At 6 months 25/26 (96.1%): 11 rivaroxaban alone, 8 rivaroxaban + aspirin, 6 aspirin alone. One aspirin-only patient relapsed from month 4. No adverse effects; INR normal. No data after stopping. Notable as a mostly male cohort. — [Gupta et al. 2026, IJDVL](https://ijdvl.com/treatment-outcomes-in-patients-of-livedoid-vasculopathy-following-antithrombotic-therapy-a-retrospective-observational-study/); [PMID 41655089](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41655089/)
- [Cohort, France] Antiplatelets (aspirin 75 to 100 mg/day, clopidogrel 75 mg/day) gave 33% complete response per flare and "should not be recommended as a first-line treatment for flares"; but aspirin or clopidogrel was the maintenance drug in all 4 patients with 2 or more years' remission after LMWH, and in 2 of 4 maintained patients with 4 or more years' remission. — [Gardette et al. 2018](https://medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-2965)
- [Review] Aspirin 75 to 325 mg; Turkish centre prefers aspirin 300 mg once daily as initial therapy for patients without identified thrombophilia; dipyridamole or pentoxifylline added if unresponsive; anticoagulants second step. Harms: GI bleeding, urticaria/angioedema. — [Bilgic et al. 2021, PMC8721056](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8721056/)
- [Case report text, 2025] "The first-line therapy for patients without an identified thrombophilia is an antiplatelet agent, such as aspirin alone for initial therapy or in combination with dipyridamole. Pentoxifylline can be used in patients who cannot tolerate aspirin." — [JAAD Case Rep 2025, PMC12648687](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12648687/)
- [Cohort, n=75, São Paulo, Brazil 2005 to 2019] Most frequent treatments were aspirin, pentoxifylline and diosmin with hesperidin; "almost 40% of cases relapsed during spring and summer months" after treatment. — [Criado et al. 2021, PMID 33496999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33496999/)

Dipyridamole
- [Series, n=27, Taiwan] Low-dose aspirin + dipyridamole plus rest and wound care was first line; 13 patients responded "either at the first attack or the recurrent episodes". — [Yang et al. 1991, PMID 1797367](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1797367/)
- [Review table] Dipyridamole 50 mg twice daily initially, up to 75 mg four times daily; harms bleeding, dizziness. — [Bilgic et al. 2021, PMC8721056](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8721056/)

Clopidogrel and dual antiplatelet therapy
- [Series, n=17, Ahmedabad, India, 1-year follow-up] All patients received aspirin 75 mg + clopidogrel (stated as 150 mg once daily in the paper). 12/17 (70.6%) reached significant improvement in 8 to 12 weeks; the other 5 remitted within 2 months after adding pentoxifylline 400 mg three times daily. However 11 of 17 flared during the year on treatment (managed with triamcinolone injections or prednisolone); 6 stayed in remission on dual antiplatelets ± pentoxifylline. Adverse events: nausea, gastritis. — [Shah et al. 2022, PMC9650729](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9650729/)
- [Case, USA] 35-year-old woman with connective tissue disease: clopidogrel 75 mg/day healed ulcers in 4 months; a 3-week interruption caused a flare that resolved on restarting; no recurrence at 1 year on continued clopidogrel. Two earlier Spanish-language cases used 150 mg/day for 2 to 3 weeks then 75 mg/day for 3 months. — [Kunzler and Chong 2018, PMC5993557](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5993557/)

Pentoxifylline
- Singapore data above: good response in 1 of 4 as monotherapy (2003); 17.5% of monotherapy successes (2012). — [PMID 14716957](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14716957/); [PMID 23052434](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23052434/)
- [Case, Korea] 29-year-old woman had recurrent painful ulcerating plaques throughout 2 years of pentoxifylline 400 mg three times daily (plus 3 months of aspirin), i.e. pentoxifylline failed to prevent relapse. — [Song et al. 2020, PMC7875231](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7875231)
- [Review] Usual dose 400 mg three times daily; harms dyspepsia, nausea, abnormal liver tests. — [Bilgic et al. 2021, PMC8721056](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8721056/)

Sulodexide (oral heparinoid; described in the Korean case report as a purified glycosaminoglycan mixture of low-molecular-weight heparin 80% and dermatan sulfate 20%)
- [Trial, prospective single-arm, n=35, Xi'an, China, J Dtsch Dermatol Ges 2026] Sulodexide assessed at weeks 1, 4, 8 and 12: 31/35 (88.6%) achieved complete clinical resolution on monotherapy; 2 (5.7%) withdrew for suboptimal response; 2 (5.7%) needed add-on baricitinib; 8 (22.9%) mild sulodexide-related adverse events; no moderate or severe events. Dose and post-treatment relapse are not given in the abstract. — [Ban et al. 2026, PMID 41482702](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41482702/)
- [Case, Korea] The pentoxifylline-refractory patient above improved markedly within 2 months on oral sulodexide 250 lipasemic units three times daily. Authors stress it causes "minimal changes to coagulation profile and low bleeding risk" and call for comparisons with warfarin, heparin and DOACs. — [Song et al. 2020, PMC7875231](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7875231)
- [Review] Lists sulodexide among anticoagulant options for the step after antiplatelets. — [Seguí and Llamas-Velasco 2022, Front Med](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515)
- Singapore: no sulodexide product was found in the HSA register dataset by ATC code B01AB11 or by ingredient name (single automated lookup; should be confirmed with a pharmacist or HSA Infosearch). — [HSA register, data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_767279312753558cbf19d48344577084/view)

Cilostazol
- [Case, India] 25-year-old woman refractory to aspirin 75 mg + pentoxifylline, dapsone, corticosteroids, colchicine and several weeks of LMWH: cilostazol 50 mg once daily improved pain and ulcers within 2 weeks; a flare at about 2 months responded to twice-daily dosing; maintained about 3 months with aspirin and pentoxifylline, then tapered off; no relapse during 1 year of follow-up. No adverse effects; clotting parameters not prolonged. — [Mendiratta et al. 2016, IJDVL](https://ijdvl.com/cilostazol-a-novel-agent-in-recalcitrant-livedoid-vasculopathy/)
- [Review table] Cilostazol 100 mg twice daily; harms headache, diarrhoea, tachycardia, hypotension, arrhythmia. — [Bilgic et al. 2021, PMC8721056](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8721056/)
- Singapore: no cilostazol product was found in the HSA register dataset by ATC code B01AC23 or by ingredient name (same caveat as above). — [HSA register, data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_767279312753558cbf19d48344577084/view)

Prostanoids (beraprost, iloprost, alprostadil / PGE1) and sarpogrelate
- [Review table citing Tsutsui 1996, Japan] Beraprost sodium (oral prostacyclin analogue): 120 micrograms/day initially then 60 micrograms/day; harms headache, flushing, diarrhoea, leg pain, nausea. — [Bilgic et al. 2021, PMC8721056](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8721056/)
- [Review] Iloprost (intravenous) is the recommended add-on when anticoagulation is insufficient: up to 20 micrograms/day over 3 to 5 days, repeated every 4 weeks; harms headache, flush, nausea, hypotension; needs cardiovascular monitoring. No response rates given. — [Burg et al. 2022, PMC9574051](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9574051)
- [Case + literature review] Alprostadil (PGE1) in LV. — [Mofarrah et al. 2013, PMID 22364635](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22364635/). [Case, Japan] Lipo-PGE1 in livedoid vasculitis with essential cryoglobulinaemia. — [Kawakami et al. 2007, PMID 17725674](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17725674/). Abstracts not available for either.
- [Case, Japan] Sarpogrelate (a serotonin antagonist antiplatelet used in Japan) failed to prevent recurrence and neuropathy in one 28-year-old woman until low-dose warfarin was added. — [Osada et al. 2010, PMID 20175830](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20175830/)
- Singapore: no beraprost product found in the HSA register dataset (ATC B01AC19); iloprost is registered only as an inhalation solution. — [HSA register, data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_767279312753558cbf19d48344577084/view)

Nifedipine
- [Review] Adjuvant use in "anecdotal reports" only (Purcell and Hayes 1986). — [Seguí and Llamas-Velasco 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515). Dose in review table: 10 to 20 mg three times daily; harms hypotension, reflex tachycardia. — [Bilgic et al. 2021, PMC8721056](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8721056/)

Comparative position
- [Cohort, China] Among followed patients treated with antiplatelets alone (n=8): 3 long-term remission, 2 short-term remission, 3 recurrence within 2 years; anticoagulant + antiplatelet (n=14): 2 / 4 / 8. Numbers are too small for conclusions and there was no significant difference between regimens. — [Ban et al. 2026, PMC13557247](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13557247/)
- Singapore: aspirin 100 mg (general sale list), clopidogrel 75 mg (13 products), dipyridamole 75 mg tablets, pentoxifylline 400 mg SR are registered. — [HSA register, data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_767279312753558cbf19d48344577084/view)

### Inferences
- The most realistic "step-down" pattern documented in the literature is anticoagulant induction followed by antiplatelet maintenance (aspirin or clopidogrel): this is what the French patients with 2 or more years' remission received, and what several reviews describe. It has never been tested against continued anticoagulation, and the Indian dual-antiplatelet series (11/17 flared in a year) and Singapore's own 2003 and 2012 data show antiplatelets alone are frequently insufficient.
- Aspirin 100 to 150 mg/day carries its own bleeding risk (mainly gastrointestinal) but is not a full anticoagulant; in a young man with a normal thrombophilia screen it is the lowest-burden pharmacological maintenance to discuss, accepting a higher chance of breakthrough than on rivaroxaban.
- Sulodexide is the alternative with the most promising recent data and a plausibly lower bleeding risk, but: single-centre, single-arm, 12-week endpoint, no relapse data, and apparently not registered in Singapore (it is used in China, Korea and parts of Europe).
- Cilostazol, beraprost and nifedipine evidence is single-case level; they are reasonable only as add-ons, and cilostazol/beraprost may also be unavailable locally.

### Gaps
- No study of any antiplatelet or vasoactive drug uses relapse-free survival as its primary endpoint.
- Sulodexide dose, duration and post-treatment relapse in the 2026 prospective study (full text not retrieved).
- Original beraprost report (Tsutsui 1996) and the number of patients treated could not be verified; a search snippet suggested about 4 beraprost patients in a prostanoid literature review, but the source could not be confirmed.
- Singapore availability findings are from one automated dataset query; brand availability through hospital special-access routes was not checked.

## 4. Fibrinolytic or anabolic options: danazol, stanozolol, tissue plasminogen activator (tPA)

### Takeaway
Low-dose danazol (200 mg/day) is the second most commonly reported monotherapy in the 2018 JAMA Dermatology systematic review and stopped new lesions in small Taiwanese and Brazilian series, but it is recommended only for short courses (4 to 12 weeks) because of androgenic and metabolic effects, so it is not a long-term maintenance alternative. tPA is a rescue treatment for refractory ulcers given intravenously in hospital, not a preventive drug.

### Cited Findings
- [SR, 29 case series, 339 patients, JAMA Dermatol 2018] Anabolic steroids were the second most commonly used monotherapy after anticoagulants; "associated with good clinical outcomes". Adverse events overall in 44 patients (13%). — [Micieli and Alavi 2018, PMID 29141075](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29141075/)
- [Series, n=7, National Taiwan University Hospital] Danazol 200 mg/day orally: 6 completed; all 6 had "rapid cessation of new lesion formation", prompt pain reduction and ulcer healing; plasminogen rose and fibrinogen fell at 1 month (p=0.028); fibrinogen paralleled disease activity; well tolerated "without major side effects". — [Hsiao and Chiu 1997, PMID 9187843](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9187843/); earlier report [Hsiao and Chiu 1996, PMID 8678566](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8678566/)
- [Series, n=4, São Paulo; mean age 45, mean disease duration 19 years] Danazol 200 mg/day gave complete ulcer healing, less pain and a mean 70% (52 to 87%) reduction in lipoprotein(a). — [Criado et al. 2015, PMID 25824252](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25824252/)
- [Series, n=24, Nanjing, China] Combination anti-inflammatory therapy "plus antiplatelet/profibrinolytic drugs promoted quick healing of ulcer and reduce recurrence"; danazol was among the profibrinolytic maintenance drugs used (one patient on 200 mg every other day per a search summary of the full text). — [Feng et al. 2014, PMID 24352295](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24352295/); [full text](https://medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-1711)
- [Cohort, n=69, Guangzhou, China 2014 to 2024] "Androgen preparations" were used in only 3/69 (4.4%) of inpatients, versus anticoagulants 100% and antiplatelets 82.6%, showing androgens are now rarely used. — [Gao et al. 2025, PMC12495966](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12495966/)
- [Review table] Danazol 200 mg/day or 4 mg/kg/day "for a short duration of 4 to 12 week"; stanozolol 4 mg/day. Listed adverse effects: hirsutism, acne, "steroid-like side effects", alopecia, menstrual disturbance, clitoral hypertrophy (the list is oriented to women). — [Bilgic et al. 2021, PMC8721056](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8721056/)
- [Case report text, 2025] Danazol "use is restricted to short courses (4 to 12 weeks) due to androgenic and metabolic adverse effects". — [JAAD Case Rep 2025, PMC12648687](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12648687/)
- [Review] Danazol 200 mg/day is the most commonly used anabolic steroid, stanozolol 4 mg/day can also be used; steroids considered especially useful with associated connective tissue disease. In a Spanish survey of dermatologists (unpublished, up to 200 patients) the practical second line was anticoagulants rather than anabolic steroids. — [Seguí and Llamas-Velasco 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515)
- tPA:
  - [Case] 33-year-old woman, PAI-1 4G/4G with raised PAI-1: heparin plus tPA healed ulcers; "continuation of anticoagulant therapy with warfarin sodium and episodic administration of tissue plasminogen activator was required for symptomatic control". — [Deng et al. 2006, PMID 17116837](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17116837/)
  - [Review] Alteplase 10 mg IV (Klein and Pittelkow 1992) for non-healing ulcers; severe bleeding risk; efficacy and safety "should be further studied"; positioned third line. — [Seguí and Llamas-Velasco 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515). Regimen quoted elsewhere as 10 mg/day for 14 days. — [Bilgic et al. 2021, PMC8721056](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8721056/)
  - [Review] Fibrinolysis has "limitations in accessibility" and side effects "hard to manage in an ambulatory setting". — [Burg et al. 2022, PMC9574051](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9574051)
  - [Case report text] tPA "may be considered in patients resistant to anticoagulants, given as a short IV regimen followed by maintenance antiplatelet or anticoagulant therapy". — [PMC12648687](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12648687/)
- Singapore: danazol 200 mg capsules are registered (1 product, prescription only); no stanozolol product found. — [HSA register, data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_767279312753558cbf19d48344577084/view)

### Inferences
- Danazol's niche is short induction, particularly where lipoprotein(a) or fibrinogen is high or fibrinolysis is impaired; with normal thrombophilia tests and remission already achieved, it offers no obvious advantage and its side-effect profile is poorly suited to indefinite use.
- None of the fibrinolytic or anabolic options is a credible replacement for long-term maintenance in a man in his 30s; they are short-course or rescue tools.

### Gaps
- No LV source describing danazol or stanozolol adverse effects specifically in men was found (the LV reviews list mainly virilising effects in women). General pharmacology concerns in men (liver enzymes, lipids, suppression of the gonadal axis and fertility) are not documented in the LV literature retrieved and would need a drug-reference source.
- Relapse rates after stopping danazol are not reported in the abstracts of the Taiwanese or Brazilian series.
- Lipoprotein(a) and PAI-1 status are relevant to danazol/tPA responsiveness in the reports above; whether these were tested in the present case is unknown.

## 5. What the main systematic reviews rank as most effective, and what they say about maintenance versus on-demand treatment

### Takeaway
All systematic and major narrative reviews rank anticoagulants first (98% favourable response as monotherapy in Micieli and Alavi; about 82% for rivaroxaban, 93% for anticoagulant monotherapy in the Thai review), followed by anabolic steroids, IVIG and antiplatelets; none of them provides evidence on how long to treat, and several state explicitly that maintenance and secondary prevention have not been studied.

### Cited Findings
- [SR, JAMA Dermatol 2018] 29 case series, 339 patients (230 female, 69 male, 40 unstated), literature to June 2017. Anticoagulants were the most common monotherapy with favourable response in 62 of 63 patients (98%). Anabolic steroids, IVIG and antiplatelets were second, third and fourth most used; all "associated with good clinical outcomes". Adverse events in 44 patients (13%). Conclusion: varied treatments with varying success; RCTs needed. The abstract says nothing on maintenance duration or relapse. — [Micieli and Alavi 2018, PMID 29141075](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29141075/)
- [Review summarising Micieli] Rivaroxaban was the anticoagulant most often used, followed by LMWH, unfractionated heparin, warfarin and fluindione. — [Seguí and Llamas-Velasco 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515)
- [SR, Dermatol Ther 2021] Rivaroxaban: 13 studies, 73 patients, 10 to 20 mg/day; 60 (82.2%) achieved remission of pain and ulceration; few adverse effects. — [Gao and Jin 2021, PMID 34197012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34197012/). Reported remission lasted from 4 weeks to 23 months. — [Seguí and Llamas-Velasco 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515)
- [SR, Cureus 2022, rivaroxaban versus IVIG, 20 articles] Of 56 rivaroxaban patients, 21.4% relapsed; in the Korean series (Lee and Cho 2020, 20 patients) 10 of 13 relapsed after discontinuation, but relapses were milder and all were controlled by re-treatment. Of 62 IVIG patients, 20 had some recurrence; in one IVIG series median time to relapse was 10.7 months; one patient stayed in remission up to 7 years. — [Ramphall et al. 2022, PMC9420174](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9420174); original Korean letter [Lee and Cho 2020, PMID 31793049](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31793049/)
- [SR within Thai cohort paper, Dermatol Ther 2021] 39 studies, 219 patients on anticoagulants; as monotherapy in 104, mean duration 7.2 ± 3.8 months, improvement in 93.3%, bleeding in 8.7%. — [Rujitharanawong et al. 2021, PMID 33219608](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33219608/)
- [SR, Clin Exp Dermatol 2025] "Clinical and patient reported treatment outcomes of livedoid vasculopathy: a systematic review" exists (Oregon Health and Science University) but has no abstract; results not retrieved. — [Gipple et al. 2025, PMID 39298646](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39298646/)
- [SR, J Dermatolog Treat 2025] A systematic review of JAK inhibitors and biologics in LV exists (outside this note's scope; relevant as the main non-antithrombotic direction for refractory disease). — [Liu et al. 2025, PMID 39828272](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39828272/)
- [Opinion, international modified Delphi consensus, J Am Acad Dermatol 2013] Consensus document on diagnosis and management of LV (pre-dates DOAC data; abstract gives no regimen detail). — [Alavi et al. 2013, PMID 24028907](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24028907/)
- [Review with algorithm, Front Med 2022] "No standardized and evidence-based therapeutic strategies are published"; algorithm: therapeutic LMWH or DOAC first, step down to semi-therapeutic dose once stable, then an "escape attempt" (trial off); add IV iloprost every 4 weeks if insufficient; IVIG 2 g/kg every 4 weeks for resistant disease (reported benefit in more than 90%). — [Burg et al. 2022, PMC9574051](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9574051)
- [Review, Front Med 2022] Stepwise "therapeutic stair": antiplatelets first (aspirin, pentoxifylline, dipyridamole), anticoagulants second or first when thrombophilia is demonstrated; "the best evidence supporting use favors anticoagulants, especially rivaroxaban, antiplatelets, and intravenous immunoglobulin". IVIG: about 80 patients, 95% global response, remission 3 months to 8 years. — [Seguí and Llamas-Velasco 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515)
- [Review, Int J Womens Dermatol 2021] Antiplatelets and anticoagulants as first line; anabolic steroids, IVIG, hyperbaric oxygen, PUVA, vasodilators, fibrinolytics for unresponsive cases. — [Bilgic et al. 2021, PMC8721056](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8721056/)
- [Cohort] Explicit statement that maintenance is unstudied: "the benefit of anticoagulation maintenance therapy as secondary prevention has not been well evaluated". — [Gardette et al. 2018](https://medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-2965)
- [Cohort] "there are no reliable criteria to define cure and recurrence in LV"; anticoagulation "alone may be insufficient ... particularly in preventing recurrence". — [Ban et al. 2026, PMC13557247](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13557247/)

### Inferences
- The ranking "anticoagulants > anabolic steroids > IVIG > antiplatelets" reflects frequency of reporting and response of active disease in uncontrolled case series (strong publication bias toward successes), not comparative efficacy and not relapse prevention.
- The literature offers two contrasting philosophies without data to choose between them: the German approach (induce, step down, then attempt to stop: "escape attempt") and the cohort reality that most patients relapse within 2 years of stopping. Both imply that a trial off treatment is acceptable practice provided re-treatment is prompt.
- The 98% anticoagulant response in Micieli and Alavi rests on only 63 monotherapy patients.

### Gaps
- Per-class patient numbers and response rates for antiplatelets, anabolic steroids and IVIG in Micieli and Alavi (full text not accessible).
- Content of the 2025 Gipple systematic review.
- No Asian or Brazilian formal consensus statement on LV treatment was found; the Thai authors explicitly call for a guideline.

## 6. Published strategies for intermittent, seasonal or flare-triggered ("pill in the pocket") anticoagulation

### Takeaway
There is no trial or prospectively defined protocol for seasonal or on-demand anticoagulation in LV. What exists is (a) the retrospective rivaroxaban series the user already knows, in which stopping and re-treating at relapse and alternate-day dosing were used; (b) one Indian letter on intermittent low-dose dabigatran maintenance (details unavailable); (c) cohorts in which heparin or warfarin was given per flare; and (d) review-level statements that treatment can be intermittent in milder cases. Reported remission after stopping anticoagulation ranges from a mean of about 7 to 8 months to more than 4 years in a minority.

### Cited Findings
- [Cohort, n=34, China, JAAD Int 2024; already known to user, cited only for the strategy] All started rivaroxaban 10 mg/day; some adjusted to 10 mg every other day or 15 mg/day. 26/34 stopped because of symptom relief after a median 9.0 months; 10 had no recurrence over a median 17.5 months (2 for more than 3 years); 16 relapsed (median time to first relapse 7.0 months per the abstract-level summary). 3 patients with mild summer relapses chose not to restart and settled spontaneously as temperatures fell; 4 had heat-triggered relapses while on treatment. Authors: "Continuous treatment does not guarantee the absence of relapses, but most patients can still achieve remission after retreatment" and "individualized dose titration ... including discontinuation and retreatment based on the severity and preferences" is an option. — [Deng et al. 2024, PMC10897505](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10897505/)
- [Letter, Korea, n=20, via SR] 10 of 13 relapsed after stopping rivaroxaban; relapses were less severe than earlier episodes and all were controlled after re-treatment. — [Ramphall et al. 2022, PMC9420174](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9420174)
- [Letter, India] Intermittent low-dose dabigatran for maintenance of remission (schedule not retrievable). — [Rajan et al. 2021, PMID 34028946](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34028946/)
- [Prospective series, Brazil] After warfarin or heparin was stopped, remission lasted an average of 7.8 months. — [Di Giacomo et al. 2010, PMID 20337814](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20337814/)
- [Series, India] One of three men relapsed 1 year after stopping 8 months of low-dose warfarin. — [Saoji and Madke 2017, PMC5618839](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5618839/)
- [Cohort, France] Per-flare LMWH courses of 15 days to 3 months; 36% reached 4 or more years flare-free, more than half of them without any maintenance drug. — [Gardette et al. 2018](https://medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-2965)
- [Cohort, China, n=236 followed] After treatment, 62.3% relapsed within 2 years, 23.3% relapsed between 2 and 4 years, 14.4% stayed in remission more than 4 years. — [Ban et al. 2026, PMC13557247](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13557247/)
- [Case] Episodic tPA plus continuous warfarin was needed in one PAI-1 4G/4G patient (the only "episodic" fibrinolytic strategy reported). — [Deng et al. 2006, PMID 17116837](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17116837/)
- [Review, Brazil, An Bras Dermatol; update on ischaemic ulcers incl. LV] A search-result summary of this review states that treatment "can be continuous or intermittent in less severe cases"; the full text could not be opened (HTTP 403), so the wording and supporting references are unverified. — [Anais Brasileiros de Dermatologia review, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0365059624002630)
- [Review] German-style algorithm: reduce to semi-therapeutic dose once stable, then "escape attempt". — [Burg et al. 2022, PMC9574051](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9574051)
- [Case report text] Natural history figure quoted: flares last about 55 days and recur about every 8 months. — [PMC12648687](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12648687/)
- Seasonality data that underpin "seasonal" thinking: summer exacerbation in 82.4% (150/183) of a Chinese cohort — [Ban et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13557247/); 87.5% (21/24) — [Feng et al. 2014, PMID 24352295](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24352295/); 63% in Taiwan — [Yang et al. 1991, PMID 1797367](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1797367/); 54% heat-triggered in France — [Gardette et al. 2018](https://medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-2965); about 40% of Brazilian relapses in spring/summer — [Criado et al. 2021, PMID 33496999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33496999/). In contrast the German review says LV "occurs perennial, not seasonal" in their experience. — [Burg et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9574051)

### Inferences
- A seasonal schedule (treat only in hot months) is conceptually derived from temperate-climate cohorts. Singapore is hot and humid all year, so there is no natural "off season"; the seasonal strategy probably translates poorly, whereas a flare-triggered or reduced-frequency (alternate-day) strategy is the more applicable concept. This is an inference; no Singapore-specific seasonality data were found.
- A true "pill in the pocket" approach relies on fast onset: reviews report pain relief within days of starting anticoagulation (and RILIVA-type data show rapid pain response), which makes early self-recognised re-treatment plausible, but the risk is that each flare can leave permanent atrophie blanche scarring before treatment takes effect. No study has measured scarring or cumulative damage under on-demand versus continuous treatment.
- Across sources, expected time to relapse after stopping anticoagulation clusters around 7 to 12 months, with roughly 60 to 77% relapsing (62.3% within 2 years in the Chinese cohort, 16/26 in the Chinese rivaroxaban series, 10/13 in the Korean series). Multi-year (4 or more years) drug-free remission was seen in about 14 to 19% in the two cohorts that report it (34/236 Chinese; 5/26 French), and 10/26 had no relapse over a median 17.5 months in the rivaroxaban series. With a history of more than 10 years of recurrences, the prior probability of relapse off treatment is probably at the higher end (inference).
- Relapses after stopping were generally described as milder and re-treatable (Korean and Chinese rivaroxaban series), which is the main reassurance for a supervised trial off or dose-frequency reduction.

### Gaps
- No prospective study, registry or guideline defines criteria for stopping, minimum duration in remission before stopping, or a re-treatment trigger.
- No published early-warning markers validated for pre-emptive re-treatment (the 2026 rivaroxaban haematology paper suggests platelet indices correlate with severity, but not as relapse predictors: [Liu et al. 2026, PMID 41527208](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41527208/)).
- Details of the dabigatran intermittent regimen (see section 1).

## 7. Availability and use in Singapore and Asia; Asian cohorts

### Takeaway
Asian practice is heterogeneous: Singapore's published National Skin Centre experience (1997 to 2007) relied on colchicine, pentoxifylline, prednisolone and aspirin with little anticoagulation; Thailand used combination anti-inflammatory/antiplatelet therapy with anticoagulant or PUVA as add-on; China now uses LMWH and rivaroxaban most, with sulodexide emerging; Japan has contributed most of the apixaban, dabigatran, edoxaban, beraprost, sarpogrelate and low-dose warfarin case reports; India reports aspirin, dual antiplatelets, low-dose warfarin, cilostazol and intermittent dabigatran. In Singapore, all four DOACs, enoxaparin, warfarin, aspirin, clopidogrel, dipyridamole, pentoxifylline and danazol are registered; sulodexide, cilostazol, beraprost and stanozolol were not found in the HSA register dataset.

### Cited Findings
- Singapore
  - [Cohort, n=70, National Skin Centre, 1997 to 2007] Mean age 39, female:male 3:1, no racial predilection; peripheral neuropathy 9%; 49 achieved remission, 55% of them needing combination therapy (colchicine, pentoxifylline, prednisolone); monotherapy successes: colchicine 59%, prednisolone 17.5%, pentoxifylline 17.5%, aspirin 6%; mean follow-up 50 months. — [Gan et al. 2012, Ann Acad Med Singap, PMID 23052434](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23052434/)
  - [Open trial, n=6, National Skin Centre] Aspirin or pentoxifylline alone: 1 of 6 good response. — [Lee et al. 2003, Ann Acad Med Singap, PMID 14716957](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14716957/)
  - [Case, Singapore Med J 2012] LV associated with factor V Leiden mutation reported from Singapore (title only). — [Yong et al. 2012, PMID 23268168](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23268168/)
  - HSA register (queried 21 Nov 2026 by ATC code): registered: rivaroxaban (33 products), apixaban (6), dabigatran (6), edoxaban (3), enoxaparin (9), warfarin (6), clopidogrel (13), aspirin antithrombotic (4), dipyridamole (2), pentoxifylline (1, 400 mg SR), danazol (1, 200 mg), iloprost (1, inhalation only). Not found: sulodexide, cilostazol, beraprost, stanozolol, tinzaparin. — [HSA listing of registered therapeutic products, data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_767279312753558cbf19d48344577084/view)
- Thailand
  - [Cohort, n=74, Siriraj Hospital] Mean age 37.6, female:male 5.2:1, median follow-up 10.5 months; 48 improved (median 11.4 months of treatment); combinations of anti-inflammatories, antiplatelets and immunosuppressants were usual; add-on anticoagulant or PUVA improved most patients so treated; Kaplan-Meier probability of improvement 38.5%, 53.7% and 57.9% at 1, 2 and 3 years (i.e. slow and incomplete control on non-anticoagulant-based regimens). — [Rujitharanawong et al. 2021, PMID 33219608](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33219608/)
- China / Taiwan
  - [Cohort, n=358, Xi'an] LMWH predominant; 62.3% recurrence within 2 years; summer exacerbation 82.4%; mean onset age 28. — [Ban et al. 2026, PMC13557247](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13557247/)
  - [Trial, n=35, Xi'an] Sulodexide 88.6% complete resolution. — [Ban et al. 2026, PMID 41482702](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41482702/)
  - [Cohort, n=69, Guangzhou] All inpatients received combination therapy: anticoagulant 100%, antiplatelet 82.6%, corticosteroid 56.5%, immunosuppressant 43.5%, lipid-lowering 21.7%, androgens 4.4%, hyperbaric oxygen 10.1%; ulcer healing in 11.5 ± 4.6 days; example regimen rivaroxaban 15 mg + prednisone 15 mg + aspirin 50 mg daily. No relapse data. — [Gao et al. 2025, PMC12495966](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12495966/)
  - [Cohorts, rivaroxaban] 10 mg/day gave disease control in 78.0% of 41 patients within 3 weeks (adverse effects: 6 menorrhagia, 2 haematochezia, 1 allergy) — [Liu et al. 2026, PMID 41527208](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41527208/); 91% (40/44) ulcer remission within 12 weeks, mild side effects 25% (mostly menorrhagia) — [Zhao et al. 2024, PMID 38287670](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38287670/). (Included only to show that the dominant bleeding events in Chinese cohorts are menstrual, which do not apply to men.)

### Inferences
- The two Indian reports with predominantly male, thrombophilia-negative patients (Gupta 2026: 20 of 26 men; Saoji 2017: 3 men with normal screens) are the closest published matches to a young Asian man with idiopathic LV; both show that low-intensity antithrombotic regimens (aspirin 150 mg; warfarin 1 to 2 mg with INR 1 to 1.5) can control disease in some such patients, at case-series level.
- Singapore's own published cohorts pre-date the DOAC era and show poor results from aspirin/pentoxifylline monotherapy, which may explain local preference for rivaroxaban today. No Singapore outcome data on rivaroxaban, other DOACs, or maintenance strategies have been published as far as this search found.
- Options realistically obtainable in Singapore without special import appear to be: another DOAC, enoxaparin, warfarin, aspirin, clopidogrel, dipyridamole, pentoxifylline, danazol. Sulodexide, cilostazol and beraprost would likely need a special access route or are simply not used locally (to be confirmed).

### Gaps
- The HSA lookup was a single automated query of the open-data register by ATC code and ingredient name; a negative result may reflect coding differences or products supplied via exemption routes. Confirm with HSA Infosearch or a hospital pharmacist.
- No Singapore, Malaysian or other South-East Asian publication on rivaroxaban or other DOAC maintenance in LV was found; no post-2012 Singapore LV cohort was found.
