# Immune-modulating, anti-inflammatory and newer or experimental treatments for preventing recurrence of livedoid vasculopathy (LV), as of November 2026

Reader notes (apply to every section):
- This is general information to discuss with a dermatologist, not a treatment recommendation.
- There are no randomised controlled trials of any LV treatment. Every figure below comes from case reports, retrospective series, single-arm studies or reviews of those. Evidence labels used: [SR] systematic review of case-level data; [Prospective single-arm]; [Retro series n=x]; [Case n=1]; [Narrative review]; [Regulator]; [Registry].
- How each source was read is flagged where it matters: "full text" (article opened), "abstract" (only abstract available), "search snippet" (seen only in search-result summary, not opened; treat as lower confidence).
- Almost all reports measure ulcer healing and pain, not relapse prevention. Relapse data after stopping treatment are sparse for every option.

## 1. Intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG): response, schedules, remission length, maintenance use, cost, Singapore access

### Takeaway
IVIG is the best-documented non-anticoagulant option (about 80 published patients, roughly 60 to 95% remission of an episode), but it does not cure: most patients relapse, typically 6 to 13 months after a course, with remission after the first course lasting longer (median about 27 months in one series) than after later courses (about 7.5 months). It is used as pulsed courses repeated on relapse, and in a minority as ongoing 4 to 8 weekly maintenance; no Singapore price was found and it would be off-label.

### Cited Findings
- [SR, 17 articles, n=80, abstract] IVIG 1 to 2.1 g/kg every 4 weeks; "effective rate" 95% (76/80); good response for pain, ulceration and neurological symptoms; no severe adverse events reported; authors note no standardised regimen and no RCTs — [Gao and Jin 2022, Ther Adv Chronic Dis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9134453/)
- [Narrative review citing the Gao SR, full text] Response usually begins between the first and third cycle; remission after IVIG "ranged from 3 months to 8 years"; headache the most common adverse event — [Seguí and Llamas-Velasco 2022, Front Med](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515/full)
- [Retro series n=11, 29 disease episodes, Vienna, abstract] 2 g/kg every 4 weeks. Complete remission of ulcers and pain in 17/29 episodes (59%) after 3 cycles, 25/29 (86%) after 6 cycles, 27/29 (93%) after up to 8 cycles; pain resolved in 80% after 2 cycles. Median remission duration 26.7 months after the initial episode and 7.5 months after subsequent episodes — [Monshi et al. 2014, J Am Acad Dermatol](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25022849); same abstract at [em-consulte](https://www.em-consulte.com/en/article/923115/iconosup)
- [Retro series n=25 of 32 treated, Tübingen, full text] 2 g/kg over 5 days every 25 to 28 days as inpatient treatment; mean 6.8 cycles (range 1 to 45); mean follow-up 28.9 months. Complete remission of all symptoms in 17/25 (68%) after a mean 4.4 cycles (range 1 to 14). 11 patients stopped right after remission; 6 continued IVIG after remission; 2 of the 17 relapsed within 3 months and stayed on IVIG (one had 45 cycles). 92% tolerated it well (2 patients with headache, nausea, dizziness); no aseptic meningitis, anaphylaxis or renal failure. 92% were also on aspirin, heparin or an oral anticoagulant, so "a synergistic effect ... cannot be ruled out". Authors advise that "therapy should be paused in case of complete remission or stable findings for at least 6 months". In Germany IVIG is off-label for LV and needs insurer pre-approval; authors call it "associated with high costs" — [Kofler et al. 2021, J Cutan Med Surg](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8474298/)
- [Retro series n=7, Korea, abstract] "Pulsed" IVIG 2 g/kg monthly for only 2 to 3 cycles; mean follow-up 51.9 months. Clinical score fell from 5.7 to 1.1. Six of seven relapsed at a mean of 12.7 ± 2.8 months (2 re-treated with IVIG, the others controlled with conventional therapy); one patient had no recurrence for over 7 years — [Kim et al. 2015, Dermatol Ther](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25845419)
- [Retro series n=5, Paris, abstract] Complete remission in 4/5; 3 relapsed at a median of 10.7 months; re-treatment with IVIG worked in all 3 — [Bounfour et al. 2013, Int J Dermatol](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/23488633)
- [Retro series n=9, abstract] Patients resistant to immunosuppressants plus anticoagulants; IVIG 2 g/kg monthly; complete response 6, partial 3 — [Ozden et al. 2020, Dermatol Ther](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/31965687). A later review reports 2 of these relapsed at 6 months with milder disease — [Ramphall et al. 2022, Cureus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9420174/)
- [Case n=1, search snippet and review citation] Severe LV resistant to immunosuppressive plus antithrombotic drugs stayed in remission for 7 years after just two IVIG cycles; authors propose short or on-demand IVIG for flares — [Takahagi et al. 2021, J Cutan Immunol Allergy](https://www.frontierspartnerships.org/journals/journal-of-cutaneous-immunology-and-allergy/articles/10.1002/cia2.12202/full)
- [SR, 10 IVIG studies n=62 vs 10 rivaroxaban studies n=56, full text] Mean time to first response about 2.3 months (IVIG) vs 2.4 months (rivaroxaban). Recurrence reported in 20/62 (about 32%) after IVIG vs 12/56 (21.4%) on or after rivaroxaban. Maintenance patterns described in the included reports: 2 g/kg initially then 1 to 2 g/kg every 4 to 8 weeks (Pitarch), or repeat courses roughly yearly with or without conventional treatment (Kim). No formal maintenance trial. Review has internal count inconsistencies (138 vs 118 total patients) — [Ramphall et al. 2022, Cureus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9420174/)
- [Retro multicentre series n=26, France, abstract/search snippet] 8 patients refractory to anticoagulation received IVIG, with good response in 6 — [Gardette et al. 2018, Acta Derm Venereol](https://medicaljournals.se/acta/content/abstract/10.2340/00015555-2965)
- [SR of all LV treatments to 2017, 29 case series, n=339, abstract] IVIG was the third most commonly used monotherapy after anticoagulants (favourable response 62/63, 98%) and anabolic steroids — [Micieli and Alavi 2018, JAMA Dermatol](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/29141075)
- [SR with treatment algorithm, full text] Algorithm places IVIG third, after anticoagulation and iloprost, "for therapy-resistant cases", 2 g/kg over 2 to 5 days every 4 weeks; cost noted as a limitation — [Burg et al. 2022, Front Med](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.1012178/full)
- [German-language review aligned to the S1 guideline, full text] IVIG reserved for therapy resistance, after insurer approval; "cost and limited availability should be weighed first" — [PMC9939248](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9939248/)
- [Case n=1, search snippet] LV with features of undifferentiated connective tissue disease reached "complete, durable remission" on IVIG plus hydroxychloroquine — [Aung and Cheah 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12535920/)
- [Singapore access] Singapore's National Drug Formulary lists several normal human immunoglobulin products as available in public healthcare institutions (no prices shown) — [NDF Singapore](https://www.ndf.gov.sg/about-drugs/active-ingredient/mb0011/). The Medication Assistance Fund subsidises listed high-cost drugs only "for specific indications"; citizens up to 75% by means test — [MOH Singapore MAF page](https://www.moh.gov.sg/managing-expenses/schemes-and-subsidies/subsidies-for-drugs-on-the-Medication-Assistance-Fund-(MAF)-list-at-public-healthcare-institutions/)
- [Regional cost reference, not Singapore] A Malaysian tertiary hospital cost analysis used a mean IVIG cost of MYR 236.29 (about USD 57) per gram — [J Pharm Policy Pract 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9040375)

### Inferences
- IVIG behaves as a remission-inducing course, not a cure: across the series with relapse data (Monshi, Kim, Bounfour, Ozden) most patients eventually relapse, usually within about 6 to 13 months, and re-treatment works again. Isolated patients stay clear for 7 years or more (Kim, Takahagi), but these are 2 individuals and spontaneous remission cannot be excluded.
- Two practical patterns exist in the literature: (a) a short pulsed course (2 to 6 monthly cycles), stop, and repeat on relapse; (b) ongoing 4 to 8 weekly infusions in a minority who relapse quickly. Pattern (a) is the closest thing to "periodic treatment instead of daily tablets", but in most reports patients stayed on antithrombotic co-medication, so IVIG has rarely been tested as a true stand-alone replacement for an anticoagulant.
- Rough cost arithmetic (illustrative only, using the non-Singapore figure above): a 2 g/kg cycle for a 70 kg adult is 140 g; at about USD 57 per gram that is roughly USD 8,000 per cycle for drug alone, before infusion or admission charges. Singapore prices are likely different and were not found.
- Because LV is not a registered indication, IVIG in Singapore would probably be off-label and unlikely to attract indication-specific subsidy; this needs confirmation with the treating hospital.

### Gaps
- No Singapore per-gram price, no confirmation of subsidy status or MediShield/Integrated Shield coverage for IVIG in LV, and no published Singapore LV patients treated with IVIG were found.
- No controlled comparison of IVIG versus continued low-dose rivaroxaban for relapse prevention exists.
- The 2025/2026 update of the German S1 guideline (AWMF register 013-098; [PDF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)) is listed on the AWMF register (search metadata suggested a version dated 1 Oct 2025, valid to 30 Sep 2030, but the file name says 2026-05) and could not be text-extracted in this session, so its current wording on IVIG and other options is unverified. The 2021 version is [Schiffmann et al., JDDG 2021](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/34739187) (paywalled).
- Full texts of Monshi 2014 and Kim 2015 were not accessible; adverse events and exact follow-up come from abstracts only.

## 2. JAK inhibitors (baricitinib, tofacitinib, upadacitinib, abrocitinib, ruxolitinib): published LV cases, relapse after stopping, safety in a young man

### Takeaway
Roughly 30 to 45 LV patients treated with JAK inhibitors have been published (2020 to 2026, almost all from China, mostly young, mostly refractory to anticoagulants), with improvement in about 80 to 100% within 1 to 3 months, but follow-up is short and where the drug was stopped or tapered relapse was common (for example 2 of 4, 1 of 3, 1 of 2 in the series that report it). No thrombotic events have been reported in LV patients, but the class carries a regulator boxed warning for blood clots and the LV sample is far too small to reassure.

### Cited Findings
- [SR to June 2024, 15 articles, abstract] 41 LV patients treated with biologics or JAK inhibitors; 36 (87.8%) had a positive response including pain relief; TNF inhibitors were the most common monotherapy, then JAK inhibitors; adverse events uncommon — [Liu, Li and Shi 2025, J Dermatolog Treat](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/39828272) (publisher page [tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09546634.2025.2451804) returned 403). As re-quoted by a later paper: 17 of these were JAK inhibitor patients (baricitinib 14, tofacitinib 2, abrocitinib 1), 13/17 met treatment goals within 2 months, with no relapse recorded during follow-up of up to 18 months — [Zhao et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12803528/)
- [Narrative review, 18 reports, about 29 de-duplicated patients, full text] Approximate split: baricitinib about 16, tofacitinib 5, abrocitinib 5, upadacitinib 3. Healing or remission generally within 1 to 4 months. No thrombotic events reported, but the authors state the sample is too small to offer reassurance, that LV "is itself a thrombotic microangiopathy", and recommend baseline cardiovascular and thrombophilia assessment plus monitoring of blood counts, lipids and liver function. They flag publication bias and overlapping same-centre reports — [Wang X et al. 2026, Clin Cosmet Investig Dermatol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13714699/)
- Baricitinib:
  - [Retro series n=8, Peking Univ First Hospital, full text] 2 mg/day, other LV drugs stopped. Clinical score 7.0 to 1.4; 6/8 reached remission in 3 to 13 weeks (mean 7.75); follow-up only 10 to 28 weeks; no adverse events including no DVT; no stopping or relapse data. Authors suggest long-term use to prevent flares — [Han and Tu 2022, Front Immunol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9646531/). An earlier 3-patient report from the same centre (Song and Tu 2022, JAMA Dermatol) likely overlaps — tabulated in [Zheng et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12634492/)
  - [Prospective single-arm open-label n=10 plus histology of 36, full text] Baricitinib 2 mg (n=3) or 4 mg (n=7) daily; 8/10 (80%) improved at 1 and 3 months; both non-responders were on 2 mg; follow-up 9 to 12 weeks; no safety data and no relapse data given — [JAAD International, Dec 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12808936/)
  - [Retro n=3 males, Wuhan, full text] Two patients in sustained remission at 12 and 20 months (apparently still on treatment); the third responded slowly, had recurrent ankle pain within 3 months of tapering baricitinib, then improved on tofacitinib 5 mg twice daily. Lesional pJAK3 was higher in LV than in atopic dermatitis; authors hypothesise biomarker-guided drug choice — [Yu et al. 2025/2026, JAAD International](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12814677/)
  - [Retro n=2, Chengdu, full text] Cumulative exposure over 62 and 75 weeks. Lesions recurred during a 12-week interruption in one patient and after dose taper plus COVID-19 infection in the other; one relapsed after baricitinib was stopped; both responded again on re-treatment. No thrombosis — [Xiao et al. 2026, Indian J Dermatol Venereol Leprol](https://ijdvl.com/long-term-efficacy-and-safety-of-jaki-in-refractory-livedoid-vasculopathy/)
- Tofacitinib: [Case n=1] 17-year-old male, 5 mg twice daily, healed by month 1, 56 weeks follow-up (Jia et al. 2020, tabulated in [Zheng et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12634492/)); [Case n=1, abstract] woman, 5 mg twice daily, healed over 2 to 3 months, no adverse events over 1 year — [Mansouri et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12119902/); [Case n=1, abstract] 37-year-old man with 8 years of LV and a VIM gene variant, improved after 3 months — [Zhu et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13189076/); [Case n=1, full text] 65-year-old woman, tofacitinib plus hyperbaric oxygen, healed after 10 HBOT sessions, only 30 days follow-up — [Mani et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12854586/)
- Upadacitinib: [Case n=1, full text] 30-year-old woman whose biopsy showed fibrinoid necrosis with lymphocytic and neutrophilic infiltrate, failed rivaroxaban, aspirin, mycophenolate and prednisone; upadacitinib 15 mg/day nearly cleared ulcers by 6 weeks; 20 weeks follow-up; 6-month maintenance planned — [Zheng et al. 2025, An Bras Dermatol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12634492/); [Case n=1, abstract] 54-year-old woman, 15 mg/day, near-complete healing by day 52, adverse event orolabial herpes simplex — [Wang C et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12533487/)
- Abrocitinib (JAK1-selective):
  - [Retro n=4 (abrocitinib 3, upadacitinib 1), full text] Remission in 5 to 8 weeks. Drug stopped at 10 to 32 weeks. Two of four relapsed after stopping (one about 32 weeks after stopping, one about 1.5 years after stopping); both responded to re-treatment within 4 weeks. One mild cholesterol rise — [Zhao et al. 2026, Case Rep Dermatol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12803528/)
  - [Case n=1, full text] 48-year-old man, 100 mg daily, tapered from month 3, stopped at month 6, no recurrence at 2 months off drug; "durability of remission is unknown" — [Wang X et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13714699/)
  - [Case n=1 plus review, full text] 35-year-old man with heterozygous factor V Leiden whose LV relapsed after 2 years controlled on rivaroxaban 20 mg; abrocitinib 200 mg/day gave complete remission in about 2 months, later switched to baricitinib 4 mg/day; rivaroxaban was stopped; complete response maintained at 10 months on the JAK inhibitor. Their review table: baricitinib 13, tofacitinib 3, upadacitinib 1, abrocitinib 1; relapse after discontinuation and thrombotic adverse events were not reported in any source report — [Criado et al. 2026, An Bras Dermatol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13049387/)
  - [Case n=1 within a multi-disease report] complete remission at 6 weeks, 12 weeks follow-up — [Chen et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10778201/)
- Ruxolitinib: no LV report found. The FDA notes its 2021 boxed-warning action does not cover ruxolitinib because it is used for blood disorders — [FDA Drug Safety Communication](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/fda-requires-warnings-about-increased-risk-serious-heart-related-events-cancer-blood-clots-and-death)
- [Prospective study of sulodexide n=35, abstract] 2 of 35 LV patients needed baricitinib added, showing JAK inhibitors are being used as add-on rescue in at least one large Chinese centre — [Ban et al. 2026, JDDG](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/41482702)
- Safety, class level:
  - [Regulator] FDA boxed warning (2021) for tofacitinib, baricitinib and upadacitinib covers serious heart-related events, cancer, blood clots and death. Basis: the ORAL Surveillance trial of tofacitinib vs TNF blockers in rheumatoid arthritis patients aged 50 or older with at least one cardiovascular risk factor, on methotrexate, median follow-up 4 years; clot and death risk were raised at both 5 mg and 10 mg twice daily. Baricitinib and upadacitinib were included by analogy. FDA advises reserving these drugs for patients who failed TNF blockers and weighing risk especially in current or past smokers and people with cardiovascular risk factors — [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/fda-requires-warnings-about-increased-risk-serious-heart-related-events-cancer-blood-clots-and-death)
  - [Meta-analyses in dermatology, search snippet only] Pooled dermatology trials found no significant increase in major cardiovascular events or venous thromboembolism versus placebo or comparator over a median of about 16 weeks; authors caution that evidence for longer use is insufficient and attribute the lower signal partly to younger, healthier patients — [JAMA Dermatology](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamadermatology/fullarticle/2811401) and [PMC10620674](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10620674)

### Inferences
- JAK inhibitors look like suppressive therapy, not a cure: relapse on taper, interruption or withdrawal is documented in at least 5 patients across three small series (Zhao 2/4, Yu 1/3, Xiao 2/2 including interruptions). Long relapse-free intervals off drug (for example 1.5 years before relapse) do occur.
- The reported population (Chinese, median age around 20 to 30, anticoagulant-refractory, often with visible inflammation on biopsy) suggests JAK inhibitors are being used where the picture is inflammatory or mixed. A biopsy with neutrophils and nuclear dust alongside fibrin thrombi is the type of case in which authors have reported JAK inhibitor responses (Zheng 2025), but that is a single case.
- For a man in his 30s without cardiovascular risk factors, the absolute clot risk implied by ORAL Surveillance (older rheumatoid arthritis patients) probably overstates his risk, but nobody has measured clot risk of a JAK inhibitor in LV specifically, a disease defined by microvascular thrombosis. Whether to keep an anticoagulant alongside is unstudied: some reports continued antithrombotics, Criado stopped rivaroxaban, Han stopped all other LV drugs.
- Infection risk (herpes simplex/zoster) and lipid rises are the adverse events actually observed in LV reports so far.

### Gaps
- No trial, no dose-finding, no head-to-head comparison between JAK inhibitors or against rivaroxaban or IVIG.
- No LV follow-up beyond about 3 years on any JAK inhibitor; most reports are under 6 months.
- Full text of the Liu 2025 systematic review was not accessible; drug-by-drug counts for the biologics half of its 41 patients are unknown.
- No Singapore-specific data on off-label JAK inhibitor cost or subsidy for LV were found; no Singapore LV patient treated with a JAK inhibitor has been published as far as this search could tell.

## 3. Other immune or anti-inflammatory drugs (hydroxychloroquine, colchicine, dapsone, tetracyclines, methotrexate, mycophenolate, ciclosporin, anti-TNF, rituximab, dupilumab, steroids, danazol): any evidence for relapse prevention?

### Takeaway
None of these has relapse-prevention evidence beyond case reports or uncontrolled retrospective series. The most relevant local data point is a 70-patient National Skin Centre (Singapore) series in which colchicine was the most successful single agent and colchicine plus pentoxifylline plus prednisolone the commonest successful combination; anti-TNF agents have about 20 published patients; systemic steroids alone performed worst in the largest cohort (74% recurrence within 2 years).

### Cited Findings
- Colchicine, pentoxifylline, prednisolone (Singapore): [Retro series n=70, National Skin Centre Singapore, 1997 to 2007, abstract] Mean follow-up 50 months. 49 patients achieved remission; 55% of those needed combination therapy, most often colchicine, pentoxifylline and prednisolone. Among monotherapy successes: colchicine 59%, prednisolone 17.5%, pentoxifylline 17.5%, aspirin 6%. Positive ANA in 14%, anti-MPO 5%, anticardiolipin 7% — [Gan et al. 2012, Ann Acad Med Singap](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/23052434)
- Colchicine, dapsone, hydroxychloroquine as a group: described only as having "shown a favorable response" with no numbers, citing the Singapore series — [Seguí and Llamas-Velasco 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515/full)
- Hydroxychloroquine: [Case n=1, full text] 37-year-old woman, biopsy with fibrin thrombi and no vasculitis, no response to rivaroxaban 20 mg/day; partial response to prednisone 5 mg twice daily; ulcers resolved after adding hydroxychloroquine 200 mg/day; follow-up duration not stated — [JAAD Case Reports 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12648687/). [Case n=1, search snippet] IVIG plus hydroxychloroquine in LV with connective-tissue-disease features; authors say hydroxychloroquine's role "remains unclear" — [Aung and Cheah 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12535920/)
- Doxycycline, sulfasalazine, nicotinic acid, ciclosporin: "supported only by case reports" — [Burg et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.1012178/full). No minocycline report found.
- Methotrexate, mycophenolate, azathioprine, ciclosporin: appear in reports mainly as failed prior therapy (for example mycophenolate plus prednisone failed before upadacitinib in [Zheng et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12634492/); an Indian series notes patients arrived having already had steroids, azathioprine, methotrexate or ciclosporin and its authors regard steroids as potentially detrimental outside connective tissue disease — [IJDVL 2026 retrospective, n=26](https://ijdvl.com/treatment-outcomes-in-patients-of-livedoid-vasculopathy-following-antithrombotic-therapy-a-retrospective-observational-study/)). In a 69-patient Guangzhou inpatient cohort 43.5% received immunosuppressants and 56.5% corticosteroids alongside universal anticoagulation, with no per-drug outcomes or relapse data — [Clin Cosmet Investig Dermatol 2025](https://www.dovepress.com/clinical-characteristics-and-disease-course-of-livedoid-vasculopathy-a-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-CCID)
- Systemic corticosteroids: [Retro cohort n=358, 236 with follow-up, Xi'an, full text] Recurrence within 2 years: glucocorticoid alone 14/19 (74%), anticoagulant alone 105/165 (64%), anticoagulant plus glucocorticoid 17/30 (57%), anticoagulant plus antiplatelet 8/14 (57%), antiplatelet alone 3/8 (38%); differences not statistically significant; subgroups small — [Ban et al. 2026, Dermatol Pract Concept](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13557247/). Reviews call steroid use "controversial", mainly for flares — [Seguí and Llamas-Velasco 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515/full)
- Anti-TNF:
  - Etanercept [Retro n=5, abstract/search snippet] Patients resistant to steroids, antiplatelets or danazol; 25 to 50 mg weekly for 12 weeks; pain relief at a mean of 2 weeks; pain reduced 34.3% after 12 weeks — [Gao and Jin 2022, J Dermatolog Treat](https://doaj.org/article/b9f1ddd9aaec45dda55942fadb10ffff)
  - Adalimumab [Retro n=3, abstract] refractory LV, improved lesions and pain — [Huang et al. 2022, Vaccines](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9026708/); [Case n=1, search snippet] 24-year-old man with 18 years of recurrent LV, eight fortnightly 40 mg doses, near-complete healing — [Cureus 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10768940/). A German review states adalimumab "was used successfully in ten patients" (no primary citation retrieved) — [PMC9939248](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9939248/)
  - TNF inhibitors were the most common monotherapy among the 41 biologic/JAK patients in the 2025 systematic review — [Liu et al. 2025](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/39828272)
- Rituximab: [Case n=1, letter, no abstract] "successful use" in recalcitrant LV — [Zeni et al. 2008, Ann Rheum Dis](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/18556452). [Case n=1, abstract] LV in active rheumatoid arthritis, steroids plus rituximab gave partial response and ulcers recurred when the biologic was interrupted — [Cureus 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13426291/). [Case n=1, search snippet] remission on a six-drug combination including rituximab, hydroxychloroquine, aspirin, pentoxifylline, rivaroxaban and a calcium channel blocker, so rituximab's contribution cannot be isolated — [Brazilian case report 2024](https://periodicos.ufes.br/rbps/article/download/51190/35504/186208)
- Dupilumab or other biologics (IL-17, IL-23, IL-6, anifrolumab): no LV reports found in PubMed/Europe PMC searches 2020 to 2026.
- Anabolic steroids (danazol, stanozolol): second most commonly reported monotherapy in the 339-patient systematic review — [Micieli and Alavi 2018](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/29141075). [Case series n=4, abstract] low-dose danazol 200 mg/day gave complete ulcer healing, less pain and a 70% fall (52 to 87%) in lipoprotein(a) — [Criado et al. 2015, Dermatol Ther](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25824252). German review regards danazol/stanozolol as "less logical given the pathophysiology" — [PMC9939248](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9939248/)
- Iloprost (prostacyclin analogue, vasodilator/antiplatelet rather than immune): placed second line in one algorithm, IV up to 20 micrograms/day over 3 to 5 days every 4 weeks, no response data — [Burg et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.1012178/full)
- Sulodexide (oral heparin-like glycosaminoglycan; antithrombotic with endothelial effects, listed here because it is new): [Prospective single-centre n=35, abstract] 31/35 (88.6%) complete clinical resolution on monotherapy by 12 weeks; 2 withdrew for poor response, 2 needed added baricitinib; mild adverse events in 22.9%; relapse and follow-up not in abstract — [Ban et al. 2026, JDDG](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/41482702)
- Histology and inflammation: in 36 LV biopsies, lymphocytic infiltrate was present in all and neutrophil infiltration with epidermal damage in 15; authors argue T-cell inflammation is central — [JAAD International 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12808936/)

### Inferences
- Colchicine (with or without pentoxifylline) is the cheapest, most locally familiar anti-inflammatory option and is also a standard first-line drug for recurrent small vessel vasculitis (section 8), so it is the one agent that plausibly covers both competing diagnoses. Its LV evidence is still only one uncontrolled retrospective series from an era before rivaroxaban, and remission there was "achieved", not shown to persist off treatment.
- Conventional immunosuppressants (methotrexate, mycophenolate, azathioprine, ciclosporin) have essentially no positive LV evidence and mostly appear as failures.
- Steroids alone do not prevent relapse and may do worst; they are used for flares or where connective tissue disease coexists.
- Anti-TNF data (about 20 patients) show pain and ulcer improvement on treatment but no off-treatment remission data.

### Gaps
- No dapsone-specific or doxycycline/minocycline-specific LV outcome data could be located beyond passing mentions.
- No source gives relapse rates after stopping colchicine, hydroxychloroquine, anti-TNF or danazol.
- The primary source for "adalimumab in ten patients" was not identified.

## 4. Procedural and physical treatments (hyperbaric oxygen, PUVA, spinal cord stimulation, sympathectomy)

### Takeaway
Hyperbaric oxygen (HBOT) heals active ulcers fast (about 3 to 4 weeks) but most patients relapse afterwards (6 of 8 in the only long-term series), so it is a rescue for flares, not prevention. Systemic PUVA has 14 reported patients who stopped forming new lesions during treatment, with no remission-duration data; spinal cord stimulation is a single case for pain; nothing was found on sympathectomy.

### Cited Findings
- HBOT [Prospective uncontrolled n=12 enrolled, 8 completed, Taiwan, abstract] Sessions five times weekly; analgesic use fell after a mean 4.9 sessions; all 8 completers' ulcers healed at a mean 3.4 weeks (2 to 5); 6 of 8 later relapsed with ulceration and responded to further HBOT; no significant side effects — [Juan et al. 2006, Br J Dermatol](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/16433793). First report, 2 patients — [Yang et al. 2003](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/14511004)
- HBOT single cases: 63-year-old woman, 2.0 ATA for 120 minutes three times weekly, nearly healed after 13 sessions, no recurrence in 8 months; authors caution the improvement "may be spontaneous remission" given LV's waxing and waning course — [Lee et al. 2023, Ann Dermatol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10608380/). 26-year-old woman, 1.45 ATA, 12 sessions, healed, no recurrence over 6 months — [Herrera-Sánchez et al. 2022](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/35904919). Indian case where authors suggest HBOT "may also help in prolonging remission periods" (opinion, n=1) — [Indian J Dermatol 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4601462). HBOT plus tofacitinib case, 30 days follow-up — [Mani et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12854586/)
- HBOT in practice: used in 7/69 (10.1%) inpatients in a Guangzhou cohort — [Clin Cosmet Investig Dermatol 2025](https://www.dovepress.com/clinical-characteristics-and-disease-course-of-livedoid-vasculopathy-a-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-CCID). Reviews class it as an alternative for refractory cases limited by cost and compliance — [Seguí and Llamas-Velasco 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515/full)
- HBOT access in Singapore: Singapore General Hospital's Hyperbaric and Diving Medicine Centre lists hyperbaric oxygen therapy and problem wound management among its services (no price published) — [SGH](https://www.sgh.com.sg/our-specialties/hyperbaric-and-diving-medicine-centre). Private wellness-type chambers advertise roughly S$150 to S$320 per 60 to 90 minute session; these are commercial, usually lower-pressure, and not equivalent to hospital HBOT — [Clifford Clinic listing](https://cliffordclinic.com/?p=155096)
- PUVA [Retro n=8, Korea, abstract] Systemic PUVA with oral 8-methoxypsoralen two to three times weekly; all patients stopped forming new lesions (mean 3.6 weeks), had symptom relief (5.9 weeks) and complete healing (10 weeks); tolerable side effects — [Lee et al. 2001, Int J Dermatol](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/11328402). [Case series n=4, Thailand, abstract] all responded — [Tuchinda et al. 2005](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/15888134). [n=2] — [Choi et al. 1999, J Am Acad Dermatol](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/10025746)
- Spinal cord stimulation [Case n=1, search snippet] 68-year-old woman; ulcers healed and pain controlled after posterior column stimulator implantation — [Revelles-Peñas et al. 2024, Actas Dermosifiliogr](https://actasdermo.org/es-use-spinal-cord-stimulator-avance-S0001731024007075) ([record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/39260614))
- Compression and smoking cessation: compression considered useful when venous insufficiency coexists and arterial disease is excluded; smoking cessation "presumed" beneficial, unstudied — [Burg et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.1012178/full). A Zurich observational study (n=15) is titled "strong association with smoking, weak association with thrombophilia" — [NCT05878327](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05878327)

### Inferences
- HBOT's 75% relapse rate in the one long-term series, with good response to repeat courses, makes it an on-demand flare treatment that could shorten ulcer episodes; it has no evidence as a preventive.
- PUVA's "stopped forming new lesions" outcome was measured during a 2 to 3 times weekly treatment programme; long-term PUVA carries cumulative skin cancer risk, which limits maintenance use (general dermatology knowledge, not from an LV source).

### Gaps
- Time to relapse after HBOT in Juan 2006 and post-treatment remission length after PUVA are not reported in the abstracts; full texts were not accessible.
- No report of sympathectomy for LV was found. No narrowband UVB data found.
- No hospital HBOT price or subsidy information for Singapore was found.

## 5. Lipid and vascular drugs repurposed for LV (statins, PCSK9 inhibitors, lipoprotein(a)-targeting agents)

### Takeaway
Raised lipoprotein(a) is the most frequent laboratory association of LV (about 42% in two series), but no published evidence was found that statins, PCSK9 inhibitors or the new Lp(a)-lowering drugs prevent LV relapses. The only Lp(a)-linked treatment report is danazol in 4 patients.

### Cited Findings
- [Retro series] Lp(a) was the most common thrombophilic factor: 30 of 72 patients tested (41.7%) in a Brazilian cohort of 75; 5 of 12 (42%) in a German series — summarised in [Seguí and Llamas-Velasco 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515/full)
- [Case series n=4, abstract] Danazol 200 mg/day in LV with high Lp(a): complete ulcer healing, less pain, Lp(a) down a mean 70% — [Criado et al. 2015](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25824252)
- [Retro cohort n=69] "Lipid-lowering therapy" was given to 15/69 (21.7%) LV inpatients in Guangzhou, with no outcome analysis — [Clin Cosmet Investig Dermatol 2025](https://www.dovepress.com/clinical-characteristics-and-disease-course-of-livedoid-vasculopathy-a-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-CCID)
- [Review] The German-language review advises measuring Lp(a) and treating it as a cardiovascular risk factor warranting preventive care, without any LV-specific lipid therapy — [PMC9939248](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9939248/)
- Nicotinic acid appears in reviews only as "case reports" — [Burg et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.1012178/full)

### Inferences
- If Lp(a) has not yet been measured, it is a cheap one-off test that both characterises LV risk and matters for long-term cardiovascular prevention; whether lowering it helps LV is unknown.
- Statins do not lower Lp(a) and PCSK9 inhibitors lower it only modestly (general lipidology knowledge noted in search results, not LV data), so there is no mechanistic or clinical basis yet for using them against LV itself.

### Gaps
- No case report or series of statins, PCSK9 inhibitors, inclisiran, pelacarsen, olpasiran, lepodisiran or lipoprotein apheresis in LV was found.
- Homocysteine-directed vitamins (folate, B6, B12) are only relevant when homocysteine is raised, which is not the case here.

## 6. Ongoing or recently completed clinical trials (2023 to 2026): clinicaltrials.gov, EU and Chinese registries

### Takeaway
No registered interventional trial for LV that is recruiting or was completed in 2023 to 2026 was found on clinicaltrials.gov or the EU Clinical Trials Register; the only interventional registration located is the German rivaroxaban RILIVA trial (2012). The newest prospective evidence is from unregistered or unlocated Chinese single-arm studies (baricitinib n=10, sulodexide n=35).

### Cited Findings
- [Registry] A title search of clinicaltrials.gov for livedoid/livedo/livedovasculopathy returned only two studies, both observational and completed: NCT00975871 (genetic polymorphism and livedo vasculitis, Taiwan, 2007 to 2009, n=50) and NCT05878327 (thrombophilia screening, University of Zurich, 2013 to 2015, n=15) — [clinicaltrials.gov API query](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies?query.titles=livedoid+OR+livedo+OR+livedovasculopathy&pageSize=50&fields=NCTId,BriefTitle,OverallStatus,StartDate,CompletionDate,InterventionName,LocationCountry,Phase,StudyType,EnrollmentCount,LeadSponsorName); [NCT05878327](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05878327)
- [Registry] EU Clinical Trials Register search returned one trial: EudraCT 2012-000108-13, phase II multicentre rivaroxaban trial (Universitätsklinikum Münster), completed — [EU CTR search](https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/search?query=livedoid+OR+livedovasculopathy+OR+livedovaskulopathie); this is the RILIVA trial (also registered in the German DRKS as DRKS00004652); its protocol paper is at [PMC4275467](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4275467/)
- [Prospective single-arm, China] Baricitinib open-label study, 10 new LV patients, 3-month outcome — [JAAD International 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12808936/). Sulodexide prospective single-centre study, 35 patients, 12 weeks — [Ban et al. 2026, JDDG](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/41482702). No registry numbers were visible in the material read.
- [Adjacent trial, not LV] ARAMIS (NCT02939573), a randomised trial of colchicine vs dapsone vs azathioprine for isolated skin vasculitis, target n=90, is still listed as enrolling with no published results — [clinicaltrials.gov NCT02939573](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02939573); [protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7189702)
- Web searches in English and Chinese for a ChiCTR registration of baricitinib, tofacitinib or rivaroxaban in LV found none (third-party registry mirrors only showed other indications).

### Inferences
- A patient in Asia currently has no identifiable LV trial to join. The research activity is concentrated in a few Chinese centres (Peking University First Hospital, Xijing Hospital Xi'an, Tongji Wuhan, West China Chengdu) publishing off-label JAK inhibitor and sulodexide experience.
- Because the differential here includes skin-limited vasculitis, the ARAMIS result (when published) will be the first randomised evidence for colchicine/dapsone/azathioprine in that alternative diagnosis; its sites are North American.

### Gaps
- ChiCTR (chictr.org.cn), WHO ICTRP and the new EU CTIS portal could not be queried directly in this session (script-driven sites); absence of trials there is not confirmed. The legacy EU register only covers trials submitted before 2023.
- Whether the Chinese prospective studies had registry entries is unknown.

## 7. Is there any treatment with durable remission lasting years off all medicine, or anything approaching a cure?

### Takeaway
No treatment is reported to cure LV. Multi-year drug-free remissions do occur, but as individual outcomes: in the largest cohort about 14% had no new ulcer for more than 4 years and about 38% had none within 2 years, regardless of regimen, while single patients stayed clear for 7 years or more after a short IVIG course.

### Cited Findings
- [Retro cohort, 236 followed of 358, full text] Long-term remission (no new ulcer for more than 4 years) in 34 (14.4%); short-term remission (2 to under 4 years) in 55 (23.3%); recurrence within 2 years in 147 (62.3%). By regimen, long-term remission: anticoagulant alone 21/165 (12.7%), anticoagulant plus glucocorticoid 7/30 (23.3%), antiplatelet alone 3/8, glucocorticoid alone 1/19. Authors: anticoagulation "alone may be insufficient ... particularly in preventing recurrence"; "no reliable criteria exist for defining cure and recurrence". Summer exacerbation in 150/183 (82.4%); mean onset age 28 — [Ban et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13557247/); abstract at [DPCJ](https://dpcj.org/index.php/dpc/article/view/6930)
- [Retro n=7] One patient with no recurrence for over 7 years after 2 to 3 IVIG cycles; the other six relapsed at about 1 year — [Kim et al. 2015](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25845419). [Case n=1] 7 years of remission after two IVIG cycles — [Takahagi et al. 2021](https://www.frontierspartnerships.org/journals/journal-of-cutaneous-immunology-and-allergy/articles/10.1002/cia2.12202/full)
- [Retro n=11] Median remission 26.7 months after a first IVIG-treated episode, shortening to 7.5 months after later episodes — [Monshi et al. 2014](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25022849)
- [Retro, Korea, via SR] After stopping rivaroxaban, 10 of 13 patients relapsed; relapses were milder and responded to re-treatment — [Ramphall et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9420174/)
- [JAK inhibitors] Off-drug relapse-free intervals of 2 to 8 months in some patients, relapse at about 32 weeks and 1.5 years in others — [Zhao et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12803528/); [Wang X et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13714699/)
- [Singapore retro n=70] 49 of 70 "achieved remission" over a mean 50 months of follow-up, on colchicine/pentoxifylline/prednisolone-based regimens (off-treatment durability not stated in abstract) — [Gan et al. 2012](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/23052434)
- [Guidance on stopping] One algorithm describes stepping anticoagulation down to a half-therapeutic maintenance dose after healing and reducing further "up to an escape attempt" (a trial off treatment) — [Burg et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.1012178/full). The Tübingen group pauses IVIG after complete remission or 6 months of stability — [Kofler et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8474298/)

### Inferences
- LV behaves as a relapsing-remitting condition whose activity can go quiet for years in a minority; every treatment so far appears to control episodes, not remove the tendency. Reported long remissions after IVIG cannot be separated from natural fluctuation.
- Because flares cluster in summer in most Chinese patients and trial-off-treatment is part of published algorithms, a monitored stop or a seasonal/intermittent strategy is a recognised concept, though untested. In an equatorial climate without seasons, the seasonal trigger pattern may not translate (speculative).
- First remissions tend to be the longest (Monshi), which argues for discussing what to do at the first relapse in advance, not for expecting permanent remission.

### Gaps
- No study has compared continuous low-dose anticoagulation against stop-and-watch or seasonal treatment.
- No predictors of who achieves long drug-free remission have been identified; the 358-patient study did not analyse them.
- Loss to follow-up was high (122 of 358), so the 14% long-term remission figure is uncertain in either direction.

## 8. If the true diagnosis were recurrent cutaneous small vessel vasculitis (CSVV) or cutaneous polyarteritis nodosa (cPAN) rather than LV: standard long-term prevention

### Takeaway
For chronic or recurrent skin-limited small vessel vasculitis, the usual long-term options are colchicine or dapsone (alone or together), then azathioprine, with steroids only for flares; for cutaneous PAN, colchicine is the common first step and steroids plus azathioprine (or methotrexate) the main option for relapsing or neuropathic disease. Anticoagulants are not standard for either, so a sustained response to rivaroxaban alone would itself favour LV, while a relapse on rivaroxaban would strengthen the case for an anti-inflammatory approach.

### Cited Findings
- [Mini-review, expert opinion] CSVV that is severe, chronic (over 4 weeks) or recurrent warrants treatment; first-line steroid-sparing agents: colchicine 0.6 mg twice daily, dapsone 100 to 150 mg daily, azathioprine 2 mg/kg daily; systemic steroids sparingly for flares and "not an appropriate long-term option"; refractory disease may need mycophenolate, methotrexate, ciclosporin or rituximab; "considerable practice variation" given lack of high-quality data — [Front Med 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.1059612/full). Colchicine or dapsone "singly or in combination" as first line for chronic idiopathic cases — ["A practical approach to the diagnosis, evaluation, and management of cutaneous small-vessel vasculitis", 2014 review (search snippet)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24756249/)
- [Trial protocol] Skin-limited vasculitis becomes chronic or recurrent in up to one third of patients. The only prior RCT of colchicine (n=20, 1 month) showed no significant benefit over placebo. ARAMIS is comparing colchicine 0.6 mg twice daily, dapsone 150 mg/day and azathioprine 2 mg/kg/day for 6 months — [ARAMIS protocol, Trials 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7189702)
- [SR, 7 case series, n=325] Cutaneous PAN: systemic corticosteroids are the most common induction; steroid-combined or steroid-sparing agents include azathioprine, hydroxychloroquine, methotrexate, mycophenolate, dapsone, colchicine and others; ulceration was associated with higher relapse risk in some studies; evidence limited — [Papachristodoulou et al. 2021, Rheumatology](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/33944902)
- [French retrospective study, search snippet] In cPAN, complete response in 11/13 (84%) on glucocorticoids plus azathioprine vs 13/42 (31%) on colchicine; colchicine judged to have a good benefit-risk balance when there is no peripheral neuropathy; steroids plus azathioprine "seem the best treatment in the event of relapse" — [J Am Acad Dermatol 2021 summary](https://www.themednet.org/publications/efficacy-and-safety-of-treatments-in-cutaneous-polyarteritis-nodosa-a-french-observational-retrospective-study)
- [Overlap with LV histology] Neutrophil infiltration with epidermal damage was seen in 15 of 36 LV biopsies in one series, and "allergic vasculitis" and Henoch-Schönlein purpura were the commonest misdiagnoses among 358 LV patients — [JAAD International 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12808936/); [Ban et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13557247/)

### Inferences
- A mixed biopsy (fibrin thrombi plus neutrophils with nuclear dust) is described within the LV spectrum, particularly near ulcers, so it does not by itself overturn an LV diagnosis; but it means the anti-inflammatory options that serve both diagnoses (colchicine first, possibly dapsone; JAK inhibitors in refractory cases) are the ones most worth discussing if rivaroxaban is stopped or fails.
- Response pattern is diagnostic information: staying clear on rivaroxaban 10 mg alone points towards a thrombotic (LV) mechanism; new lesions despite it would shift weight towards vasculitis or an inflammatory LV phenotype.
- Dapsone needs G6PD testing before use, which is relevant in Southeast Asian populations (general pharmacology knowledge; not from an LV source).

### Gaps
- No randomised evidence yet for any long-term CSVV or cPAN regimen; ARAMIS results were not found as of November 2026.
- The French cPAN study was seen only via a summary page, not the original article.
- No source directly addresses how to treat cases where pathologist and clinician disagree between LV and small vessel vasculitis.
