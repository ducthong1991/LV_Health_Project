# Duration, tapering and long-term safety of low-dose rivaroxaban in livedoid vasculopathy (LV)

Notes compiled 21 Nov 2026. General information to discuss with a dermatologist, not a recommendation to change or stop medicine. Each finding is labelled **[LV]** (LV-specific evidence) or **[VTE-borrowed]** (evidence from venous thromboembolism, atrial fibrillation or other populations, which are older and sicker than a healthy man in his 30s). Items marked *(search summary)* come from a search-result or secondary summary and were not checked in the primary full text.

## 1. What do the German S1 guideline and other expert recommendations say about duration, step-down, stopping and restarting?

### Takeaway
The updated German S1 guideline (version dated 1 Oct 2025, valid to 30 Sep 2030) gives no fixed treatment duration, but its algorithm explicitly lists a trial of stopping ("Auslassversuch") as an option once disease is stable, both after stepping LMWH down and at rivaroxaban 10 mg/day. It says nothing about seasonal or every-other-day dosing, and stresses LV is a year-round disease.

### Cited Findings
- **[LV]** Version: "Stand: 01/10/2025, Gültig bis: 30/09/2030", AWMF register no. 013-098, coordinators Görge and Schiffmann. All therapies are off-label and described as "individuelle Heilversuche" (individual treatment attempts); no phase 3 trials are expected. — [AWMF S1 guideline, 2025 update](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- **[LV]** Algorithm (Figure 1), in order: first diagnosis or exacerbation: LMWH at full therapeutic dose; "Stabiler Befund - abgeheilte Ulzerationen: Reduktion auf NMH in halbtherapeutischer Dosis" (stable, healed ulcers: reduce to half-therapeutic LMWH); "Rezidiv: Steigerung auf NMH in volltherapeutischer Dosis" (relapse: back to full dose); "Keine Verschlechterung bei Reduktion: Auslassversuch" (no worsening on reduction: trial of stopping); "Hohe Gefahr der Verschlechterung: Fortführung der Therapie oder Umstellung auf Rivaroxaban 20 mg/Tag" (high risk of worsening: continue or switch to rivaroxaban 20 mg/day); "Stabiler Befund: Rivaroxaban 10 mg/Tag oder Auslassversuch" (stable: rivaroxaban 10 mg/day or trial of stopping). — [AWMF S1 guideline, 2025 update](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- **[LV]** Recommendation 7 (expert consensus scores, scale -2 to +2): "Eine Umstellung der Therapie mit NMH auf Rivaroxaban bis zu 15 mg 2x tägl. ist möglich (EK +1,8)"; "Anschließend kann eine Therapie mit Rivaroxaban 20 mg 1xtägl. erfolgen (EK +1,9)"; "Eine Reduktion auf 10 mg/Tag kann bei stabilem Befund erfolgen (EK +1,9)" (a reduction to 10 mg/day can be made when stable). — [AWMF S1 guideline, 2025 update](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- **[LV]** Text on study dosing: "In Studien erfolgte der Einsatz von Rivaroxaban 10 mg 1x täglich zur Erhaltungstherapie und 10 mg 2 x täglich bei Exazerbation oder Rezidiv" (10 mg once daily for maintenance, 10 mg twice daily for exacerbation or relapse). — [AWMF S1 guideline, 2025 update](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- **[LV]** Recommendation 6: worsening of wounds is usually preceded by a phase of increasing pain, so a dose increase "soll bereits bei deutlicher Schmerzzunahme ... erwogen werden" (EK +1.9); a pain diary is recommended (EK +1.9). Pain falls within 2 to 4 days of starting or escalating anticoagulation, "sodass ein Therapieerfolg schnell evaluiert werden kann". — [AWMF S1 guideline, 2025 update](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- **[LV]** Seasonality: "Die Symptomatik kann saisonal schwanken, bei einigen Patienten kommt es zu einer Verschlechterung in den Sommermonaten", but "die LV [ist] ein ganzjährig vorkommendes Krankheitsbild"; clusters in spring/summer or winter and year-round disease are all described. The summary adds that classic summer ulceration is only part of the picture and the disease is, "vielleicht im Rahmen des Klimawandels, ganzjährig vorkommend zu behandeln". — [AWMF S1 guideline, 2025 update](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- **[LV]** Other points: DOACs should not be used if antiphospholipid antibodies are present; other DOACs have only single case reports (apixaban 10 mg/day, edoxaban 15 to 60 mg/day, dabigatran 220 mg/day); compression class I (20 mmHg) can be considered if tolerated; large temperature swings should be avoided; stated goal is "die langfristige Besserung und Erhaltung der Lebensqualität". — [AWMF S1 guideline, 2025 update](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- **[LV]** The 2021 version carried the same two rivaroxaban switching statements (up to 15 mg twice daily, then 20 mg once daily) *(search summary; the 2021 PDF returned a server error)*; published in English as JDDG 2021;19(11):1667-1678. — [AWMF 2021 version](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie__2021-02.pdf)
- **[LV]** RILIVA protocol dosing: 10 mg morning and evening for 12 weeks; "After 7 pain-free days, the dose will be reduced to 1 tablet each morning"; "If pain returns, the dose will immediately be increased to the original dose". The protocol background calls LV a disorder "that recurs if treatment is stopped" and notes summer exacerbation with temporary winter remission. No off-drug follow-up was planned. — [RILIVA protocol, JMIR Res Protoc 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4275467/)
- **[LV]** Münster group follow-up (Hillgruber, Drerup, Goerge and colleagues, Adv Wound Care 2026): 26 patients treated per the S1 guideline, follow-up from at least 3 months to more than 24 months; abstract reports sustained control of pain and disease activity and concludes long-term guideline-based anticoagulation is effective. The abstract gives no numbers on dose reduction, stopping or relapse. — [PubMed 41264385](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41264385/)
- **[LV]** Systematic review (Gao and Jin 2021, as cited in a 2022 review): rivaroxaban "initial dose of 20 mg that can be tapered to 10 mg/day for maintenance"; reported remissions lasted 4 weeks to 23 months; doses of 10 to 20 mg/day effective in 82.2% of up to 73 patients. — [Frontiers in Medicine 2022 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9773082/)

### Inferences
- The guideline's own ladder ends at exactly where this patient is now (stable on 10 mg/day), and its next listed option is a supervised trial of stopping. A planned stop with a pain diary and a pre-agreed restart rule is therefore within guideline practice, not a departure from it.
- The guideline's logic is symptom-led (pain as the early warning, response within 2 to 4 days), which supports a "stop, watch, restart early" plan better than a fixed calendar duration.
- No guideline or consensus statement defines how long remission should last before a stop attempt.

### Gaps
- No guideline text on minimum duration, on doses below 10 mg/day, alternate-day dosing or seasonal treatment.
- The Hillgruber 2026 full text (which collected relapse data by questionnaire) was not accessible; it may contain the best real-world data on step-down under the guideline.
- No ISTH, ASH or CHEST guidance addresses LV.

## 2. What does the 2024 JAAD International long-term study (PMC10897505) actually show, and are there similar cohorts?

### Takeaway
Every-other-day dosing is confirmed in the source text but only as a one-line mention with no patient count or outcomes; no seasonal regimen and no predictors of remission were analysed. The paper's practical message is that stop-and-retreat works for most, with second courses much shorter than the first.

### Cited Findings
- **[LV]** Design: 34 biopsy-confirmed patients, Xiangya Hospital (China), June 2018 to June 2023; mean follow-up 32.79 ± 13.1 months; 79.4% female; median onset age 17 years (IQR 14 to 30.25); median disease duration 5 years (IQR 3 to 10.25). — [Deng et al., JAAD Int 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10897505/)
- **[LV]** Dosing, verbatim: "All patients received 10 mg of rivaroxaban daily initially ... with some adjusting their dosage to either 10 mg every other day or 15 mg every day based on disease severity." The number on each regimen and their outcomes are not reported in the text. — [Deng et al., JAAD Int 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10897505/)
- **[LV]** Stopping: 26 of 34 stopped after symptom relief (median first course 9.0 months, IQR 3.0 to 15.5); 3 stopped for insufficient improvement; 5 stayed on drug to end of follow-up (median 33.0 months, range 18.0 to 38.0). Stopping was not described as tapered. — [Deng et al., JAAD Int 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10897505/)
- **[LV]** After stopping: 10 had no recurrence over a median 17.5 months (IQR 7.5 to 34.75), two of them for more than 3 years; 16 relapsed at a median 7.0 months (IQR 5.0 to 12), with median relapse severity score 2.5 (IQR 1.0 to 3.75) versus a baseline median of 7.0. — [Deng et al., JAAD Int 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10897505/)
- **[LV]** Retreatment: 14 of the 16 resumed rivaroxaban and 12 stopped again after remission; median second course 3.0 months (IQR 2.0 to 5.0). The paper gives no formal retreatment remission rate, only that "most patients can still achieve remission after retreatment". — [Deng et al., JAAD Int 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10897505/)
- **[LV]** Heat: 3 patients had mild summer relapses, chose not to restart, and improved as temperatures fell; 4 had mild to moderate relapses while on treatment after heat exposure and remitted on continued rivaroxaban. Authors: "Continuous treatment does not guarantee the absence of relapses." — [Deng et al., JAAD Int 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10897505/)
- **[LV]** Predictors: none analysed statistically. Baseline labs only: low protein C 26.4%, raised lipoprotein(a) 12.9%, homocysteinaemia 23.3%. Bleeding: hematochezia in 2 (5.9%), heavy periods in 6 (17.6%); severity not graded. — [Deng et al., JAAD Int 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10897505/)
- **[LV]** Korean letter (Lee and Cho, JEADV 2020): per a secondary systematic review, 10 of 13 patients relapsed after stopping rivaroxaban but "severity was lower" *(secondary summary; the review's own tables are internally inconsistent and the letter has no abstract)*. — [Ramphall et al., Cureus 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9420174/); [PubMed 31793049](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31793049)
- **[LV]** Same review pooled 56 rivaroxaban patients (10 to 20 mg/day, treatment 2 to 14 months): 12 of 56 (21.4%) relapsed. — [Ramphall et al., Cureus 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9420174/)
- **[LV]** Other cohorts with no stop data: 55 patients, 91% (40/44) complete ulcer remission by 12 weeks, mild bleeding-type side effects in 25% (10 menorrhagia, 3 gum bleeding, 1 haemorrhage) (Zhao 2024); 41 patients on 10 mg/day, disease control in 78.0% within 3 weeks, non-responders had higher severity scores (Liu 2026). — [PubMed 38287670](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38287670/); [PubMed 41527208](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41527208/)
- **[LV]** Single case (Drerup and Goerge, JEADV 2017;31(12):e532): 49-year-old man, 4 weeks of heparin then rivaroxaban 10 mg/day long term with no relapse *(secondary summary)*. — [British Library record](https://eld.bl.uk/catalog/100055464996_0x000001); [Ramphall et al., Cureus 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9420174/)

### Inferences
- Of the 26 who stopped after remission, 16 (62%) relapsed and 10 (38%) had not relapsed at last follow-up; this matches the 62.3% two-year recurrence in the 358-patient Chinese cohort (section 4), so roughly 6 in 10 relapsing within about 2 years of stopping is the most consistent figure available.
- Relapses after a course were milder than the original disease (score 2.5 versus 7.0) and second courses were a third as long (3 versus 9 months), which is the best published support for an intermittent "treat to remission, stop, retreat early" pattern.
- The cohort was 79% female with onset in the teens; applicability to a man with onset in his 20s and more than 10 years of disease is uncertain.

### Gaps
- Number and outcomes of patients on 10 mg every other day: **unconfirmed** beyond the single sentence; Figure 1 (individual courses) could not be read.
- No cohort reports predictors of staying in remission (sex, disease duration, thrombophilia, lipoprotein(a), season of stopping).

## 3. Any reports of intermittent, seasonal or symptom-triggered anticoagulation in LV?

### Takeaway
No study has tested seasonal (hot months only) or pre-planned intermittent anticoagulation in LV with any agent. What exists is symptom-triggered dose escalation (RILIVA, S1 guideline), repeated stop-and-retreat courses (Deng 2024), and intermittent iloprost infusions.

### Cited Findings
- **[LV]** A targeted search found no trial or series of seasonal or intermittent anticoagulation; reviews describe only continuous treatment. — [Frontiers in Medicine 2022 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9773082/)
- **[LV]** Symptom-triggered step-up is protocolised: RILIVA returned patients to 10 mg twice daily "immediately" if pain recurred; the S1 guideline uses 10 mg twice daily for exacerbation or relapse and recommends acting on rising pain. — [RILIVA protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4275467/); [AWMF S1 guideline, 2025 update](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- **[LV]** RILIVA outcome context: 6 of 20 evaluable patients needed additional enoxaparin; 8 treatment-related adverse events in 6 of 25 (24%), mostly menorrhagia *(abstract-level summary)*. — [RILIVA abstract, University of Lübeck record](https://research.uni-luebeck.de/en/publications/anticoagulation-with-rivaroxaban-for-livedoid-vasculopathy-riliva/)
- **[LV]** De facto intermittent use: in Deng 2024, 12 patients completed two separate courses (median 9 then 3 months) with drug-free intervals; 3 rode out mild summer relapses without drug. — [Deng et al., JAAD Int 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10897505/)
- **[LV]** Seasonal burden in a large Asian cohort: summer exacerbation in 82.4% (150/183) of patients asked. — [Ban et al., Dermatol Pract Concept 2026](https://www.dpcj.org/index.php/dpc/article/download/6930/3938/114821)
- **[LV]** Non-anticoagulant intermittent precedent: iloprost infusions given once or twice a year for 5 days when pain returns are reported (S1 guideline rates iloprost only EK +0.1). — [AWMF S1 guideline, 2025 update](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)

### Inferences
- In Singapore there is no cool season, so a "hot months only" schedule has no natural off-period; the heat-related relapses on treatment in Deng 2024 suggest heat exposure (travel, exertion, hot environments) may matter more than the calendar.
- A symptom-triggered plan (restart at first pain or new purpuric spot, since response takes 2 to 4 days) is the only intermittent approach with any published backing.

### Gaps
- No data on intermittent or seasonal LMWH, apixaban, edoxaban or dabigatran in LV.
- No data comparing continuous versus on-demand treatment for scarring (atrophie blanche) or neuropathy outcomes.

## 4. What proportion of LV patients reach long-term drug-free remission, and after how many years of disease?

### Takeaway
The best estimate is that roughly 14% reach remission lasting more than 4 years and about 60% relapse within 2 years; no study relates remission to years of disease, so "burn-out" after a decade is neither supported nor excluded.

### Cited Findings
- **[LV]** 358 Chinese patients (2010 to 2022), follow-up available in 236: 34 (14.4%) long-term remission (no new ulcer for more than 4 years), 55 (23.3%) short-term remission (2 to under 4 years), 147 (62.3%) recurrence within 2 years. Anticoagulant-treated subgroup: 21, 39 and 105 of 165 respectively. Antiplatelet alone: 3, 2 and 3 of 8. No significant difference between anticoagulant monotherapy and other regimens. — [Ban et al., Dermatol Pract Concept 2026](https://www.dpcj.org/index.php/dpc/article/download/6930/3938/114821)
- **[LV]** The same authors conclude that with anticoagulation "it is difficult to reduce short-term recurrence or prevent long-term relapse" and that "there are no reliable criteria to define cure and recurrence in LV". Their definition is inconsistent (methods: no new ulcer for more than 4 years; discussion: no recurrence 4 years after stopping treatment); 34% were lost to follow-up. — [Ban et al., Dermatol Pract Concept 2026](https://www.dpcj.org/index.php/dpc/article/download/6930/3938/114821)
- **[LV]** Deng 2024: 10 of 26 who stopped rivaroxaban were relapse-free at a median 17.5 months; 2 beyond 3 years. — [Deng et al., JAAD Int 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10897505/)
- **[LV]** Thai cohort: 74 patients, mean age 37.6 ± 14.7, female to male 5.2:1, median follow-up 10.5 months; Kaplan-Meier estimates of disease improvement (not drug-free remission) 38.5%, 53.7% and 57.9% at 1, 2 and 3 years; literature review within it: anticoagulant monotherapy improved 97 of 104 (93.3%), bleeding in 9 (8.7%) *(search summary of abstract)*. — [Rujitharanawong et al., Dermatol Ther 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33219608/)
- **[LV]** Singapore (single centre, 1997 to 2007): 70 patients, mean age 39, female to male 3:1, mean follow-up 50 months; 49 achieved remission, 55% of them needing combination therapy (colchicine, pentoxifylline, prednisolone); monotherapy successes: colchicine 59%, prednisolone 17.5%, pentoxifylline 17.5%, aspirin 6%. Pre-DOAC era; relapse not reported. — [Gan et al., Ann Acad Med Singap 2012](https://www.themednet.org/publications/a-ten-year-retrospective-study-on-livedo-vasculopathy-in-asian-patients)

### Inferences
- With more than 10 years of relapsing disease already, this patient sits in the group for whom spontaneous permanent remission has not happened; the published 14% long-term remission figure is an average across all durations and is probably optimistic for him.
- Around 4 in 10 staying clear for 1.5 years or more after a course (Deng) means a stop attempt has a real chance of buying a long drug-free interval, and relapse appears to be retreatable.

### Gaps
- No study reports remission by disease duration, age at onset or sex.
- No prospective natural-history data; all figures come from retrospective series with heavy loss to follow-up.

## 5. Long-term safety of rivaroxaban 10 mg daily

### Takeaway
In VTE trials (not LV), 10 mg daily caused major bleeding in about 0.4% of patients over a median of about 1 year, statistically indistinguishable from aspirin 100 mg, in a population with a median age of about 60. Randomised data now extend to 5 years (RENOVE) but still in people in their 60s; there are no bleeding rates for healthy adults under 50, and risk is best understood as a roughly constant yearly rate that adds up with years of use.

### Cited Findings
- **[VTE-borrowed]** EINSTEIN CHOICE: 3,365 patients, median treatment 351 days; mean age 58.8 ± 14.7 and median 60 (IQR 48 to 69) in the 10 mg arm. Major bleeding 5/1127 (0.4%) on 10 mg, 3/1131 (0.3%) on aspirin, 6/1107 (0.5%) on 20 mg; HR 10 mg versus aspirin 1.64 (95% CI 0.39 to 6.84). Clinically relevant non-major bleeding 22 (2.0%), 20 (1.8%), 30 (2.7%). Major or clinically relevant non-major 27 (2.4%), 23 (2.0%), 36 (3.3%). — [Weitz et al., NEJM 2017](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10050925/1/nejmoa1700518.pdf)
- **[VTE-borrowed]** Sites of the five major bleeds on 10 mg: 1 intracranial (non-fatal), 1 intramuscular, 2 gastrointestinal, 1 abdominal; no fatal bleed on 10 mg (1 fatal on aspirin, 1 on 20 mg). Any bleeding: 13.4% on 10 mg versus 12.2% on aspirin. — [Weitz et al., NEJM 2017](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10050925/1/nejmoa1700518.pdf); [FDA Xarelto label 2023](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/022406s041lbl.pdf)
- **[VTE-borrowed]** Mean treatment duration was 293 days (10 mg) and 286 days (aspirin); bleeding led to permanent discontinuation in 1% in each group. — [FDA Xarelto label 2023](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/022406s041lbl.pdf)
- **[VTE-borrowed]** Pooled EINSTEIN EXTENSION and CHOICE: major bleeding 15/2832 (0.5%) on rivaroxaban (either dose), 3/1131 (0.3%) on aspirin, 0/590 on placebo. — [Prins et al., Blood Adv 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5894264/)
- **[VTE-borrowed]** RENOVE (Lancet 2025): 2,768 patients, reduced dose (rivaroxaban 10 mg daily or apixaban 2.5 mg twice daily) versus full dose, median follow-up 37 months. Five-year cumulative clinically relevant bleeding 9.9% reduced versus 15.2% full dose (HR 0.61, 95% CI 0.48 to 0.79); recurrent VTE 2.2% versus 1.8% *(secondary summaries, consistent across three sources)*. Median age in the rivaroxaban stratum 62.6 years. — [The Hospitalist summary](https://www.the-hospitalist.org/hospitalist/article/39034/in-the-literature/evidence-for-reduced-dose-doac-in-those-at-high-risk-for-vte-recurrence-in-extended-phase-anticoagulation-the-renove-trial/); [Deng et al. review, PMC12722643](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12722643/); [Girard et al., Eur Respir J 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42919641/)
- **[VTE-borrowed]** Meta-analysis of extended anticoagulation after unprovoked VTE (14 trials, 13 cohorts; 7,220 on DOACs, mostly full dose): major bleeding 1.12 per 100 person-years with DOACs (95% CI 0.72 to 1.62) and 1.74 with warfarin-type drugs; case fatality of a major bleed on DOACs 9.7% (95% CI 3.2 to 19.2). Bleeding was significantly higher in those over 65, with creatinine clearance under 50 mL/min, prior bleeding, concurrent antiplatelet therapy, or haemoglobin under 100 g/L. Data were insufficient to estimate DOAC bleeding beyond 1 year. — [Khan et al., Ann Intern Med 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34516270/)
- **[LV]** Bleeding on rivaroxaban in LV series is mostly menstrual: 6 menorrhagia and 2 hematochezia in 34 (Deng); 10 menorrhagia, 3 gum bleeding, 1 haemorrhage in 55 (Zhao); pooled literature 8.7% bleeding on anticoagulant monotherapy. No major bleed is described in these LV series. — [Deng et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10897505/); [Zhao et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38287670/); [Rujitharanawong et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33219608/)
- **[Other populations]** Liver: postmarketing reports of jaundice, cholestasis and hepatitis including hepatocellular injury are listed in the label. In a US atrial fibrillation claims cohort, liver-injury hospitalisation was 6.6 per 1,000 person-years on rivaroxaban versus 9.0 on warfarin; a French cohort found a higher risk of serious acute liver injury with rivaroxaban than warfarin-type drugs (adjusted HR 1.41, 95% CI 1.05 to 1.91); an Icelandic cohort estimated about 1 in 1,100 rivaroxaban users developed drug-induced liver injury *(search summaries; elderly populations)*. — [FDA Xarelto label 2023](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/022406s041lbl.pdf); [Alonso et al., Heart 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5429195); [French cohort, Sci Rep 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7363898); [Icelandic cohort record](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_journals_2425719057)
- **[Other populations]** Kidney: observational atrial fibrillation cohorts associate rivaroxaban with fewer adverse kidney outcomes than warfarin (for example kidney failure HR 0.389, 95% CI 0.300 to 0.499 in an Asian nationwide cohort) *(search summary)*. — [Renal outcomes cohort, PMC9996329](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9996329/)
- **[Other populations]** Bone: network meta-analysis of 31 studies (463,495 patients): rivaroxaban had a lower risk of any fracture than warfarin (RR 0.72, 95% CI 0.60 to 0.86). The S1 guideline notes osteoporosis risk with LMWH is low. — [Fracture network meta-analysis 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9168033/); [AWMF S1 guideline, 2025 update](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- **[Pharmacology]** Terminal half-life is 5 to 9 hours in healthy subjects aged 20 to 45 (11 to 13 hours in the elderly); routine coagulation monitoring is not recommended. — [FDA Xarelto label 2023](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/022406s041lbl.pdf)

### Inferences
- Converting EINSTEIN CHOICE using the label's mean exposure (my arithmetic, not a published figure): about 0.55 major bleeds and about 3.0 major or clinically relevant non-major bleeds per 100 patient-years on 10 mg, versus about 0.34 and 2.6 on aspirin.
- A quarter of EINSTEIN CHOICE participants were aged 48 or younger (lower quartile of age), but no bleeding rate was published for them. Because age over 65, kidney impairment, prior bleeding and antiplatelet co-therapy drive bleeding, a healthy man in his 30s plausibly sits below the trial average; this is extrapolation, not measured.
- Risk does not appear to grow with duration through a cumulative toxic mechanism; it behaves as a yearly hazard that sums (10 years at about 0.5% per year is roughly 5% cumulative chance of a major bleed at trial-average risk, likely less at his age, rising as he ages).
- No signal of cumulative kidney or bone harm; liver injury is rare and idiosyncratic, so occasional liver tests are a reasonable topic to raise.

### Gaps
- No published major-bleeding rate for rivaroxaban 10 mg in adults under 50, and no data beyond about 5 years at this dose.
- No long-term safety data specific to LV patients (longest reported continuous use about 38 months).
- The age-stratified EINSTEIN CHOICE analysis (Drugs and Aging 2025) was paywalled; its bands start at under 65.

## 6. Practical consequences of long-term use for a young active man

### Takeaway
Day-to-day impact is modest: no food or monitoring requirement at 10 mg, short interruptions for procedures, and care with NSAIDs and collision sport. The main change since 2025 is that the specific reversal agent andexanet alfa has been withdrawn from the US market, leaving prothrombin complex concentrate as the usual emergency option.

### Cited Findings
- **Missed dose:** for 10 mg once daily, take the missed dose immediately on the same day; do not double up. — [FDA Xarelto label 2023](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/022406s041lbl.pdf); [SingHealth rivaroxaban page](https://www.singhealth.com.sg/medicine/rivaroxaban)
- **Food:** 2.5 mg and 10 mg tablets can be taken with or without food (15 and 20 mg need food). — [SingHealth rivaroxaban page](https://www.singhealth.com.sg/medicine/rivaroxaban)
- **Surgery:** stop at least 24 hours before a procedure and restart once haemostasis is secure; the onset of effect on restarting is short. Epidural or spinal procedures carry a boxed warning for spinal haematoma. — [FDA Xarelto label 2023](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/022406s041lbl.pdf)
- **Dental:** Scottish SDCEP guidance: low-bleeding-risk procedures (for example simple extraction of 1 to 3 teeth) are done without interrupting the DOAC; for higher-risk procedures rivaroxaban's morning dose is delayed, and an evening dose needs no change. Recommendations are conditional, very low certainty. — [SDCEP guidance, 2nd edition](https://www.sdcep.org.uk/media/ypnl2cpz/sdcep-management-of-dental-patients-taking-anticoagulants-or-antiplatelet-drugs-2nd-edition.pdf); [SDCEP DOAC recommendations](https://companion.sdcep.org.uk/management-of-dental-patients-taking-anticoagulants-or-antiplatelet-drugs/treatment-recommendations/direct-oral-anticoagulants-doacs/recommendations/)
- **NSAIDs and aspirin:** chronic NSAID use, aspirin and other antiplatelets increase bleeding risk; SSRIs and SNRIs are also listed. Interacting drugs named locally: rifampicin, itraconazole, carbamazepine, phenytoin, St John's Wort. The S1 guideline allows NSAIDs only as supportive pain relief with no disease benefit. — [FDA Xarelto label 2023](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/022406s041lbl.pdf); [SingHealth rivaroxaban page](https://www.singhealth.com.sg/medicine/rivaroxaban); [AWMF S1 guideline, 2025 update](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- **Sport:** Singapore patient advice is to "avoid sports or activities that may cause injury" and see a doctor after any fall or injury. Specialist opinion: non-contact sport is safe on anticoagulation; contact and collision sport is traditionally discouraged. — [SingHealth rivaroxaban page](https://www.singhealth.com.sg/medicine/rivaroxaban); [ACC, Antithrombotic Therapy in Athletes 2023](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2023/08/31/11/58/antithrombotic-therapy-in-athletes)
- **Timed dosing for sport (expert opinion only):** after rivaroxaban 10 mg in healthy young volunteers, levels fall below 50 ng/mL at about 10 hours and below 30 ng/mL at about 13 hours; the authors propose under 30 ng/mL as a possibly safe level for contact sport and restarting 1 to 2 hours after an uneventful event, but call the threshold "empiric" and the approach "not suitable to be widely used". — [Moll et al., Hematology ASH Educ Program 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6246004/)
- **Reversal:** the label says an agent to reverse rivaroxaban is available and that prothrombin complex concentrate may be considered though not evaluated in outcome studies; rivaroxaban is not dialysable. AstraZeneca ended US sales of Andexxa (andexanet alfa) on 22 Dec 2025 after ANNEXA-I showed thrombosis in 14.6% versus 6.9% with usual care; four-factor prothrombin complex concentrate is described as standard of care for life-threatening bleeding. Ondexxya remains licensed for hospital use in the EU and UK. — [FDA Xarelto label 2023](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/022406s041lbl.pdf); [FDA Andexxa safety update](https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/safety-availability-biologics/update-safety-andexxa); [AABB news, Dec 2025](https://www.aabb.org/news-resources/news/article/2025/12/23/astrazeneca-withdraws-factor-xa-reversal-agent-from-u.s.-market); [Ondexxya UK SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/10933/smpc)

### Inferences
- With a 5 to 9 hour half-life at his age, a 10 mg dose is largely cleared within about a day, so taking the tablet in the evening after training places the lowest drug level during the next day's sport. This follows from the pharmacokinetics above but is not a tested strategy.
- For travel, a once-daily drug with a same-day catch-up rule tolerates time-zone shifts; no source addresses this directly.

### Gaps
- Andexanet alfa registration or stocking in Singapore could not be confirmed; no HSA listing was found.
- No authoritative statement on alcohol and rivaroxaban was found in the label or the SingHealth page; neither lists alcohol as an interaction.
- No LV-specific data on sport, trauma or surgery while anticoagulated.

## 7. Rebound risk after stopping

### Takeaway
There is no good evidence of pharmacological rebound thrombosis after stopping rivaroxaban; what happens is loss of protection within a day or two. In LV, relapse after stopping is usually delayed by months (median 7), though a few cases flared within a week.

### Cited Findings
- **[VTE-borrowed]** Boxed warning: premature discontinuation of any oral anticoagulant increases thrombotic risk; this reflects loss of protection in patients with an ongoing indication (largely atrial fibrillation). — [FDA Xarelto label 2023](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/022406s041lbl.pdf)
- **[VTE-borrowed]** EINSTEIN CHOICE, 30 days after the end of treatment: symptomatic recurrent VTE in 2 (0.2%) after 20 mg, 4 (0.4%) after 10 mg and 6 (0.6%) after aspirin, that is no excess after stopping rivaroxaban. — [Weitz et al., NEJM 2017](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10050925/1/nejmoa1700518.pdf)
- **[Mixed populations]** Systematic review of DOAC cessation (29 eligible reports, mostly 16 case reports plus 14 original studies): stopping "may be associated with an increased risk of thrombotic events" but a true rebound is unproven and its mechanism unclear. — [Frydrych et al., J Clin Med 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11546199/)
- **[Manufacturer]** "No evidence of rebound phenomenon was observed", though it "cannot be entirely excluded". — [J&J Medical Connect](https://www.jnjmedicalconnect.com/products/xarelto/medical-content/events-after-treatment-discontinuationrebound-of-xarelto)
- **[LV]** Time to relapse after stopping: median 7.0 months (IQR 5.0 to 12) in 16 patients, with relapses milder than baseline. — [Deng et al., JAAD Int 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10897505/)
- **[LV]** Two Mayo Clinic cases (Winchester 2015): rivaroxaban stopped for drowsiness, symptoms returned within a week and improved on resuming 10 mg/day *(secondary summary)*. — [Ramphall et al., Cureus 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9420174/)

### Inferences
- An interquartile range of 5 to 12 months to relapse argues against an immediate rebound flare in most LV patients; early flares probably mean the disease was still active under treatment rather than rebound.
- Because drug effect ends within a day or two, tapering (for example to alternate days) has no pharmacological rationale for preventing rebound; its only possible value is as a lower-exposure maintenance step, which is untested.

### Gaps
- No LV study measured coagulation markers or lesion counts in the first weeks after stopping.
- No comparison of abrupt stop versus taper in LV or VTE.

## 8. Rivaroxaban versus low-dose aspirin for bleeding, and is aspirin a credible step-down?

### Takeaway
In the only head-to-head trial (VTE patients), rivaroxaban 10 mg and aspirin 100 mg had similar bleeding, so switching to aspirin does not clearly buy safety. Aspirin's evidence in LV is thin: it is absent from the German guideline's recommendations and performed poorly as monotherapy in the Singapore series, though one small 2026 Indian series reports healing on aspirin 150 mg alone.

### Cited Findings
- **[VTE-borrowed]** Efficacy in the same trial: recurrent VTE in 13/1127 (1.2%) on 10 mg versus 50/1131 (4.4%) on aspirin (HR 0.26, 95% CI 0.14 to 0.47). — [Weitz et al., NEJM 2017](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10050925/1/nejmoa1700518.pdf)
- **[VTE-borrowed]** Head-to-head (EINSTEIN CHOICE): major bleeding 0.4% versus 0.3%; clinically relevant non-major 2.0% versus 1.8%; any bleeding 13.4% versus 12.2% (10 mg versus aspirin). Authors: rivaroxaban "was associated with a similar risk of bleeding". — [Weitz et al., NEJM 2017](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10050925/1/nejmoa1700518.pdf)
- **[Primary prevention populations]** Aspirin alone raises major gastrointestinal and extracranial bleeding from 0.07% to 0.10% per year (individual data, about 95,000 low-risk people); a 2019 meta-analysis of 13 trials found major bleeding 23.1 versus 16.4 per 10,000 person-years (HR 1.43). — [Antithrombotic Trialists, Lancet 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19482214/); [Zheng and Roddick, JAMA 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6439678)
- **[VTE-borrowed]** ASH 2020 VTE guideline: if secondary prevention is continued, an anticoagulant is recommended over aspirin, and a standard or lower DOAC dose is suggested; ASH judged aspirin's bleeding risk similar to anticoagulants. — [ASH 2020 guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7556153); [AAFP summary](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2021/1000/p429)
- **[LV]** The 2025 S1 guideline's therapy table lists LMWH, DOACs and IVIG as first line; antiplatelet drugs (aspirin, clopidogrel, dipyridamole) do not appear anywhere in its therapy table or numbered recommendations (text search of the PDF), and NSAIDs are described as having no described causal benefit, usable only for supportive pain relief. — [AWMF S1 guideline, 2025 update](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- **[LV]** India, 26 biopsy-proven patients (20 male): at 3 months 17 had complete healing and pain score 0 (11 on rivaroxaban 10 mg, 6 on aspirin 150 mg); at 6 months 25 of 26 (11 rivaroxaban, 8 rivaroxaban plus aspirin, 6 aspirin); no adverse effects; relapse not reported. — [Gupta et al., IJDVL 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41655089/)
- **[LV]** Singapore 1997 to 2007: aspirin accounted for 6% of monotherapy remissions (colchicine 59%). China: antiplatelet alone in 8 patients, 3 long-term remission, 3 recurrence. — [Gan et al. 2012](https://www.themednet.org/publications/a-ten-year-retrospective-study-on-livedo-vasculopathy-in-asian-patients); [Ban et al. 2026](https://www.dpcj.org/index.php/dpc/article/download/6930/3938/114821)
- **[LV]** Reviews: aspirin (75 to 325 mg), dipyridamole and pentoxifylline were historically common first therapies, but LV-specific aspirin evidence is at case-report level; anticoagulants showed favourable response in up to 98% (Micieli and Alavi 2018, as cited). — [Frontiers in Medicine 2022 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9773082/)
- **Combination caution:** aspirin added to rivaroxaban was an independent risk factor for major bleeding in ROCKET AF. — [FDA Xarelto label 2023](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/022406s041lbl.pdf)

### Inferences
- Aspirin as a step-down is a low-evidence option: it cannot be assumed safer than rivaroxaban 10 mg, and it is probably less effective given LV's fibrin-rich microthrombi and the VTE trial result (recurrence 4.4% on aspirin versus 1.2% on 10 mg). It could still be discussed as a trial in someone who strongly prefers to avoid an anticoagulant, with the same pain-diary restart rule.
- Aspirin should not be layered on top of rivaroxaban for maintenance without a clear reason.

### Gaps
- No trial compares aspirin with rivaroxaban, or with no treatment, for preventing LV relapse.
- No aspirin bleeding data specific to men in their 30s were found.

## 9. Correcting "the stroke drug": what rivaroxaban is, and Singapore generics and cost

### Takeaway
Rivaroxaban is a factor Xa inhibitor with ten US-labelled uses, most of them about vein clots; the 10 mg tablet is the low, preventive dose and is not the stroke-prevention dose (15 or 20 mg). Multiple generics are registered in Singapore, but no retail price for the 10 mg strength could be found.

### Cited Findings
- US-labelled indications: stroke prevention in non-valvular atrial fibrillation; treatment of DVT; treatment of PE; reduction of recurrent DVT or PE; DVT prophylaxis after hip or knee replacement; VTE prophylaxis in acutely ill medical patients; reduction of cardiovascular events in coronary artery disease and in peripheral artery disease (2.5 mg twice daily with aspirin); and two paediatric indications. — [FDA Xarelto label 2023](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/022406s041lbl.pdf)
- Dose by use: atrial fibrillation 15 or 20 mg daily; DVT or PE treatment 15 mg twice daily for 21 days then 20 mg daily; 10 mg daily is the dose for long-term prevention of recurrent clots after at least 6 months of treatment, after joint replacement, and in medical inpatients. — [FDA Xarelto label 2023](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/022406s041lbl.pdf)
- Singapore: a generic 10 mg product (Rivaroxaban-Teva, SIN17450P, prescription only) is registered for exactly two uses: prevention of VTE after hip or knee replacement, and treatment and prevention of recurrent DVT and PE. Stroke prevention is not among the listed indications for this strength. — [National Drug Formulary, SIN17450P](https://www.ndf.gov.sg/about-drugs/product-information/SIN17450P/)
- Singapore registers 14 rivaroxaban tablet brands; those with a 10 mg strength besides Xarelto include Estaelto, Invarox, Ixobran, Jbrivo, Oranxa, Ralteg, Rivaroxaban-Teva, Rivoban, Xabarem, Xafariv, Xarid and Zyroxaban. — [National Drug Formulary, rivaroxaban](https://www.ndf.gov.sg/about-drugs/active-ingredient/m01108/)
- Subsidy: the formulary's Standard Drug List entry reads "Rivaroxaban Tablet 15 mg, 20 mg"; the 10 mg strength is not listed there. An Agency for Care Effectiveness fact sheet (2 June 2026) lists rivaroxaban, apixaban and warfarin as subsidised for blood clots and states patients on these newer agents "have a lower risk of bleeding compared with those taking warfarin". — [National Drug Formulary, rivaroxaban](https://www.ndf.gov.sg/about-drugs/active-ingredient/m01108/); [ACE fact sheet](https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/68/7c8a7dd8-3b8f-4672-bd75-3384f45c4851/oral-treatments-for-blood-clot.pdf)
- Earlier national guidance (withdrawn 6 Feb 2026) covered 15 and 20 mg for subsidy and noted in Nov 2021 that the proposed price of 10 mg was higher per mg than 20 mg *(search summary)*. — [ACE NOAC guidance for VTE, withdrawn](https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/68/784ab413-b833-4838-9c0b-b37a23e614c0/NOACs%20for%20the%20treatment%20and%20secondary%20prevention%20of%20VTE%20(Withdrawn%206%20Feb%202026).pdf)
- LV is itself a clotting disorder of small skin vessels: the guideline calls it a thrombotic occlusion of dermal vessels and describes consensus that "die LV eine Gerinnungserkrankung ist". — [AWMF S1 guideline, 2025 update](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)

### Inferences
- An accurate plain description: "a low-dose clot-prevention tablet, the same dose given for a few weeks after knee replacement, used here to stop tiny clots in skin vessels". Stroke prevention is one use at double the dose in a different, much older population.
- Because LV use is off-label and the listed subsidy strengths are 15 and 20 mg, the 10 mg tablet is likely paid at the unsubsidised price; with a dozen generics registered, asking the pharmacy for the cheapest registered generic is reasonable.

### Gaps
- No Singapore retail or hospital price for generic rivaroxaban 10 mg was found in any public source; only a Malaysian online price for branded Xarelto 10 mg surfaced, which is not transferable.
- Registration dates and current marketing status of each generic were not checked on HSA InfoSearch.
