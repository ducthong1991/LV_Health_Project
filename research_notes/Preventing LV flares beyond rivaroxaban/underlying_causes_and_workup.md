# Underlying, correctable causes of livedoid vasculopathy (LV) and whether treating them prevents recurrence (status: 21 November 2026)

General information to discuss with a dermatologist or haematologist. It is not a recommendation. Evidence in LV is almost entirely retrospective cohorts, case series and case reports; there are no randomised trials of "find and treat the cause". Where a figure comes from a search-result abstract or snippet and not from a full text I read, this is stated.

Key to how sources were read:
- Full text read: German S1 guideline (2025 version), Ban 2026 (358 Chinese patients), Köykkä 2026 (Finland), Deng 2023, Gao and Jin 2021, Burg 2022, Eswaran 2022, Gardette 2018, Argobi 2025, Gao Y 2025, Tsuda 2020, Moser and Smock 2021.
- Abstract only (via Europe PMC or publisher page): Hairston 2006, Criado 2021, Gan 2012, Weishaupt 2019, Di Giacomo 2010, Criado 2015, Zhao 2024, Yuan 2024, Apti Sengun 2023, Rujitharanawong 2021.
- Search snippet only: EULAR 2019, ISTH 2020, 2023 ACR/EULAR criteria, Mimouni 2003, VITRO, HOPE-2, Wang 2018, DADA2 sources, case reports on paraprotein and cryoglobulin, PCSK9 meta-analysis.

## 1. What proportion of LV is primary/idiopathic versus associated with thrombophilia, autoimmune disease, paraproteinaemia or cancer?

### Takeaway
In most cohorts a laboratory "procoagulant" abnormality is found in roughly 40 to 60% of tested patients (range 41 to 77%), and the 2025 German S1 guideline summarises this as "under 50% in larger collectives"; autoimmune disease is found in a minority (about 4 to 16%) and paraprotein or cancer only in isolated cases. Denominators are small and inconsistent because most patients were tested for only some markers, so "idiopathic" often means "incompletely tested".

### Cited Findings
- German S1 guideline (AWMF 013-098, status 01/10/2025, valid to 30/09/2030, coordinators Görge and Schiffmann): in larger collectives procoagulant parameters were detected in under 50% of cases; having one of these abnormalities is not regularly associated with developing LV; the diagnosis of LV is possible without any procoagulant finding (expert consensus +1.9). The guideline uses "secondary LV" for LV with a detected procoagulant parameter and "primary LV" when none is found. — [AWMF S1 guideline PDF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- Same guideline: no statistically increased association with malignancy is observed; "secondary LV-like lesions" can occur with autoimmune disease or malignancy and then need causal therapy and further workup; no specific comorbidity has been shown to be more frequent in LV than in the general population (raised BMI and hypertension are described cofactors). — [AWMF S1 guideline PDF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- Proportion with any procoagulant factor, as tabulated in a systematic review: Hairston (Mayo) 41% (12/29), Di Giacomo (Brazil, prospective) 52% (18/34), Weishaupt (Germany) 44% (11/25), Lee (Korea) 42.5% (17/40), Gardette (France) 77% (20/26), Gao 73% (8/11), Criado (Brazil) 66% (48/72 fully examined). — [Burg et al., Front Med 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9574051)
- Mayo Clinic, Hairston 2006: 45 biopsy-proven patients 1990 to 2000, 32 (71.1%) female, mean age 45. Factor V Leiden heterozygous 2/9 (22.2%), decreased protein C or S 2/15 (13.3%), prothrombin G20210A 1/12 (8.3%), lupus anticoagulant 5/28 (17.9%), anticardiolipin 8/28 (28.6%), raised homocysteine 3/21 (14.3%), reduced transcutaneous oxygen 20/27 (74.1%), intraluminal thrombosis in 44/45 biopsies (97.8%). — [Hairston et al., Arch Dermatol 2006 (abstract)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17116831/)
- Brazil, Criado 2021: 75 biopsy-confirmed patients 2005 to 2019, 78.66% Caucasian women, mean age 39.9; about 66% had thrombophilia factors; about 40% relapsed in spring and summer. — [Criado et al., Dermatol Ther 2021 (abstract)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33496999/). Lp(a) was the most frequent factor, 30/72 (41.66%); other counts from a secondary table: factor V Leiden 3, prothrombin mutation 2, protein C 2, homocysteine 5, lupus anticoagulant 7, anticardiolipin IgM 10 and IgG 10. — [Eswaran et al., Front Med 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515)
- Brazil, Di Giacomo 2010 (prospective): 18/34 (52%) had a procoagulant abnormality; antiphospholipid antibodies 6/34 (17.64%), factor V Leiden heterozygous 6/34 (17.64%), protein C and/or S deficiency 3/34 (8.82%). — [Di Giacomo et al., JEADV 2010 (abstract)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20337814/); breakdown from [Eswaran et al., Front Med 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515)
- Germany, Weishaupt 2019 (27 patients from the rivaroxaban phase IIa trial): raised Lp(a) 5/12, raised homocysteine 10/12, raised BMI 11/27, hypertension 19/27, female to male 2.1:1, median age 53. — [Weishaupt et al., JEADV 2019 (abstract)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31009111/)
- France, Gardette 2018: 26 patients, 20/26 (77%) with at least one thrombophilia factor (one factor in 14, two in 4, three in 1, five in 1); hyperhomocysteinaemia in 50% (none above 50 µmol/L); one patient with lupus; 42% smokers. — [Gardette et al., Acta Derm Venereol 2018](https://www.medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-2965)
- China, Ban 2026 (largest cohort, 358 patients, Xijing Hospital, 2010 to 2022; tests done only in subsets): PAI-1 4G/5G promoter polymorphism 32/38 (84.2%), anti-endothelial cell antibodies 13/27 (48.1%), lupus anticoagulant 6/14 (42.9%), protein S or C deficiency 28/74 (37.8%), raised homocysteine 30/80 (37.5%), anticardiolipin 14/81 (17.3%), vitamin B12 deficiency 16/90 (17.8%), rheumatoid factor 2/114 (1.8%), ANCA 0/113, cryoglobulins 0/71. Median age at onset 23 years; 41.6% onset before age 20; male to female 1:2.4. — [Ban et al., Dermatol Pract Concept 2026](https://dpcj.org/index.php/dpc/article/view/6930) ([PDF](https://www.dpcj.org/index.php/dpc/article/download/6930/3938/114821))
- China, Deng 2023 (34 patients on rivaroxaban, Xiangya Hospital): raised Lp(a) 4 (12.9%), hyperhomocysteinaemia 7 (23.3%), positive ANA 6 (20.7%), anti-dsDNA 0, decreased protein C 9 (26.4%), decreased antithrombin 2 (5.9%). — [Deng et al., JAAD Int 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10897505/)
- China, Feng 2014: 24 patients; 14/24 (58.3%) had positive antiphospholipid antibodies; peak onset age 14 to 20; 87.5% summer exacerbation. — [Feng et al., Acta Derm Venereol 2014](https://medicaljournals.se/acta/content/abstract/10.2340/00015555-1711)
- China, Gao Y 2025 (69 patients, Sun Yat-sen University): ANA raised in 5.3%, rheumatoid factor 2.1%, antithrombin abnormal 7.7%, factor VIII abnormal 25.9%, fibrinogen raised 38.1%, complement C3 abnormal 42.1%; Lp(a), homocysteine, antiphospholipid antibodies and protein C/S were not reported. — [Gao et al., Clin Cosmet Investig Dermatol 2025](https://www.dovepress.com/clinical-characteristics-and-disease-course-of-livedoid-vasculopathy-a-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-CCID)
- Singapore, Gan 2012 (National Skin Centre, 70 patients 1997 to 2007, mean age 39, female to male 3:1, no racial predilection): among those screened, hepatitis B 7%, hepatitis C 4%, positive ANA 14%, anti-myeloperoxidase antibody 5%, anticardiolipin 7%, lupus anticoagulant 2%; peripheral neuropathy 9%. The authors called for a standardised prothrombotic screening protocol. — [Gan et al., Ann Acad Med Singap 2012 (abstract)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23052434/)
- Thailand, Rujitharanawong 2021 (Siriraj Hospital, 74 patients, mean age 37.6, female to male 5.2:1): "most had primary LV"; laboratory breakdown not available in the abstract. — [Rujitharanawong et al., Dermatol Ther 2021 (abstract page)](https://murex.mahidol.ac.th/en/publications/livedoid-vasculopathy-clinical-course-and-long-term-outcome-in-as/)
- Finland, Köykkä 2026 (24 patients, 2014 to 2019): 12/21 screened (57%) had a prothrombotic or inflammatory marker: activated protein C resistance 5, raised factor VIII 3, factor V Leiden 2, lupus anticoagulant 2, prothrombin mutation 1, anticardiolipin 1, ANA 1. Screened but not detected: hyperhomocysteinaemia, protein C, protein S and antithrombin deficiency, cryoglobulinaemia, cold agglutinin. Rheumatoid arthritis 3/24 (12.5%); solid carcinoma in 2. — [Köykkä et al., J Wound Manag 2026](https://journals.cambridgemedia.com.au/application/files/2517/8295/4560/koykka.pdf)
- USA, Argobi 2025 (Mass General Brigham records, 108 LV patients, 84.3% White, 6.5% Asian): 17/108 (15.74%) had an autoimmune or connective tissue disease: lupus 10, Sjögren 3, dermatomyositis 2, systemic sclerosis 2. Reported positivity: "phospholipid antibodies" 62 (57.4%), anticardiolipin 47 (43.5%), hyperhomocysteinaemia 56 (51.85%), activated protein C resistance 13 (12.03%). Quality caveat: single author, no control group, internal inconsistencies in denominators and software version. — [Argobi, Clin Cosmet Investig Dermatol 2025](https://www.dovepress.com/prevalence-of-livedoid-vasculopathy-among-patients-with-connective-tis-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-CCID)
- Turkey, Apti Sengun 2023 (16 LV patients, case-control): antithrombin deficiency 31.3%, protein S deficiency 12.5%, hyperhomocysteinaemia 31.3%, factor V Leiden heterozygous 6.3%, anti-beta-2 glycoprotein I 12.5%, lupus anticoagulant 6.3%, anticardiolipin 6.3%, MTHFR C677T heterozygous 43.8% and homozygous 18.8%. — [Apti Sengun et al., Microvasc Res 2023 (abstract)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37543163/)
- One review states that about 20% of cases are idiopathic (citing an older source). — [Eswaran et al., Front Med 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515); this conflicts with the under-50% detection figure in the [AWMF S1 guideline](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf) and with the 50% "no identifiable hypercoagulable association" figure quoted in [Köykkä et al. 2026](https://journals.cambridgemedia.com.au/application/files/2517/8295/4560/koykka.pdf).

### Inferences
- The realistic range for "nothing found" after a reasonably complete screen is 25 to 60%. The lower "idiopathic" estimates come from series that count very common, weakly prothrombotic variants (PAI-1 4G/5G, MTHFR) as positive findings.
- Asian cohorts differ from European and Brazilian ones: much younger onset (median 17 to 23 years in Chinese series versus 40 to 53 in German and Finnish series), stronger summer worsening, and a different thrombophilia mix (protein C/S abnormalities and antiphospholipid antibodies reported more often, factor V Leiden and prothrombin mutation rarely).
- The German guideline's definition of "secondary LV" (any procoagulant marker) differs from older usage (LV secondary to lupus, antiphospholipid syndrome, paraprotein and so on). A report writer should say which definition is being used.
- The Singapore series is the closest population match to this case. Its screening predates routine Lp(a), anti-beta-2 glycoprotein I and genetic testing, so it under-ascertains thrombophilia.

### Gaps
- No Vietnamese LV cohort was found. No cohort reports results by Southeast Asian ethnicity.
- Full-text laboratory breakdown of the Korean (Lee and Cho 2020, 40 patients) and Thai series could not be retrieved; only the 42.5% (17/40) overall figure for Korea is available second-hand.
- No cohort reports the yield of a fully standardised panel applied to every patient.

## 2. Lipoprotein(a): how often raised, does lowering it change LV, and drug approval status in late 2026

### Takeaway
Raised Lp(a) is one of the most frequent findings where it is measured (about 42% in a Brazilian and a German series, about 13% in a Chinese series), but the only evidence that lowering it helps LV is a four-patient danazol series in which the drug has other fibrinolytic effects. As of 21 November 2026 no Lp(a)-specific drug is approved anywhere I could confirm, and the first outcomes trial (pelacarsen, Lp(a)HORIZON) failed to reduce cardiovascular events despite a 78% fall in Lp(a); none of these drugs has been studied in LV.

### Cited Findings
- Prevalence: raised Lp(a) in 30/72 (41.66%) Brazilian patients, the most common thrombophilic factor in that cohort. — [Eswaran et al., Front Med 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515); 5/12 (42%) in the German multicentre analysis. — [Weishaupt et al. 2019 (abstract)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31009111/); 4 patients (12.9%) in a Chinese rivaroxaban cohort of 34. — [Deng et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10897505/)
- "Lp(a) was only screened in a few studies." — [Burg et al., Front Med 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9574051)
- Guideline position: Lp(a) has structural similarity to plasminogen and therefore antifibrinolytic properties; raised Lp(a) is listed as a "frequent" prothrombotic parameter in LV (with antithrombin deficiency and hyperhomocysteinaemia) and is in the recommended initial laboratory panel (expert consensus +1.1). It is also a cardiovascular risk factor. The guideline gives no Lp(a)-lowering treatment recommendation. — [AWMF S1 guideline PDF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- Danazol: four LV patients (mean age 45, mean disease duration 19 years) given danazol 200 mg daily had complete ulcer healing, less pain and a 70% fall in Lp(a) (range 52 to 87%). Authors note the small sample. — [Criado et al., Dermatol Ther 2015 (abstract)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25824252)
- German guideline on danazol: doses of 3 to 5 mg/kg or 200 mg daily were used in studies with response after 1 to 2 weeks; danazol sits in the lower-ranked columns of the therapy table (not first line), off label. — [AWMF S1 guideline PDF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- Other reports: a child with LV and raised Lp(a) responded promptly to continuous low molecular weight heparin (anticoagulation, not Lp(a) lowering). — cited in [Burg et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9574051). A young woman with raised factor VIII and Lp(a) cleared on doxycycline, pentoxifylline, aspirin, clindamycin and naproxen, with the authors stating the role of Lp(a) in LV needs more research. — [Case report, PMC13639980](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13639980/)
- Existing lipid drugs lower Lp(a) only modestly: PCSK9 antibodies by about 27% on average (95% CI 24.1 to 29.8) in a meta-analysis of randomised trials (preprint); evolocumab 26.9% in FOURIER; inclisiran 18.6% in ORION-11. — [PCSK9 inhibitor meta-analysis, medRxiv 2024](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.07.10.24310245.full.pdf) (search-snippet level)
- Pelacarsen (Novartis/Ionis antisense): Lp(a)HORIZON topline announced 4 September 2026: no reduction in the composite of cardiovascular death, non-fatal myocardial infarction, non-fatal stroke and urgent coronary revascularisation versus placebo, despite substantial Lp(a) lowering and acceptable safety. — [Novartis media release](https://www.novartis.com/news/media-releases/novartis-announces-lpahorizon-phase-iii-topline-results-pelacarsen-patients-elevated-lpa-and-established-cardiovascular-disease-cvd)
- Lp(a)HORIZON full results (AHA, 7 November 2026; published in NEJM, DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa2610159): 8,323 participants, 797 sites, 42 countries, Lp(a) at least 70 mg/dL with established cardiovascular disease, median follow-up 4.7 years; Lp(a) fell about 78% at 12 months; major cardiovascular events 12.3% (511) on pelacarsen versus 12.1% (503) on placebo; no benefit in the subgroup with Lp(a) at least 90 mg/dL; discontinuation for adverse events 20.6% versus 7.5%; 19 anaphylaxis cases (0.5%) versus none. Novartis told patient organisations on 16 October 2026 that it would discontinue the pelacarsen development programme for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (single source). — [FH Europe Foundation summary, 16 November 2026](https://fhef.org/news/lpahorizon-results-of-landmark-clinical-trial-presented-at-aha-2026/); trial record [NCT04023552](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04023552)
- Olpasiran (Amgen siRNA): OCEAN(a)-Outcomes is active, not recruiting, 7,297 enrolled, registry primary completion date 31 March 2028 (record updated 27 October 2026). — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05581303](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05581303). Secondary sources gave December 2026 as the expected readout; the registry date is later, so the two conflict. — [Review, Curr Atheroscler Rep 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11883-026-01453-9)
- Lepodisiran (Lilly siRNA): ACCLAIM-Lp(a), active not recruiting, 17,300 enrolled, primary completion March 2029. — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06292013](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06292013)
- Muvalaplin (Lilly oral small molecule): phase 3 outcomes trial recruiting, 10,450 planned, primary completion March 2031. — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07157774](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07157774)
- Zerlasiran: described as phase 3 ready with no active pivotal outcomes trial identified. — [Review, Curr Atheroscler Rep 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11883-026-01453-9) (search-snippet level)
- Approval status: search results in November 2026 consistently state that there are no approved Lp(a)-lowering therapies and that the pelacarsen result was the first major test of the Lp(a) hypothesis. — [Medscape, 2026](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/not-so-fast-throwing-out-lp-hypothesis-these-experts-say-2026a10011hv); [Review, Curr Atheroscler Rep 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11883-026-01453-9) (search-snippet level)

### Inferences
- "Associated with LV": yes, in Brazilian and German patients. "Treating it improves LV": unproven. The danazol series cannot separate Lp(a) lowering from danazol's other effects on fibrinolysis, and it has four patients.
- Measuring Lp(a) once is still reasonable: it is in the German guideline's initial panel, it is genetically determined and stable, it is an immunoassay that should not be affected by rivaroxaban (inference, no source found that tested this), and a high value matters for cardiovascular risk management independent of LV.
- A raised Lp(a) would not currently open a proven LV-specific treatment. PCSK9 inhibitors lower Lp(a) by only about a quarter and have no LV data. The specific agents are unapproved, the first outcomes trial was negative for cardiovascular events, and a skin microvascular benefit has never been tested.
- The lower Lp(a) prevalence in the Chinese cohort (about 13%) suggests the yield in East and Southeast Asian patients may be lower than the 42% European and Brazilian figures.

### Gaps
- No report of niacin, PCSK9 inhibitor, inclisiran, lipoprotein apheresis, pelacarsen, olpasiran, lepodisiran, zerlasiran or muvalaplin in LV was found.
- I could not confirm from a regulator's own website that no Lp(a) drug is approved in any jurisdiction; this rests on secondary sources and on the fact that all outcomes trials other than pelacarsen's are still running.
- The Novartis discontinuation of pelacarsen is single-sourced (patient organisation summary).
- Lp(a) thresholds used to define "raised" differ between LV studies and were not reported in the abstracts read.

## 3. PAI-1 4G/4G and impaired fibrinolysis: how common, and is there targeted treatment?

### Takeaway
The PAI-1 4G allele is found in about 85% of tested LV patients (4G/4G homozygous about 22 to 29%), but it is also common in the general population and one French study found no difference from controls, so it has little diagnostic value. Targeted treatment is limited to case reports of low-dose intravenous tPA for refractory ulcers; no PAI-1 inhibitor has been reported in LV.

### Cited Findings
- Systematic review of 30 reports, 265 patients: PAI-1 -675 4G/5G tested in 95 patients; heterozygous 53 (55.79%), 4G/4G homozygous 28 (29.47%), any 4G carrier 81 (85.26%). In Asian patients the carrier rate was 82.90%, in European patients 94.45%. — [Gao and Jin, Int Wound J 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8450804)
- Chinese cohort: PAI-1 4G/5G promoter polymorphism in 32/38 (84.2%), the most prevalent laboratory finding. — [Ban et al. 2026](https://dpcj.org/index.php/dpc/article/view/6930)
- French cohort: genotypes 4G/4G 22%, 4G/5G 45%, 5G/5G 33%, "not different from control samples"; PAI-1 antigen was raised in only one patient (5G/5G, with severe hypertriglyceridaemia). — [Gardette et al. 2018](https://www.medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-2965)
- Turkish case-control study: plasma PAI-1 2.3 ng/mL in 16 LV patients versus 1.89 in controls (P = 0.007), with reduced flow-mediated dilation, suggesting endothelial dysfunction. — [Apti Sengun et al. 2023 (abstract)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37543163/)
- Guideline: LV patients show raised PAI-1 (4G/5G polymorphism) and reduced tPA activity; PAI-1 4G/5G genotyping is listed among optional genetic tests, not in the recommended initial panel. — [AWMF S1 guideline PDF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- tPA: a 33-year-old woman with refractory ankle ulcers, raised plasma PAI-1 and 4G/4G homozygosity healed completely with heparin plus tPA; authors proposed the genotype might identify responders to fibrinolytic therapy and asked for further study. — [Deng et al., Arch Dermatol 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17116837/)
- Guideline on tPA: may be considered for therapy-refractory ulcers, off label; alteplase 10 mg per day (about one tenth of the myocardial infarction dose), reported as intravenous infusion over 4 hours on 14 days. Fibrinolytics are not among first-line options. — [AWMF S1 guideline PDF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- Review comment: tPA has limited accessibility and bleeding risk; no outcome numbers. — [Burg et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9574051); [Eswaran et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515)
- Danazol is described as inducing hepatic synthesis of protease inhibitors and altering lipids including Lp(a). — search-snippet level, see [Criado et al. 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25824252)

### Inferences
- "Associated with LV": weakly, and non-specifically. "Treating it improves LV": case-report level for tPA, which is a rescue treatment for an active refractory flare and not a maintenance or relapse-prevention strategy.
- Genotyping PAI-1 is unlikely to change management for someone already controlled on rivaroxaban. Genetic tests are unaffected by anticoagulants.

### Gaps
- No report of an oral PAI-1 inhibitor (for example TM5614) in LV was found; my search returned nothing on it.
- No study compares relapse rates by PAI-1 genotype.

## 4. MTHFR variants and homocysteine: does folic acid or B vitamin treatment help, and is there any point when homocysteine is normal?

### Takeaway
B6, B12 and folic acid are recommended by the German guideline only when homocysteine is raised, as an add-on to anticoagulation, based on case reports. With a normal homocysteine (7.3 µmol/L here) there is no evidence of benefit, MTHFR genotyping is discouraged by thrombosis specialists, and randomised trials show homocysteine lowering does not prevent venous thrombosis.

### Cited Findings
- Guideline recommendation 11: in hyperhomocysteinaemia, add vitamin B6, B12 and folic acid (off label), expert consensus +1.4; studies "show a positive effect in addition to anticoagulation". Fasting homocysteine is in the recommended initial panel (+1.2); vitamins B6, B12 and folate and MTHFR C677T are in the wider optional list. — [AWMF S1 guideline PDF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- Review: vitamin substitution for hyperhomocysteinaemia has "only modest therapeutical consequences" but is easy to do. — [Burg et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9574051)
- Case reports: two refractory LV cases with hyperhomocysteinaemia resolved after folic acid plus B6 and B12 (ulcers healed at 7 weeks and 2 months) with homocysteine normalisation. — [Errichetti and Stinco, Acta Derm Venereol](https://medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-2416). A further case healed under "complex therapy" including folic acid, B6 and B12 with no recurrence in 16 months. — [Meiss et al. 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16581568)
- Japanese case, MTHFR 677TT: ulcers healed within 2 weeks after smoking cessation plus folic acid and B12; the authors' opinion was that folic acid is advisable in 677TT regardless of homocysteine level. — [Asahikawa Medical University case report](https://asahikawa-med.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/4078/files/4794.pdf) (search-snippet level)
- MTHFR carrier rates in LV: C677T 71/129 (55.04%; TT 19.38%), A1298C 36/82 (43.90%). In Asian LV patients C677T carrier rate 51.15%. — [Gao and Jin 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8450804)
- Hyperhomocysteinaemia frequency in LV: 3/21 (14.3%) Mayo; 10/12 Germany; 50% France; 30/80 (37.5%) China; 7 (23.3%) in a second Chinese cohort. — [Hairston 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17116831/); [Weishaupt 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31009111/); [Gardette 2018](https://www.medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-2965); [Ban 2026](https://dpcj.org/index.php/dpc/article/view/6930); [Deng 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10897505/)
- Outside LV: the VITRO randomised trial found B vitamins did not prevent recurrent venous thrombosis (43/353 versus 50/348 events). — [VITRO, PubMed 16960155](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16960155/). A HOPE-2 analysis found no reduction in venous thromboembolism with homocysteine-lowering vitamins. — [HOPE-2 analysis, PubMed 17470822](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17470822/)
- A 2022 call to action states MTHFR polymorphisms are not risk factors for arterial or venous thrombosis and should not be part of thrombophilia testing, citing the 2013 ACMG guideline "Lack of evidence for MTHFR polymorphism testing" and Choosing Wisely. — [Res Pract Thromb Haemost 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/9175241) (search-snippet level)

### Inferences
- "Associated with LV": raised homocysteine, variably. "Treating it improves LV": only case reports, always alongside other treatment or smoking cessation.
- With homocysteine already normal, MTHFR genotyping is unlikely to add anything actionable. A normal homocysteine already answers the functional question the MTHFR test is trying to ask.
- Checking B12 and folate is cheap and the Chinese cohort found B12 deficiency in 17.8% of those tested, but a normal homocysteine makes a functionally important deficiency unlikely.

### Gaps
- No controlled data on B vitamins in LV. No study of B vitamin supplementation in LV with normal homocysteine.
- Whether the patient's homocysteine was measured fasting is unknown; the guideline specifies fasting.

## 5. Inherited thrombophilias: prevalence in LV and in East and Southeast Asians, and does a positive result change treatment?

### Takeaway
Factor V Leiden and prothrombin G20210A are found in roughly 14% and 11% of tested LV patients overall but are close to absent in East and Southeast Asian populations, where protein C, protein S and antithrombin deficiencies are the relevant inherited thrombophilias (protein C or S abnormality reported in 26 to 38% of tested Chinese LV patients). A positive result rarely changes LV treatment, with specific exceptions: warfarin must be bridged with heparin in protein C or S deficiency, and antithrombin deficiency matters for pregnancy; no data link any of these to how long anticoagulation should continue.

### Cited Findings
- LV overall: factor V G1691A (Leiden) 19/135 (14.07%, all heterozygous); prothrombin G20210A 9/85 (10.59%). By region among LV patients tested: Asia factor V 6.45% and prothrombin 1.67%; Europe 21.74% and 15.79%; South America factor V 17.14%. Authors note the distribution may depend on geography and ethnicity and that onset is earlier in Asian patients. — [Gao and Jin 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8450804)
- German guideline classifies factor V mutation, prothrombin G20210A, "plasminogen activator inhibitor deficiency" and protein C and S deficiency as "rare (in LV)", and Lp(a) elevation, antithrombin deficiency and hyperhomocysteinaemia as "frequent (in LV)"; protein C and protein S are nevertheless in the recommended initial panel (each +1.3), while antithrombin and the genetic tests are in the wider list. — [AWMF S1 guideline PDF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- Chinese LV data: protein S or C deficiency 28/74 (37.8%). — [Ban et al. 2026](https://dpcj.org/index.php/dpc/article/view/6930); decreased protein C 9 (26.4%) and decreased antithrombin 2 (5.9%) of 34. — [Deng et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10897505/)
- Western LV data: decreased protein C or S 2/15 (13.3%). — [Hairston 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17116831/); protein C and/or S deficiency 3/34 (8.82%). — [Eswaran et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515); none detected in the Finnish series. — [Köykkä et al. 2026](https://journals.cambridgemedia.com.au/application/files/2517/8295/4560/koykka.pdf)
- General Asian population: factor V Leiden and prothrombin G20210A prevalence 0 to 0.2% in healthy Asian subjects and 0% in Asian venous thrombosis patients; in Asian venous thrombosis patients protein S deficiency 10.7 to 17.8%, protein C deficiency 8.9 to 10.7%, antithrombin deficiency 4.7 to 8.1% (data mainly Chinese, Indian and Malay cohorts). — [Wang et al., Thrombosis Journal 2018](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1186/s12959-017-0155-z) (search-snippet level)
- ISTH multinational study (1,061 venous thrombosis patients, 1,789 controls; Japan, Korea, Singapore, Hungary, Brazil): protein C p.Arg189Trp carried by 10.53% of Singapore Chinese patients versus 0.85% of controls (odds ratio 13.87, 95% CI 1.73 to 111.39) and by 22.73% of Singapore Malay patients; protein C p.Lys193del in 5.26% of Singapore Chinese patients; protein S Tokushima confined to Japanese; all three variants absent in Caucasians and Brazilians. Factor V Leiden and prothrombin G20210A are "prevalent among the Caucasian population but rare among non-Caucasians". No Vietnamese or Thai samples were included. — [Tsuda et al., Res Pract Thromb Haemost 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7695562)
- Does a result change treatment? Broad coagulation testing is "not generally recommended" by one review because detecting a thrombophilia rarely changes treatment; factor V Leiden, prothrombin mutation, MTHFR and PAI are called optional or academic. — [Burg et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9574051)
- Guideline points where a result does matter: (a) in protein C or S deficiency, starting phenprocoumon or warfarin requires heparin cover because of coumarin necrosis risk, and coumarins are now reserved for exceptions such as antiphospholipid syndrome; (b) antithrombin is "of high relevance" for women planning pregnancy; (c) the guideline calls LV a purely cutaneous vasculopathy and treats evidence of other occlusive or thrombotic manifestations as an exclusion criterion, in which case the underlying syndrome is the diagnosis; (d) several abnormalities together may raise risk more than additively; (e) a haemostasis specialist can be involved in individual cases. — [AWMF S1 guideline PDF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- In the Finnish series the patients who failed first- or second-line treatment included ones with factor V Leiden, activated protein C resistance and raised factor VIII. — [Köykkä et al. 2026](https://journals.cambridgemedia.com.au/application/files/2517/8295/4560/koykka.pdf)

#### Tests distorted by rivaroxaban and when to draw them
- Falsely positive on rivaroxaban: lupus anticoagulant by dRVVT (a particular risk with rivaroxaban) and aPTT-based lupus anticoagulant tests. — [Moser and Smock, Hematology ASH Educ Program 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8791165)
- Falsely raised (a true deficiency can look normal): clot-based protein S activity, clot-based protein C activity, anti-Xa-based antithrombin activity. Activated protein C resistance can be falsely negative. — [Moser and Smock 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8791165)
- Not affected: free and total protein S antigen, protein C antigen, chromogenic protein C activity, thrombin (factor IIa)-based antithrombin activity, anticardiolipin and anti-beta-2 glycoprotein I ELISAs, DNA tests for factor V Leiden and prothrombin G20210A. The article does not address homocysteine or Lp(a). — [Moser and Smock 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8791165)
- Timing: sample at trough (about 24 hours after a once-daily dose, just before the next tablet); interference can persist even at trough. Stopping for 2 to 3 days is mentioned but often not feasible. DOAC-Stop (activated carbon) reduces but does not always eliminate interference. Antigen assays do not detect rare dysfunctional (type II) deficiencies. — [Moser and Smock 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8791165)
- ISTH 2020 lupus anticoagulant guidance: collect before anticoagulation where possible; in DOAC-treated patients drug adsorption may be considered. British Society for Haematology 2020 advised against aPTT or dRVVT lupus anticoagulant tests when DOAC is detectable. In one study dRVVT stayed positive in 16% of rivaroxaban samples after a DOAC filter. — [Review of lupus anticoagulant testing on anticoagulation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8922544) and linked sources (search-snippet level)
- Genetic testing requires counselling by a qualified person under German law (context for the guideline's separation of genetic tests). — [AWMF S1 guideline PDF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a man of Vietnamese background, factor V Leiden and prothrombin G20210A testing has a very low prior yield. Protein C, protein S and antithrombin are the inherited tests most likely to be informative, and protein C variants are documented in Southeast Asian (Singapore Chinese and Malay) populations.
- Because rivaroxaban falsely raises clot-based protein C and S activity and anti-Xa-based antithrombin, a normal result on treatment does not exclude deficiency unless the laboratory used chromogenic or antigen assays (or a thrombin-based antithrombin assay), or the sample was taken at trough with DOAC removal. It is worth asking the laboratory which method it uses.
- The patient's normal lupus anticoagulant is more reassuring than a positive one would be if it was drawn on rivaroxaban, since the drug's typical error is false positivity. This is an inference from the direction of interference.
- The high protein C/S "deficiency" rates in Chinese LV cohorts should be read cautiously: the reports do not state the assay type, whether patients were on anticoagulants or warfarin, or whether results were repeated.
- A confirmed strong thrombophilia (antithrombin, protein C or protein S deficiency) might reasonably make a clinician more cautious about stopping anticoagulation, but I found no LV data showing it predicts relapse. That is a judgement for the haematologist.

### Gaps
- No Vietnamese-specific prevalence data for protein C, S or antithrombin deficiency were found.
- No LV study stratifies treatment response, dose or relapse by inherited thrombophilia status.
- No guidance was found that specifies a rivaroxaban interruption interval for thrombophilia testing in LV.

## 6. Antiphospholipid syndrome: why a complete, repeated screen matters and how it changes drug choice

### Takeaway
Antiphospholipid antibodies are among the most frequently reported abnormalities in LV (from 2 to 7% in the Singapore series up to 58% in a small Chinese series), LV lesions are now a recognised microvascular feature in the 2023 ACR/EULAR classification criteria, and a positive result is the one finding that clearly changes drug choice: the German LV guideline says DOACs should not be used when antiphospholipid antibodies are present, and EULAR and ISTH advise warfarin over rivaroxaban for triple-positive or arterial disease. A screen is incomplete without anti-beta-2 glycoprotein I IgG and IgM.

### Cited Findings
- German guideline recommendation 5: initial laboratory workup should include antiphospholipid antibodies, defined as lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin antibodies and beta-2 glycoprotein I antibodies (expert consensus +1.7, the highest-rated laboratory item). — [AWMF S1 guideline PDF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- German guideline, DOAC section: "when antiphospholipid antibodies are present, treatment with a DOAC should not be given, because rivaroxaban in patients with antiphospholipid syndrome showed an increased risk of recurrent thrombotic events". Coumarins (INR 2.5 to 3.5 stated) remain an option only in exceptional cases such as antiphospholipid syndrome. Antiphospholipid syndrome and Sneddon syndrome are differentials that "shall be considered" (+1.6 each). — [AWMF S1 guideline PDF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- EULAR 2019: rivaroxaban should not be used in antiphospholipid syndrome with triple antibody positivity; this followed the TRAPS trial, stopped early after a mean 1.6 years for excess (mainly arterial) events on rivaroxaban versus warfarin. A second trial (Ordi-Ros 2019) found more arterial thrombosis, especially stroke, on rivaroxaban (9 versus 0). — [EULAR recommendations, PubMed 31092409](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31092409/) and search-result summaries of [PMC11034817](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11034817)
- ISTH 2020 guidance (Zuily et al.): warfarin first choice; patients with arterial antiphospholipid syndrome or triple positivity should receive warfarin; single- or double-positive venous cases may be DOAC candidates. The European Medicines Agency recommends against DOACs in antiphospholipid syndrome, especially triple-positive; British guidance recommends switching triple-positive patients from DOAC to warfarin. — [ISTH SSC guidance, PubMed 32881337](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32881337/) (search-snippet level)
- 2023 ACR/EULAR classification criteria: entry requires at least one positive antiphospholipid test within 3 years of a clinical criterion; six clinical domains include a microvascular domain whose items include livedo racemosa and livedoid vasculopathy; laboratory domains are lupus anticoagulant and solid-phase anticardiolipin and anti-beta-2 glycoprotein I; classification needs at least 3 points from clinical and 3 from laboratory domains. — [2023 ACR/EULAR criteria, PubMed 37640450](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37640450/) (search-snippet level)
- Frequency in LV cohorts: lupus anticoagulant 5/28 (17.9%) and anticardiolipin 8/28 (28.6%) at Mayo. — [Hairston 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17116831/); 6/34 (17.64%) in Brazil. — [Eswaran et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515); 14/24 (58.3%) in a Chinese series. — [Feng et al. 2014](https://medicaljournals.se/acta/content/abstract/10.2340/00015555-1711); lupus anticoagulant 6/14 (42.9%) and anticardiolipin 14/81 (17.3%) in the 358-patient Chinese cohort. — [Ban et al. 2026](https://dpcj.org/index.php/dpc/article/view/6930); anticardiolipin 7% and lupus anticoagulant 2% in Singapore. — [Gan et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23052434/)
- Anti-beta-2 glycoprotein I can be the positive test when the others are not: in a Turkish series it was positive in 12.5% while lupus anticoagulant and anticardiolipin were each 6.3% (16 patients). — [Apti Sengun et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37543163/)
- Assay interference: anticardiolipin and anti-beta-2 glycoprotein I ELISAs are unaffected by rivaroxaban; lupus anticoagulant clotting tests are prone to false positives. — [Moser and Smock 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8791165)
- German guideline, open research questions: antiphospholipid syndrome, protein S or C deficiency, antithrombin or tPA deficiency can cause LV symptoms; when the coagulopathy has its own manifestations the condition should be named as that syndrome with LV as part of it. — [AWMF S1 guideline PDF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)

### Inferences
- The patient has two of the three criteria tests (anticardiolipin IgG/IgM and lupus anticoagulant, both normal). Adding anti-beta-2 glycoprotein I IgG and IgM completes the screen; it is an ELISA and can be drawn at any time on rivaroxaban.
- This is the most decision-relevant missing test: a persistently positive result would call into question rivaroxaban as the right long-term drug (and would argue against simply stopping anticoagulation), whereas a complete negative screen supports the current DOAC choice.
- Persistence on repeat testing at least 12 weeks apart is the long-standing classification requirement for antiphospholipid syndrome; a single positive, especially low-titre IgM, is common and non-specific. The exact weights in the 2023 criteria (I recall "suspected" LV lesions on examination scoring lower than biopsy-"established" LV) were not verified in a full text and should be checked before being quoted.
- Antibody status can change over years, so a screen done long ago may reasonably be repeated when a long-term decision (staying on or stopping a DOAC) is being made. This is an inference, not a cited recommendation.

### Gaps
- I could not open the full text of the 2023 ACR/EULAR criteria, the EULAR 2019 recommendations or the ISTH 2020 guidance; the points above are from abstracts and search summaries.
- No trial compares DOAC with warfarin specifically in antiphospholipid-positive LV.
- The dose studied in the antiphospholipid trials was rivaroxaban 20 mg; nothing addresses 10 mg in this setting.

## 7. Cryoglobulins, cryofibrinogen, cold agglutinins, monoclonal gammopathy, hepatitis B and C, connective tissue disease, malignancy

### Takeaway
These are uncommon in LV cohorts (cryoglobulins 0/71 and ANCA 0/113 in the largest Chinese series; hepatitis B 7% and C 4% in Singapore; connective tissue disease about 4 to 16%), but they are the group in which treating the cause can plausibly clear the skin disease, because the lesions are then LV-like manifestations of another disorder. Evidence that treatment clears LV is limited to case reports, mainly for paraprotein and cryoglobulin disease and for lupus or Sjögren syndrome.

### Cited Findings
- German guideline laboratory table lists Lp(a), cryoglobulin, cryofibrinogen, cold agglutinin, fasting homocysteine, B6, B12, folate, protein C, protein S, antithrombin, ANA, lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin IgG/IgM and beta-2 glycoprotein I IgG/IgM, plus genetic tests. It states an association with cryoglobulins is rare, that cryoglobulins should be measured if hepatitis C is detected, and that type I cryoglobulinaemic occlusive vasculopathy, cryoglobulinaemic vasculitis, ANCA-positive vasculitis, essential thrombocythaemia, cholesterol embolism, haemoglobinopathies and drug-induced ulcers (for example hydroxyurea) are differentials. — [AWMF S1 guideline PDF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- Review workup list adds protein electrophoresis, immunoglobulins, hepatitis and HIV serology and inflammatory markers as routine tests to exclude neoplastic and infectious associations, with cryoglobulin and cryofibrinogen as optional. — [Burg et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9574051)
- Frequencies: cryoglobulins 0/71, ANCA 0/113, rheumatoid factor 2/114 (1.8%). — [Ban et al. 2026](https://dpcj.org/index.php/dpc/article/view/6930). Cryoglobulinaemia and cold agglutinin screened and not detected in Finland. — [Köykkä et al. 2026](https://journals.cambridgemedia.com.au/application/files/2517/8295/4560/koykka.pdf). Hepatitis B 7%, hepatitis C 4%, ANA 14%, anti-MPO 5% in Singapore. — [Gan et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23052434/)
- Connective tissue disease: 17/108 (15.74%) in a US records cohort (lupus 10, Sjögren 3, dermatomyositis 2, systemic sclerosis 2). — [Argobi 2025](https://www.dovepress.com/prevalence-of-livedoid-vasculopathy-among-patients-with-connective-tis-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-CCID); 1/26 lupus in France. — [Gardette et al. 2018](https://www.medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-2965); rheumatoid arthritis 3/24 in Finland. — [Köykkä et al. 2026](https://journals.cambridgemedia.com.au/application/files/2517/8295/4560/koykka.pdf)
- Guideline on treatment: glucocorticoids have no direct benefit in LV, but with an associated autoimmune disease they should be used to treat that disease (recommendation 10, +1); case reports exist for Sjögren syndrome and lupus. General principle stated: "an underlying disease or a procoagulant defect should, where possible, be treated specifically." — [AWMF S1 guideline PDF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- Malignancy: no statistically increased association; LV-like lesions with malignancy need causal therapy. — [AWMF S1 guideline PDF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf). Solid carcinoma in 2/24 Finnish patients (older cohort, median age 53.5). — [Köykkä et al. 2026](https://journals.cambridgemedia.com.au/application/files/2517/8295/4560/koykka.pdf)
- Paraprotein and cryoglobulin case reports (search-snippet level): extensive LV with type I cryoglobulinaemia that improved on rivaroxaban and led to a diagnosis of multiple myeloma, with authors recommending long-term follow-up. — [Case report, Arch Clin Biomed Res 2023](https://www.doi.org/10.26502/acbr.50170374). Two MGUS cases in which gammopathy, cryoglobulinaemia, occluding vasculopathy and vasculitis disappeared after 7 months of treatment. — [PMC6403116](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6403116). Plasma cell disease treated with bortezomib and dexamethasone with disappearance of cryoglobulin and improvement of purpura. — [PMC10937136](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10937136/)
- Cryofibrinogenaemia: LV associated with secondary cryofibrinogenaemia after COVID-19; lesions recurred after a later viral infection despite anticoagulation. — [JAAD Case Rep 2022, PMC8977222](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8977222)
- Hepatitis B: one case report of LV with subcutaneous necrotising venulitis in an asymptomatic hepatitis B carrier. — cited in [Eswaran et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515)

### Inferences
- "Associated with LV": occasionally. "Treating it improves LV": plausible and reported for paraprotein or cryoglobulin disease and autoimmune disease, at case-report level only.
- These tests are cheap, mostly done once, and unaffected by rivaroxaban (serology, electrophoresis, complement, ANCA are immunoassays; inference). Hepatitis B and C serology is particularly relevant in a Southeast Asian adult given the 7% and 4% Singapore figures and regional hepatitis B prevalence, and hepatitis B is also the classic association of polyarteritis nodosa.
- Cryoglobulin and cryofibrinogen need correct handling (sample kept at 37 °C until separation; cryofibrinogen needs plasma, not serum), otherwise false negatives are likely. This is standard laboratory practice and not from a source cited here.
- After more than 10 years of disease in a man in his 30s with normal blood count, an occult malignancy or paraprotein driving the disease is unlikely but a serum protein electrophoresis with immunofixation is a one-off, low-cost exclusion.

### Gaps
- No cohort reports the frequency of monoclonal gammopathy or cryofibrinogen in LV with a stated denominator.
- No series follows LV outcomes after treatment of hepatitis B or C.
- The case reports were read only as search summaries.

## 8. Chronic venous insufficiency and venous hypertension without significant reflux; role of venous intervention

### Takeaway
Venous disease coexists with LV in a variable minority to majority of patients (58% with duplex-detected venous insufficiency in an older Finnish series; stasis-dermatitis-like presentation in 3.4% of a young Chinese cohort), and compression is widely used, but I found no LV-specific evidence that venous ablation or other venous intervention prevents LV recurrence, and none at all for patients without significant reflux.

### Cited Findings
- Finland: venous insufficiency on duplex in 14/24 (58.3%), past deep vein thrombosis in 8/24 (33.3%), peripheral arterial disease 4/24 (16.7%); compression is "standard care for every LV patient if no contraindication" at that centre (bandages in 11/24, stockings eventually in 16/24). Cohort median age at diagnosis 53.5, overweight or obese in 75%. — [Köykkä et al. 2026](https://journals.cambridgemedia.com.au/application/files/2517/8295/4560/koykka.pdf)
- German guideline: venous ulcer from chronic venous insufficiency is one of the two most important differentials (develops more slowly, with duplex reflux, lipodermatosclerosis, ochre pigmentation, varicosities, corona phlebectatica). Compression class I or 20 mmHg "can be considered" if tolerated. Pathogenesis section: higher perfusion pressure and lower temperature in the lower limb mean lower local concentration of thrombolytic factors. — [AWMF S1 guideline PDF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- Reviews: venous insufficiency is among the most reported comorbidities (no numbers); compression "may be beneficial, especially in patients with venous insufficiency"; no trial data. — [Burg et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9574051); [Eswaran et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515)
- Mayo: transcutaneous oxygen was reduced in 20/27 (74.1%) tested. — [Hairston 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17116831/)
- China: lower limb oedema in 20/69 (29.0%); lower limb thrombosis on ultrasound in 2/69 (2.9%). — [Gao et al. 2025](https://www.dovepress.com/clinical-characteristics-and-disease-course-of-livedoid-vasculopathy-a-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-CCID). Stasis-dermatitis-like lesions in 12/358 (3.4%); livedo worsens with cold exposure or prolonged standing. — [Ban et al. 2026](https://dpcj.org/index.php/dpc/article/view/6930)
- Atrophie blanche without venous insufficiency or thrombophilia should prompt exclusion of polyarteritis nodosa. — [Mimouni et al., Br J Dermatol 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12752140)

### Inferences
- "Associated with LV": yes, especially in older Western patients. "Treating it improves LV": compression is supported by expert opinion only; venous ablation is unsupported in LV. With a duplex showing no significant reflux there is no target for intervention.
- Venous stasis and dependency as an aggravating factor (standing, heat, oedema) fit the guideline's pathogenesis statement and the summer pattern in Asian cohorts. That supports low-risk measures (compression if tolerated, leg elevation, avoiding prolonged standing and heat) more than procedures.
- The 58% Finnish figure probably reflects the age and weight of that cohort and does not transfer to a normal-weight man in his 30s.

### Gaps
- No study of venous ablation, sclerotherapy or perforator treatment in LV was found. The early-ablation ulcer trial data concern ordinary venous ulcers.
- No study measures ambulatory venous pressure or tests compression against no compression in LV.
- No data on LV in patients with obstruction or calf pump failure without reflux.

## 9. Is there evidence that patients with an identified and treated cause relapse less than those with idiopathic LV?

### Takeaway
No. I found no study comparing relapse between "cause found and treated" and idiopathic LV, and the available indirect evidence does not show that thrombophilia-positive patients do better or worse. The clearest signals of durable remission after correcting a factor are anecdotal: smoking cessation, renal transplantation, clearance of a paraprotein, and treatment of an associated autoimmune disease.

### Cited Findings
- French cohort: of 9 patients in long-term remission (no flare for at least 4 years), 5 had no maintenance treatment; 2 of these remitted after stopping smoking and 2 after renal transplantation; LV disappeared spontaneously in 2. 42% of the cohort smoked. The paper did not analyse response by thrombophilia status. — [Gardette et al. 2018](https://www.medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-2965)
- Chinese 358-patient cohort: no significant difference in outcome between anticoagulant monotherapy and other regimens; authors conclude anticoagulation alone "may be insufficient" to prevent recurrence. Outcome was not analysed by laboratory finding. — [Ban et al. 2026](https://dpcj.org/index.php/dpc/article/view/6930)
- Brazilian prospective study: 18/34 had thrombophilia; 13 were treated with warfarin or heparin and 11 responded; remission lasted on average 7.8 months after treatment stopped. — [Di Giacomo et al. 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20337814/)
- Finnish series: both patients whose ulcers never healed had prothrombotic findings (activated protein C resistance with factor V Leiden; raised factor VIII). — [Köykkä et al. 2026](https://journals.cambridgemedia.com.au/application/files/2517/8295/4560/koykka.pdf)
- A 2026 Brazilian report describes JAK inhibitors for LV "associated with thrombophilia and refractory to anticoagulation". — [Criado et al., An Bras Dermatol 2026 (title only)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41875726/)
- IVIG review: about 80 cases, 95% response; 22.2% had thrombophilia. — [Eswaran et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515)
- Guideline: finding a procoagulant parameter does not secure the diagnosis and its absence does not exclude it; a cause "should be treated specifically where possible". The first-line treatment (anticoagulation) is the same with or without a thrombophilia, except with antiphospholipid antibodies. — [AWMF S1 guideline PDF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- Review: detecting a thrombophilia rarely changes treatment. — [Burg et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9574051)
- Smoking: a Swiss multicentre study (15 patients, Zurich, Basel, Bern) is registered under the title "Livedoid vasculopathy: strong association with smoking, weak association with thrombophilia"; no results are posted. — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05878327](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05878327)

### Inferences
- The realistic value of a fuller workup is in three places, not in a general promise of cure: (1) ruling antiphospholipid syndrome in or out, which changes the drug; (2) excluding a mimic or driver that has its own treatment (polyarteritis nodosa, cryoglobulin or paraprotein disease, connective tissue disease, hepatitis); (3) identifying a strong thrombophilia that informs how cautious to be about a trial off anticoagulation.
- Most laboratory "causes" in LV (Lp(a), PAI-1, MTHFR, factor V Leiden) are lifelong traits with no corrective therapy shown to alter LV, so finding them explains but does not cure.
- Modifiable non-laboratory factors with at least anecdotal support are smoking, heat exposure, venous stasis and weight or blood pressure (guideline cofactors).

## 10. Natural history: duration of activity, drug-free remission, predictors of relapse after stopping anticoagulation

### Takeaway
LV is chronic and relapsing for years, but a meaningful minority reach long treatment-free remission: 14.4% had no new ulcer for more than 4 years in the largest Chinese cohort and 36% reached at least 4 years in a French cohort, while about 60% relapse within 2 years of a successful course and median time to relapse after stopping rivaroxaban was 7 months. No validated predictor of relapse exists; summer or heat is the most consistent trigger, and the German guideline's algorithm explicitly allows a trial off treatment once stable.

### Cited Findings
- Chinese cohort (236 with follow-up): long-term remission (no new ulcer for more than 4 years) 34 (14.4%); short-term remission (2 to under 4 years) 55 (23.3%); recurrence within 2 years 147 (62.3%). By regimen, anticoagulants alone: 21 long-term remission, 39 short-term, 105 recurrence (of 165). Average disease duration at presentation 3.9 ± 4.7 years (range 10 days to 30 years). — [Ban et al. 2026](https://dpcj.org/index.php/dpc/article/view/6930)
- Rivaroxaban cohort (34 patients, mean follow-up 32.8 months, starting dose 10 mg daily): 26 stopped after resolution (median treatment 9.0 months); 16 relapsed at a median 7.0 months (IQR 5.0 to 12) with mostly mild severity; 10 had no recurrence over a median 17.5 months, 2 of them for over 3 years. Of those who relapsed, 14 restarted and 12 stopped again after a median second course of 3.0 months. Three mild summer relapses off treatment settled without restarting as temperatures fell; 4 patients relapsed with heat exposure while on treatment. No formal predictor analysis. — [Deng et al., JAAD Int 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10897505/)
- French cohort (26 patients, median follow-up 5 years): long-term remission (at least 4 years without flare) 9 (36%), 5 of them off all treatment; short-term remission (at least 2 years) 4 (16%); 12 remained active, 8 of them severe and refractory. — [Gardette et al. 2018](https://www.medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-2965)
- Brazilian prospective study: after warfarin or heparin, remission lasted an average of 7.8 months after stopping. — [Di Giacomo et al. 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20337814/)
- Singapore: 49 of 70 achieved remission (55% needed combination therapy), mean follow-up 50 months; described as "high morbidity, chronic relapsing". — [Gan et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23052434/)
- Thailand: Kaplan-Meier improvement 38.5% at 1 year, 53.7% at 2 years, 57.9% at 3 years; median treatment duration 11.4 months among the 48 who improved. — [Rujitharanawong et al. 2021](https://murex.mahidol.ac.th/en/publications/livedoid-vasculopathy-clinical-course-and-long-term-outcome-in-as/)
- Finland: after first-line healing, 12 of 17 needed second-line treatment; in 4 patients wounds reopened within 2 to 9 months; median treatment duration 11 months. — [Köykkä et al. 2026](https://journals.cambridgemedia.com.au/application/files/2517/8295/4560/koykka.pdf)
- Rivaroxaban, China: 91% (40/44) complete ulcer remission within 12 weeks; some patients relapsed despite maintenance therapy, which the authors take to suggest additional pathogenic pathways. — [Zhao et al., Clin Exp Dermatol 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38287670/)
- Reported remission ranges: 4 weeks to 23 months after rivaroxaban, 3 months to 8 years after IVIG. — [Eswaran et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515)
- Seasonal pattern: summer worsening in 150/183 (82.4%) Chinese patients with a seasonal pattern, winter in 21 (11.5%). — [Ban et al. 2026](https://dpcj.org/index.php/dpc/article/view/6930); about 40% spring or summer relapse in Brazil. — [Criado et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33496999/); 1/24 in Finland. — [Köykkä et al. 2026](https://journals.cambridgemedia.com.au/application/files/2517/8295/4560/koykka.pdf). The German guideline stresses LV occurs year-round. — [AWMF S1 guideline PDF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- Guideline algorithm: after healing, reduce to half-therapeutic heparin; if no worsening on reduction, a trial off treatment ("Auslassversuch"); if high risk of worsening, continue or switch to rivaroxaban 20 mg; with stable findings, rivaroxaban 10 mg daily or a trial off. Rising localised burning pain ("angina cutis") typically precedes new ulcers by 1 to 3 days and treatment in that window can often prevent necrosis; a pain diary is recommended (+1.9) and the dose should be increased at marked pain increase. In studies rivaroxaban was used at 10 mg once daily for maintenance and 10 mg twice daily for flare or relapse. — [AWMF S1 guideline PDF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- Peripheral neuropathy develops in a subset (9% Singapore; 10/20 tested in France, median 11.4 years after skin onset), which is one argument for not leaving disease active. — [Gan et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23052434/); [Gardette et al. 2018](https://www.medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-2965)

### Inferences
- Indefinite continuous anticoagulation is not the only pattern in the literature. Published practice includes stopping after resolution and restarting at relapse (Deng), and the German algorithm includes a trial off or a 10 mg maintenance dose once stable. Relapses after stopping were mostly mild and re-responded.
- In a tropical climate with no cool season, the "stop in winter, cover the summer" approach suggested by the Chinese data has no natural equivalent; heat exposure is constant, which may partly explain a long relapsing course. This is an inference.
- A disease duration over 10 years places this case in the persistent group; cohorts do not report how remission probability changes with duration.
- Early-warning pain as a trigger for stepping dose up is guideline-supported and gives a basis for intermittent or pain-guided strategies under supervision.

### Gaps
- No formal analysis of predictors of relapse after stopping anticoagulation (thrombophilia status, D-dimer, duration of remission before stopping, season, dose) was found.
- No data on tapering schedules (for example alternate-day rivaroxaban) versus abrupt stopping, beyond Deng's mention that some patients used 10 mg every other day.
- No cohort from an equatorial climate reports relapse after stopping.

## 11. Does a mixed biopsy (thrombi plus leukocytoclasis) change the workup? Tests that separate LV from small vessel vasculitis and cutaneous polyarteritis nodosa

### Takeaway
Some inflammation does not exclude LV: lymphocytic vasculitis was present in 38.3% of 358 LV biopsies and immunoreactant deposition is common and non-specific, particularly in late biopsies. But neutrophilic infiltration of vessel walls, leukocytoclasis or fibrinoid wall necrosis is not characteristic of LV and should prompt a search for primary vasculitis, above all cutaneous polyarteritis nodosa, which was found on deep repeat biopsy in 6 of 29 patients presenting with atrophie blanche.

### Cited Findings
- German guideline histology: fibrin in vessel walls and fibrin thrombi in upper and mid dermal vessels are characteristic; perivascular inflammatory infiltrates and leukocytoclasia "are not characteristic and are found in the acute stage at most to a minor degree"; "prominent inflammatory permeation of vessel walls or (fibrinoid) vessel wall necrosis should suggest a primary vasculitic process"; biopsies taken late can show a secondary inflammatory infiltrate and are then no longer proof but still useful to exclude differentials. Histological proof is possible only in the acute ischaemic stage, ideally from a spindle biopsy at the lesion edge. Immunofluorescence is non-specific and immunoglobulin or complement deposits have no pathogenic relevance. — [AWMF S1 guideline PDF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- Lymphocytic vasculitis in LV: 137/358 (38.3%); 48 involved medium-sized vessels (arterioles and venules), 89 small vessels only; infiltrate mainly CD4-positive T cells. — [Yuan et al., J Cutan Pathol 2024 (abstract)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37837185/)
- In atypical LV cases all biopsies showed thrombosis or fibrin in superficial vessels "lacking perivascular neutrophil infiltration", which the authors use to separate LV from leukocytoclastic vasculitis; 191 patients had previously been labelled allergic vasculitis (100) or Henoch-Schönlein purpura (75). — [Ban et al. 2026](https://dpcj.org/index.php/dpc/article/view/6930)
- Direct immunofluorescence positivity in LV: multiple vascular conjugates in 31/36 (86.1%) at Mayo. — [Hairston 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17116831/); C3 41.4%, IgM 20.7%, IgA 17.2%, IgG 3.5% of 29 Chinese patients. — [Gao et al. 2025](https://www.dovepress.com/clinical-characteristics-and-disease-course-of-livedoid-vasculopathy-a-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-CCID)
- Cutaneous polyarteritis nodosa: among 29 patients presenting with atrophie blanche at Johns Hopkins (1996 to 2002) who had deep and multiple biopsies, 6 had medium-vessel vasculitis consistent with polyarteritis nodosa; 3 of the 6 had earlier been diagnosed with livedoid vasculitis. Authors advise excluding it in atrophie blanche without venous insufficiency or thrombophilia, especially with mononeuritis multiplex, and note that repeated deep biopsies are often needed. — [Mimouni et al., Br J Dermatol 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12752140) (search-snippet level)
- Guideline differential table: polyarteritis nodosa is the top-rated differential (+1.8); features favouring it are palpable subcutaneous nodules before ulceration, mononeuritis multiplex, initial response to glucocorticoids, and vasculitis of medium-sized vessels at the dermis-subcutis junction, deeper than the vessels affected in LV. Also to be considered: ANCA-positive vasculitis (+1.3), cryoglobulinaemia (+1.3), IgA/IgG/IgM vasculitides, systemic sclerosis, pyoderma gangrenosum. — [AWMF S1 guideline PDF](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/013-098l_S1_Diagnostik-Therapie-Livedovaskulopathie_2026-05.pdf)
- Biopsy technique: at least two biopsies with deep excision in the acute stage to exclude polyarteritis nodosa; do not sample the ulcer base. — [Burg et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9574051)
- Serology in an Asian LV cohort: anti-MPO positive in 5%, hepatitis B 7%, hepatitis C 4%, ANA 14%. — [Gan et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23052434/); ANCA 0/113 in China. — [Ban et al. 2026](https://dpcj.org/index.php/dpc/article/view/6930)
- ADA2 deficiency (DADA2): a recessive monogenic vasculopathy that mimics polyarteritis nodosa and commonly shows livedo racemosa; a case series advised checking ADA2 in patients with inflammation and livedoid vasculitis, especially with stroke or other neurological findings, family history or resistance to conventional treatment. — [ADA2 deficiency case series, PubMed 31848804](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31848804/). Diagnosis is by biallelic ADA2 variants and/or low (under 5% of normal) plasma ADA2 activity. — [GeneReviews: Adenosine Deaminase 2 Deficiency](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK544951/). Plasma ADA2 activity was 99.0% sensitive and 99.7% specific versus controls in a 200-case cohort, though 9% of genetically confirmed cases had detectable activity. — [PubMed 41147740](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41147740/) (all search-snippet level)
- Neuropathy occurs in LV itself (thrombo-occlusive vasculopathy without vasculitis on nerve biopsy in 2 French patients), so neuropathy alone does not prove polyarteritis. — [Gardette et al. 2018](https://www.medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-2965)

### Inferences
- A mixed biopsy widens the workup. The extra tests that distinguish the alternatives are: ANCA with MPO and PR3; complement C3 and C4; cryoglobulins (with rheumatoid factor); hepatitis B and C and HIV serology; serum protein electrophoresis with immunofixation; ANA with extractable antigens if ANA is positive; urinalysis with protein and red cells plus creatinine to look for systemic small or medium vessel vasculitis; direct immunofluorescence on a fresh lesion (IgA-predominant deposits point to IgA vasculitis); and, if doubt persists, a deep incisional biopsy including subcutis from the edge of a new lesion. Urinalysis and kidney function are standard vasculitis screening and are not taken from an LV-specific source.
- The timing and type of the original biopsy matter: a late or ulcer-edge biopsy can show secondary leukocytoclasis in true LV, while a superficial punch can miss the medium-vessel arteritis of polyarteritis nodosa.
- Clinical discriminators cited above: nodules, response to steroids and deep-vessel arteritis favour polyarteritis nodosa; palpable purpura that is not painful or ulcerating in a stellate pattern favours small vessel vasculitis; burning pain preceding porcelain-white stellate scars and prompt response to anticoagulation favour LV (response to anticoagulation is a minor diagnostic criterion in the guideline).
- This distinction matters for the patient's question, because polyarteritis nodosa and small vessel vasculitis are treated with immunomodulation (and DADA2 with anti-TNF agents) and not with lifelong anticoagulation. A decade of control on rivaroxaban alone favours LV.
- ADA2 testing is a low-probability consideration here (adult, no stroke, no systemic inflammation described) but is the one genetic mimic with a specific treatment.

### Gaps
- No study quantifies how often biopsy-labelled LV with leukocytoclasis is reclassified on follow-up.
- No sensitivity or specificity figures for deep versus punch biopsy in separating LV from polyarteritis nodosa beyond the 6/29 series.
- The 9/118 figure for ADA2 variants in polyarteritis nodosa appeared in search results but I could not tie it to a specific URL, so it is not cited.
