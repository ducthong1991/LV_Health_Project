# Food and supplements

No food or supplement treats or prevents LV. Food helps wounds heal. Supplements mostly add bleeding risk on top of rivaroxaban, so the plan is mainly about what to avoid.

## Food

Rivaroxaban has no food rules, unlike older blood thinners.

- **Protein at every meal:** eggs, fish, chicken, tofu, beans. This is what wounds need most.
- **Fruit and vegetables, 3 to 5 portions a day:** guava, oranges, papaya, kiwi, broccoli, kailan, spinach, kang kong.
- **Fish 2 to 3 times a week:** salmon, sardines, mackerel (saba). Sliced fish soup is a good hawker choice.
- **Zinc:** meat, seafood, nuts, seeds.
- **Whole grains:** oats, brown rice.
- **Water:** 1.5 to 2 litres a day, more in the heat.
- **Cut back:** alcohol, sugary drinks, fried and processed food, very salty food such as instant noodles.
- **No smoking.**

### Sample day

- **Breakfast:** oats or wholemeal toast with 2 eggs, kopi O kosong
- **Lunch:** sliced fish soup with extra vegetables, or yong tau foo in soup
- **Snack:** guava or an orange, and a handful of nuts
- **Dinner:** steamed fish or chicken with brown rice and greens, or canh chua cá

## Drinks

| Drink | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| Water | Main drink |
| Unsweetened green tea, ginger tea | Fine |
| Kopi O or teh O kosong | Fine |
| Coke Zero | Fine, about 1 can a day. Not instead of water |
| Chagee milk tea | Fine a few times a week with no sugar or less sugar |
| Regular soft drinks, full sugar bubble tea | Rare treat |
| Turmeric milk | Fine in small amounts: about 1 teaspoon in milk with a pinch of black pepper, unsweetened. A healthy extra, not a treatment |
| Alcohol | Keep light. Heavy drinking raises bleeding risk |

## Seafood

- Seafood does not trigger LV. LV is a clotting problem, not a food allergy.
- The belief that seafood makes wounds fester has no scientific basis. Fish and seafood help healing.
- Avoid a seafood only if it causes an allergic reaction.
- With open wounds, prefer cooked seafood, and keep wounds away from seawater and raw seafood juices. Vibrio bacteria can infect wounds badly.

## Supplements

| Product | Verdict | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plain multivitamin | Fine, with pharmacist approval | No herbal extracts and no high dose vitamin E. Does not prevent LV |
| Vitamin D | Only if a blood test shows it is low | No LV evidence |
| Protein or a nutrition drink | Only if appetite or diet is poor | Food is enough otherwise |
| Folic acid, B6, B12 | Not needed | Homocysteine is normal at 7.3 umol/L |
| Zinc, vitamin C tablets | Not needed | No benefit shown for leg ulcers unless deficient |
| Collagen, L arginine, probiotics, resveratrol | Not needed | No LV data |
| Daflon (diosmin and hesperidin) | Not needed unless the doctor suggests it | Sold over the counter in Singapore. Made for vein disease, and the duplex scan showed no significant reflux. Animal data suggest it may raise rivaroxaban levels |
| Fish oil capsules | Leave out, or 1 standard capsule a day only with approval | No LV benefit. Large doses can prolong bleeding. Eat fish instead |
| Vitamin E above multivitamin levels | Avoid | Adds bleeding risk |
| Nattokinase, lumbrokinase | Avoid | They break down clots and add bleeding risk |
| Ginkgo | Avoid | Bleeding reports and no benefit |
| Turmeric or curcumin capsules | Avoid | Bleeding interaction. Rare liver injury with high absorption products |
| Garlic or ginger extract capsules | Avoid | Add bleeding risk. Normal amounts in cooking are fine |
| St John's wort | Avoid | Lowers rivaroxaban by about a quarter |
| High dose niacin | Avoid | More bleeding and no benefit in a large trial |
| Aspirin bought without a prescription | Avoid | Adding it to a blood thinner raises major bleeding risk |
| Chinese herbal "blood circulation" products such as danshen and dong quai | Avoid | Major interactions with blood thinners. Some herbal products have been found to contain hidden drugs |

Show the pharmacist the full label of anything new before taking it. Buy only from established pharmacies.
