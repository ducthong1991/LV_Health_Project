**What to expect on LV treatment**

- Blood tests for clotting disorders: done 13 Oct, all normal.
- Medication that reduces clotting, such as rivaroxaban or an antiplatelet drug.
- Pain often improves within 1 to 2 weeks; ulcers heal over about 1 to 3 months.
- Recurrence is common, so treatment may run for months or during flare seasons.

**Seek urgent care if**

- Bleeding keeps soaking through after 15 to 20 minutes of firm pressure, or is dripping.
- Stitches come loose or the wound gapes open.
- Spreading redness, swelling, warmth, pus, fever, or rapidly worsening pain.

## LV treatment and self care plan

Rivaroxaban, prescribed on 26 Oct, has the strongest evidence for LV. Pain often eases within about 2 weeks and ulcers heal over 1 to 3 months; food, exercise and protecting the skin help alongside it.

### Medicines

| Medicine | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Rivaroxaban (blood thinner) | Strongest. In a [25 patient trial](https://research.uni-luebeck.de/en/publications/anticoagulation-with-rivaroxaban-for-livedoid-vasculopathy-riliva/), pain fell from 65 to 6 out of 100 over 12 weeks. A [review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.993515/full) of about 70 patients found it worked in 82% |
| Aspirin, dipyridamole | Cheaper common first step, weaker evidence |
| Pentoxifylline | Improves blood flow, often added to aspirin; small studies only |
| IVIG (hospital drip) | About 95% respond, but costly, so kept for stubborn cases |
| Folic acid, B6, B12 | Not needed: your homocysteine was normal (7.3) |

Apart from aspirin and B vitamins, these need a prescription in Singapore.

- Avoid NSAIDs, including Arcoxia, unless the doctor approves; use paracetamol.
- Skip the massage gun and hard tackles unless the doctor approves.

### Food

No diet treats LV directly; these support wound healing and healthy blood vessels.

- Protein at every meal: eggs, fish, chicken, tofu, beans
- 3 to 5 portions of fruit and vegetables a day for vitamin C: guava, oranges, kiwi, broccoli, capsicum
- Dark leafy greens, beans and lentils for folate, which helps keep homocysteine down
- Zinc from meat, seafood, nuts and seeds
- Oily fish such as salmon or sardines twice a week
- 1.5 to 2 litres of water a day, more in hot weather
- Less salt and alcohol, and no smoking, since smoking cuts blood flow to the skin
- On a blood thinner, check with a pharmacist before fish oil, turmeric or ginkgo supplements

### Exercise

Calf exercises and walking stop blood pooling in the legs, which helps wounds heal; they support treatment but don't clear LV clots.

- **While wounds are open:** ankle pumps (20 every hour of sitting) and leg elevation above heart level (20 to 30 minutes, 2 or 3 times a day). Calf raises (10 to 20, 3 times a day) and gentle walking if the foot isn't sore. Seated or upper body gym work is fine. No swimming.
- **Once healed:** build up to 8,000 to 10,000 steps a day. Cycling, swimming and the gym are all good. Avoid big temperature swings such as ice baths.
- **Massage gun (optional):** calf muscles only, lowest setting, 1 to 2 minutes per side. Keep it off shins, ankles, feet, wounds and the back of the knee. Skip it on a blood thinner unless a doctor approves.

### Football

Football is fine once every wound has closed and stayed closed for a few weeks.

- Shin guards that also cover the ankles, every game, since knocks can start new ulcers
- Boots with a roomy toe box and thick socks; tight boots press where the foot sores are
- Check feet after every game and clean any scrape straight away
- Play in cooler evenings, since LV tends to flare in hot months
- On a blood thinner, avoid hard tackles and see a doctor right away after any head knock
