# Wound care and red flags

## Daily wound care

1. Wash hands.
2. Rinse the wound gently with saline. Do not scrub.
3. Do not use alcohol, hydrogen peroxide or iodine on the wound.
4. Pat the skin around it dry.
5. Cover with a non stick dressing. If there is no pus, ask the clinic nurse about a moisture keeping dressing such as hydrocolloid or foam.
6. Change it daily, or sooner if wet with sweat or fluid.
7. Moisturise the dry skin around the wound, not the wound.

Wounds heal faster covered and slightly moist than left to dry.

## Things not to do

- Do not poke, squeeze or pop anything.
- Do not peel scabs, dark crusts or the skin over a wound, even with pus underneath. On a blood thinner it can bleed a lot, and it lets bacteria in. Dead tissue is removed by a doctor or nurse.
- Do not put steroid cream or turmeric on an open wound.
- Do not take leftover antibiotics.
- Do not soak the foot.

## After a knock to the leg or foot

1. Sit and raise the foot above hip level for 20 to 30 minutes.
2. Hold a cold pack wrapped in cloth on it for 10 minutes. No ice straight on the skin.
3. If the skin is broken, press for 10 minutes, rinse with saline and cover.
4. Use paracetamol for pain. Arcoxia and ibuprofen raise bleeding risk with rivaroxaban, so check with the doctor before using them.
5. Mark the edge of any bruise with a pen and take a photo.
6. Keep taking rivaroxaban.
7. Check the spot daily. If it turns dark purple or black, blisters or opens, call the clinic within 1 to 2 days.

## What an early flare looks like

- A new burning or stabbing pain in a small spot around the ankle or on top of the foot, often before anything shows.
- Then small red or purple dots, star shaped purple patches or a net pattern.
- Then small, very painful crusted ulcers.

## Action plan

| What is noticed | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| New burning pain in a spot, new red or purple dots, or pain up 2 or more points for 2 days | Photograph it, rest, raise the legs, keep cool. Contact the dermatology clinic within 1 to 2 working days |
| Pus, spreading redness, warmth, swelling, bad smell | See a GP or polyclinic the same day. Mark the edge of the redness with a pen. Tell the doctor about rivaroxaban, since some antibiotics interact |
| Infection signs with fever, chills, fast heartbeat, dizziness or confusion | A&E or call 995 |
| Redness past the pen line, red streaks up the leg, black area spreading, pain rising fast | A&E |
| Nosebleed over 10 minutes or a cut that won't stop after 10 minutes of firm pressure | A&E |
| Pink, red or brown urine; black or red stools; vomiting or coughing blood; large unexplained bruises | Medical review the same day |
| Any knock to the head, sudden severe headache, confusion, slurred speech, weakness | A&E or 995 at once. Say "I take rivaroxaban" |
| Foot tight and hard after a knock, cannot bear weight, toes numb, cold or pale | A&E |
| Foamy urine that lasts, swelling of the face or both legs, passing much less urine | Urine test and kidney check within days. Same day if urine is red or brown |
| Joint pain, stomach pain, numb feet or crops of raised purple dots | Tell the dermatologist soon. These can mean vasculitis beyond the skin |
