# Daily prevention routine

LV cannot be prevented with certainty. The aim is fewer and milder flares. Most of this advice comes from small LV studies, expert guidance and general wound care, because no trial has tested lifestyle steps for LV.

## What matters most, in order

1. **Take rivaroxaban every day and ask if the dose is right.** Strongest evidence.
2. **Agree a stop and restart plan with the doctor.** Relapse after stopping is common, and restarting early works.
3. **Act on pain early.** Rising or new burning pain usually comes before a new ulcer. Call the clinic within 1 to 2 days.
4. **Avoid heat and big temperature swings.** Heat is the best documented trigger. In studies, between a third and 88% of patients were worse in summer.
5. **Protect feet and ankles from knocks and rubbing.** A knock can start a new ulcer.
6. **Keep blood moving.** Move every 30 minutes. Avoid standing still for long.
7. **Treat infections fast. Don't smoke.**
8. **Ask about light compression once healed.**

Supplements, a massage gun and skipping tablets do not help.

## Daily routine

### Morning

- Take rivaroxaban at the usual time.
- Check both feet and ankles for one minute: new red or purple dots, a net pattern, new sore spots, redness, warmth or pus.
- Do wound care if a wound is open. See 08_Wound_Care_and_Red_Flags.md.
- Moisturise healthy skin. Dry well between the toes.
- Wear cushioned socks and roomy closed shoes that do not rub the ankle bones or the top of the foot.

### At work

- Every 30 minutes: walk for 2 to 3 minutes, or do 20 ankle pumps and 10 calf raises. Garmin Move Alert can remind.
- Avoid long periods of standing still. With a standing desk, switch about every 30 minutes.
- Use a footrest. Do not cross the legs.
- Keep the aircon comfortable, about 24 to 26 degrees, with no cold air blowing on the legs. Wear long trousers in cold rooms.
- Take calls standing or walking. Walk 10 minutes after lunch, indoors or in the shade.
- Watch desk edges, drawers and chair wheels. On 11 Nov a drawer hit the foot.
- Keep in the bag: a spare rivaroxaban tablet, saline, non stick dressings, tape, spare socks, paracetamol.

### Evening

- Raise the legs above heart level for 20 to 30 minutes, once or twice.
- Shower lukewarm, not hot. Once a day is enough.
- Log pain from 0 to 10, new spots, heat exposure, missed doses and any bleeding.

### Weekly

- Sunday photo of both ankles and the tops of both feet. Same light, same distance, with a coin or ruler for scale.
- Count open wounds and measure the largest.
- Review steps and the longest sitting block on Garmin.
- Check the tablet supply.

## Exercise

No exercise has been tested in LV. This advice is expert opinion, borrowed from vein disease and from general blood thinner safety. Confirm it with the dermatologist.

- **While wounds are open:** flat walking, seated or upper body gym work, stationary bike if nothing presses on a wound. No swimming, no barefoot training, no jumping.
- **Gym on rivaroxaban:** prefer machines to free weights near the feet. Protect the shins. Expect easier bruising. Any knock to the head means seeing a doctor.
- **Steps:** aim for a steady 7,000 to 10,000 every day, weekends included. Garmin to 21 Nov 2026 showed about 9,800 on weekdays but about 1,900 on weekend days. See 11_Tracking_Log.md.
- **Walk out of the heat:** many recorded walks start around 3:15pm, the hottest part of the day. Heat is the best documented trigger, so use shade or indoor routes, or walk in the evening.
- **Strength:** two light leg sessions a week add calf pump work. See the next section.
- **Once healed:** cycling and swimming are good. Wear pool shoes.
- **Skip:** sauna, hot soaks and ice baths.

### Leg training at the gym

Added 21 Nov 2026. Leg training is fine while wounds are open, as long as nothing presses on or rubs a wound.

- **Fine to do:** leg press with shoes on and moderate weight. Seated hip abduction and adduction. Glute machines. Calf raises. Leg extension and leg curl only if the pad does not sit on a wound or the biopsy site.
- **Avoid for now:** jumping, box jumps, sprints and barefoot training. Free weights near the feet, such as deadlifts or carrying plates and dumbbells. A dropped weight on rivaroxaban means a large bruise and possibly a new ulcer.
- **Before:** cushioned socks, roomy closed shoes, dressing on. Start light and build up slowly.
- **During:** train in a cool room. Check that no pad, strap or shoe rubs the ankle bones or the top of the foot.
- **After:** rinse lukewarm and change a wet dressing. Raise the legs for 20 to 30 minutes, then check both feet.
- **Stop the session if:** a new burning or stabbing pain starts at the ankle or foot, a wound bleeds, or the dressing soaks through.
- **Skip leg day and see a doctor first if:** a wound has pus, spreading redness, warmth or swelling.

## Sleep

Added 22 Nov 2026. No LV study looks at sleep, so this is general healing advice and not an LV treatment.

- **Target:** 7 hours or more a night.
- **Where it stands:** Garmin for 9 to 22 Nov 2026 showed an average of 5 h 50 min, down from 7 h 23 min in September. Sleep starts around 1:20am and ends around 7:30am, with almost no waking in between. The late bedtime looks like the cause, not pain.
- **How to fix it:** move bedtime 15 to 20 minutes earlier every few nights until asleep by midnight.
- **If leg pain wakes you:** log it and tell the dermatologist. Night pain can be an early flare sign.
- **Evidence:** in one study, healthy adults who slept 2 hours a night for 3 nights took 5.0 days to restore the skin barrier instead of 4.2. That is far less sleep than 6 hours, so the size of the effect here is not known. A 2022 review of 31 studies found that sleep loss lowers pain thresholds in healthy people. Sources are in 12_Sources_and_Related_Chats.md.

## Football

- Contact football is generally advised against while on a blood thinner. Head knocks are the main danger.
- Ask the dermatologist before playing.
- A realistic path: non contact drills or a light kickabout once every wound has been closed for 4 or more weeks, with shin and ankle guards, roomy boots and thick socks. Matches only if the doctor agrees.
- Check the feet after every session and clean any scrape straight away.

## Compression stockings

- Hold off while any wound is open, painful or infected. The sores sit where a stocking rubs, and the duplex scan showed no significant reflux, so the benefit is small.
- Once healed, ask the clinic about light class 1 stockings (about 20 mmHg), ideally open toe and knee high.
- Stop if there is more pain, numbness, or cold or blue toes.

## Massage gun

Skip it while on rivaroxaban. It can cause deep bruising and does not clear LV clots. Gentle hand massage of the calf or thigh is fine, stroking upward and staying away from the ankle, foot and discoloured skin.

## Temperature and washing

- Aim for a steady, comfortable temperature. Too cold narrows the vessels. Too hot triggers flares.
- Wash the legs once a day. More often dries the skin and makes wounds soggy.
- Use a mild cleanser without soap or fragrance. Pat dry.
- After sweating, a quick lukewarm rinse is fine. Change a wet dressing.
- No foot soaks.

## Moving somewhere cooler

A cooler climate may reduce flares for people who are heat sensitive, but LV still occurs in Europe and cold can also worsen it. To learn whether heat is a trigger, note the weather and sun exposure before each flare.
